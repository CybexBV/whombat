# iOS's own menus, sheets and swipes on iPhone (2026-10-28)

John, 2026-10-28, in the main checkout, after a look at how T3 Code's iPhone app uses iOS's own elements (a long press
on one of its threads opens iOS's menu): “Implement 1, 2, 4, 5”, that is: small confirmations as a toast, iOS's own menu
everywhere a long press or a ⋯ button opens one, swipe rows like theirs, and the system's sheets. No preview window on a
long press (he doesn't want it). And on the open iPhone Duo the lines over the two reply boxes (“Ask Dusty” on the list,
“Reply” beside it) should be level, as the lines under the two headers are. No contract or runtime change.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-6270 | **A small confirmation on iPhone is the app's toast** (`showNotice`: “Restored”, “Stopped”, “Disconnected”, a copy), at the bottom and gone by itself; Android keeps its own toast. Questions and failures stay alerts. | An alert to tap away after every small success is not how an iPhone app confirms anything; AP-6264 did this for copies only. |
| AP-6271 | **Every menu on iPhone is iOS's own** (`modules/whombat-menu`, `ui/nativeMenu.tsx`): a long press on a row opens it where it was held, a tap on a ⋯ button opens it from the button. Besides a message (AP-6260): a row of a room's Settled list (Open again, Reply in thread, Copy link), a file in Files, held or its ⋮ (its name on top; Download, Open message; Rename, Move; Remove, red), a conversation's ⋯ (Notifications with All, Mentions and Nothing one level down, the current one ticked and named under it; Pinned messages, Plans, Files) and a notice line's ⋯ or the line held (its title on top; Ignore and Remind me tomorrow, then Stop watching, with what they mean under them). Android keeps its sheets; Settled's menu there is a sheet of the same choices (`MenuSheet`). | One way to reach a thing's actions on iPhone, drawn as iOS draws them, where a sheet sliding up for a few choices read as foreign. |
| AP-6272 | **The menu is a control's, compact, as T3 Code's** (a `UIButton` whose own menu interaction sits on the row with a long press, the button itself with a tap): it opens where the row was held, in glass, without lifting the row or dimming the screen, and **keeps the keyboard up**, so holding a message while typing leaves the reply as it was. Supersedes AP-6260's lift and dimming and AP-6262. A menu that opened while the finger was down means letting go isn't a tap on the row. A list re-rendering the same menu doesn't rebuild it; a changed one waits until the one on screen has gone. | What T3 Code does (their patch to `@react-native-menu/menu`); a plain view's context menu hides the keyboard and lifts the message over a dimmed screen, which John found less good than theirs. |
| AP-6273 | **The menus say more in fewer lines**: groups with a line between them, submenus, a tick on the chosen one, a quiet line under an item and the system's symbols (`data/nativeMenu.ts`). A Settled row's menu is also its list of accessibility actions. | iOS's own menus do; the conversation's levels fit on one line until opened. |
| AP-6274 | **Swipe rows have actions on both sides** (`ui/swipeRow.tsx`, `data/swipe.ts`): pulled sideways a row shows its 96 dp panels under it; let go past half of them and they stay open to tap; pulled on past 60 % of the row (or flicked past the panels) the edge's action runs by itself, with a tick when letting go will run it and again when it won't. A tap on an open row, a scroll of its list or another row opening closes it. Settled's rows swipe left to ↶ Open again at the edge (it runs on a full swipe, as AP-6157's swipe to the right did) and More beside it (iOS's menu from the panel on iPhone, the sheet on Android; a menu can't open by itself). | As iOS's Mail and T3 Code's thread list swipe; a swipe that sprang back unless it went all the way hid that there was anything to tap. Both on the right as T3 Code's: from iOS 26 a pull to the right anywhere on a page goes back, so Open again on the left never opened on a phone. |
| AP-6275 | **On iPhone a sheet is iOS's own** (`ui/sheet.tsx`, `modules/whombat-sheet`): the same panel in a React Native `Modal` presented as a form sheet, as tall as what it holds (at most its `maxHeight` share of the window), with the system's grabber, slide, dimming and drag down to close; a tap beside it closes it too, and with a field it rises above the keyboard by itself. Its colour stays the app's (`bg`, or `sf` for the one-Whombat sheets) and it is light or dark as the app is. On the open Duo it is a centred card a phone's width; on the closed Duo its content keeps clear of the bar under the clock, which a sheet doesn't hand to what it shows. Android keeps the app's sheet. | The system's drag and dimming are what an iPhone's sheets feel like; the app's colours keep its words readable whatever the phone's own appearance. |
| AP-6276 | **On two pages the lines over the reply boxes meet** as the header lines do (AP-5814): each box reports how tall it is at rest, empty and counted from the window's bottom (a tab's screen ends above the room the side tabs keep), and every box is at least the tallest's, its pill at the bottom. A wrapped “Ask Dusty anything” on a narrowed list, or a settled thread's “A reply opens it again.”, makes the other box as tall; what is typed grows only its own box. | The boxes differed by what was in them, and by the home indicator's room only a tab's screen keeps under it. |
| AP-6277 | **On iPhone a message's words are selected with a hold** (John, same day: “in the chat I would like to be able to copy … select and select all; now I see a context window”): its paragraphs and headings are iOS's own text view (`@bsky.app/react-native-uitextview`, `RichText selectable`), so a hold selects a word with its handles and the system's Copy, Look Up, Translate and Share, and Select All, which iOS leaves out for text that can't be edited (a marked patch to the library); links still open with a tap, a tap elsewhere still opens the thread, and while words are selected a tap only deals with them (shows Copy again or clears them). The message's menu (AP-6260, AP-6271) opens from a hold on the rest of the message: its name line, its cards and pictures, its replies line. Wherever messages show (Chat, Feedback, a thread, a conversation); lists of threads keep the menu on the whole row. Android keeps the sheet on a long press. | What iOS's own apps and T3 Code's conversation do with words; React Native's selectable text can only copy a whole paragraph. A library rather than a module of our own: Bluesky keeps it for their app, MIT, tested on React Native 0.86, and it lays the text out as React Native does. |

## Checked

`pnpm check`; the demo world's Release build on Xcode 27.1's iPhone Duo simulator (iOS 27.1), open and closed, light and
dark: a message held with and without the keyboard up (the menu opens where it was held, the keyboard stays, an agent's
reply has its runtime as the title), More reactions (the emoji sheet: the card on the open Duo, the bottom sheet clear of
the bar when closed, closed by a tap beside it), Edit (the sheet above the keyboard, Save clear of the bar), Copy text
(the toast), a Settled row held (its menu; letting go opens nothing), swiped left (both panels stay open, More's menu opens from
its panel, Reply in thread opens the thread; a full swipe runs Open again), one open row closing the other, a notice
line's ⋯ on a server page (title, words under Ignore and Stop watching), and the two reply boxes level with the list at
its narrowest and beside a settled thread. Android's swipe row and Settled sheet are covered by the unit tests
(`data/swipe.test.ts`, `data/nativeMenu.test.ts`), not run on an emulator.

AP-6277, the same simulator closed, in Chat and in a thread: a hold on a word selects it with its handles and the
system's bar (no menu), Select All selects the whole paragraph and a tap on it brings Copy back without opening the
thread, a hold on the name line opens the message's menu, a quick tap on the words still opens the thread.
