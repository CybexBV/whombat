# The account menu, Settings out of the sidebar, notifications on by themselves (ACM)

John, 2026-11-27: clean up the web's account menu (the one that opens from the person's name at the bottom of the
sidebar), turn notifications on by themselves where the browser allows it, and say when an update is ready. He approved
the mockup (the account menu with Light · Dark · Auto, Settings with its shortcut, the version with “Up to date”, and the
Chat's one question about notifications) and chose to leave Devices and Shared links out of the menu too, and to add no
“pause notifications” item. The phone app has its own task.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ACM-01 | **The account menu, in this order**: the person's face (36px), name and “Your profile” with a chevron, which opens Settings → You → Profile; the theme as Light · Dark · Auto with their icons; a line; Settings with its shortcut (“⌘ ,” on a Mac, “Ctrl ,” elsewhere); a line; Get the phone app, Report a problem or idea (only where it showed before) and Sign out, each with a muted icon; a line; the version (ACM-05). Profile, Notifications, Devices, Shared links and Prompts, “Turn on/off notifications” and “Switch to … theme” left it: Settings → You keeps every one of them. | One way to each thing: the person's own settings were listed twice and the theme and notifications were one-way switches that said the action, not the state. |
| ACM-02 | **Settings leaves the sidebar's places: Chat · Inbox · Overview · Automations · Files · Vault.** Settings is in the account menu, at ⌘, / Ctrl , and in Search (its pages and its place); no place is selected while Settings is open. The phone's own tab bar keeps its Settings tab. | The account menu became the way to Settings, as in most apps; one row less in a list people scan all day. |
| ACM-03 | **⌘, / Ctrl , opens Settings from anywhere**, a text field too, like ⌘K / Ctrl K: no text field uses the combination, and desktop apps open their settings with it. The menu's row names it (`aria-keyshortcuts`). | The shortcut people already know for settings. |
| ACM-04 | **Auto is a theme of its own: the account's `system`.** Choosing it (in the menu or Settings' Theme row, which offers the same three) clears this device's choice so the page follows the computer from its first paint, and saves `system` to the account like Light and Dark. An account preference of `system` arriving from the Whombat follows the computer too. The visual tests set the browser's colour scheme with the stored theme, since the fixture account's theme is Auto. | Auto could be shown but not kept, and an account set to `system` was ignored, so the last device's choice stuck. |
| ACM-05 | **The version line says the version without its build and whether the Whombat is up to date.** Left: “Whombat 0.2.0” (“app 3.171.0” while the Whombat's version is unknown); its tooltip and name are the whole line (“Whombat 0.2.0-20261126… · app 3.171.0”) and a click copies it, with a short toast. Right, on a box that says: “● Up to date” in the ok colour while it runs its release with nothing waiting, or “Update ready” in the warn colour while a release waits for the owner's yes (`updateReady`), which opens This Whombat → About, where Update now is. Anything in between (an update on its way, failed or paused), a self-hosted server and no answer yet show the version alone. The Whombat is asked once the pointer or the focus comes to the person's row, not while it opens. | The build number cut the line off and said nothing to a person; “Up to date” only when it is true. |
| ACM-06 | **Notifications come on by themselves where this browser already allows them.** On each page load, where the page can send notifications (the hosted app; a box's own secure page), the browser's permission is granted and this browser isn't registered with the Whombat, it subscribes without asking, unless the person turned them off in this browser. Where the browser has never asked, the Chat shows one card at the top of its list: a bell, “Get a ping when {assistant} needs you?”, one reason, Turn on (the browser's own question comes straight from that click) and Not now. Not now, Turn on and Off are kept in this browser (`whombat:web-push-choice`), so the card doesn't come back and Off isn't undone. Blocked, can't, already on or answered: no card. Every screen shares one state per Whombat (`push/useWebPush.ts`), and the decision is one pure function (`push/pushChoice.ts`). | John's ask: notifications on without a hunt through a menu, asked once with a reason where the browser must ask (the phone's MN-05 does the same), never re-enabled against the person's word. |
| ACM-07 | **Settings → Notifications' THIS BROWSER is one switch, “Notifications in this browser”.** On asks the browser from the click and subscribes; Off unsubscribes and is remembered here (ACM-06). Blocked, the switch rests and the line says notifications are blocked in this browser's site settings; where this browser can't show any, Get the app; a box page that can't send them says so, as before. | The menu row is gone, so Settings holds the one switch, in the same form as every other on/off setting there. |
