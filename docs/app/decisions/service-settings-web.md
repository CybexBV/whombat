# A service's settings on the web (AP-7000…AP-7009)

The web's part of Whombat `docs/decisions/service-settings.md` (SST-01…SST-11), drawn from the mockup John saw
(`service-dash-v2`, screens 1, 2, 3 and 5). Contracts: `ThingSetting`, the `settings` panel, `ThingPanelAsk` and
`ThingPage.verdict` in core `domain/thing-page.ts`; `ThingActionResponse.undoable`; `spaceUse` in
`@whombat/client/hosts`.

| ID | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-7000 | **A settings card** is the app's card with one row per setting: name, sentence, the last change with **Put it back**, the value in words, then the app's `Toggle`, a small **Change** or the service's name as a small mono tag that opens its own settings in a new tab. A row that can still be put back is tinted `--hi`, like the mock's fresh row. A switch shows the flipped state and “On”/“Off” at once, the page's own words with the next read (`useControlGuesses`, as Controls do). | The mock's row, from the parts Controls already use. |
| AP-7001 | **The sheet is the app's `Modal`** (560 px, padding 24, `--sf`): the title, the help, real radio buttons drawn as the mock's rings, “Now” on the current one, the hint with a check, and **Ask {assistant} instead** · Cancel · **Change it**. Change it stays disabled until another choice is picked, the sheet stays up, busy, until the service answered, and the toast says how it went. | SST-09, with a keyboard-usable radio group. |
| AP-7002 | **A settings tab lays out in two equal columns** whenever its panels include a `settings` card; its untitled top text is a plain line with its link, its titled bottom text the “Something else?” row with the assistant's face, across the width. | Mock screen 2; the 1.85 : 1 Summary columns would make one column of settings cramped. |
| AP-7003 | **Ask on a card** sits last in the card's head (after its buttons and corner link) on every card with `ask`: tables, lists, bars, galleries, controls, settings, Worth a look, facts and titled text. It opens a popover drawn over the page (cards clip what overflows), right edges with the button, that follows the page as it scrolls; focus goes to the first question, Escape or a click elsewhere closes it and focus returns to Ask. | SST-08; the mock's popover reaches outside its card. |
| AP-7004 | **Put it back** — in the toast and on a row — is `changes.undo`, then the page and Activity are read again; it toasts “Put back.”, the undo's own words when it asked or was handed to the assistant, and the server's refusal as it says it. | SST-03; one way, wherever it is pressed. |
| AP-7005 | **The verdict** under a service's name is the server pages' `VerdictLine` (the status's dot and the sentence), placed between the name and the fact line. | SST-10: the same as a server's. |
| AP-7006 | **The thing page's own tab is “Connection”** in every test and link text (“Change in Connection” on What Whombat may do). | SST-06. |
| AP-7007 | **Storage opens with up to three tiles in words**, drawn like the Summary's figures: Space (the Summary's `spaceWords` bands, the fullest volume's share), Full in / Full by (the hosts service's `fullInDays` in the Summary's forecast words; “Not filling up”), Disks (failing, to watch, all healthy, the Storage table's own rule). No growth tile: the tab reads no history of the volumes, and inventing a figure isn't words. | SST-11 without a new read or new thresholds. |
| AP-7008 | **What uses the space** follows Pools and filesystems: each machine's and app's own disk (`spaceUse`), a link to its page, a bar against the biggest, its size, then “Everything else”; meta “Machines’ and apps’ own disks”. No pool switcher and no Measure again: nothing measures per pool yet. | SST-11. |
| AP-7009 | **Fixture mode's Plex** (`apps/web/src/app/plexSettingsFixtures.ts`) adds the settings tab, Ask, Scan all and the verdict over the shared service pages' Plex, in the web's layer, and keeps every change so a refusal can be shown. The shared `@whombat/fixtures` Plex is unchanged. | The phone is built at the same time; the web can be looked at without touching the shared world. |
