# Bluetooth on the web, and the shared Bluetooth model (AP-3700…AP-3749)

John, 2026-10-20: “Implement your original idea so we can pair keyboards, mice, speakers, etc”. The product decisions
are Whombat's `docs/decisions/bluetooth.md` (BT-01…BT-11; BT-11 are the words); this is stream W of
`docs/implementation/bluetooth/assignment.md` there: the client's Bluetooth model (shared with the phone, stream M,
`bluetooth-android.md`, AP-3750…) and the web's Settings → This Whombat → Overview → BLUETOOTH with its Add a device
dialog. Contracts 0.109.0 (CT-670…CT-699: `BluetoothState`, `BluetoothScan`, `BluetoothPairing`, the `host.bluetooth*`
session ops, feature `host.bluetooth`). No mockup: Settings' own rows and dialogs, as NETWORK and its Wi-Fi dialog
(`hardware-support-web.md`).

The rules and words are `@whombat/client` (`packages/client/src/host/bluetooth.ts`, tested), the fake
`@whombat/client/host/fake` (`bluetooth-fake.ts`); the web's parts are `apps/web/src/features/machine/`.

## The client (shared with the phone)

| ID | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-3700 | **The wire shapes are the contracts'** (0.109.0), re-exported in `host/contract.ts` beside the network's. | AP-3701 | **One Bluetooth model for both clients**: `BluetoothApi` (`bluetoothApi(session)`, null on the HTTP transport; `bluetoothOffered`), `bluetoothSectionView`, `bluetoothDeviceRows`, `bluetoothDeviceMeta`, `bluetoothFoundRows`, `bluetoothPairingView`, `bluetoothFailureText`, `bluetoothErrorCode`/`bluetoothErrorText`, `bluetoothMissing`/`bluetoothShown`, `bluetoothPairingRunning`, `bluetoothScanRunning`, `bluetoothKindText`, `bluetoothSignalText` and `createBluetoothPoller`. | AP-3702 | **A paired device's row**: its kind's icon, the name, “Connected · Battery 80 %” (the battery only while connected: it is read then), “Connected” or “Not connected”; connected first, then by name (sorted again here, whatever arrives); a label for screen readers (“Keychron K3, keyboard, connected, battery 80 %”); the menu's first item is Connect or Disconnect (`toggle`), Remove follows. | BT-05, BT-11; §2.1's order. |
| AP-3703 | **No adapter, no section** (a VM, a PC without the radio: `missing` `no_adapter`, or no adapter and nothing said). | AP-3704 | **Nothing paired yet**: “No devices yet” over “Keyboards, mice, speakers and headphones work with this Whombat.”, with **Add a device** on that row; with devices, Add a device ends the card on a row of its own. | A box gets its first keyboard from the app (BT-02); new words in BT-11's voice. |

An ended pairing isn't shown there (the new row says it; the dialog told its ending). | BT-07: one pairing at a time; the owner sees why Add a device waits. |
| AP-3706 | **Following the box** (`createBluetoothPoller`): `host.bluetooth` once when BLUETOOTH opens and the tab is visible, then every `BLUETOOTH_POLL_MS` while the answer's scan has its `until` ahead or a pairing runs, never otherwise (and not past `until` or the deadline by more than 5 s). What the app did itself — a scan (`scanned`), a pairing started, answered or cancelled (`pairing`; a running pairing ends the looking, BT-07), a device connected or disconnected (`device`), one removed (`removed`) — shows at once, and an answer asked for before it is dropped. While following, a failed answer is ignored and asked again; with nothing shown it says “Bluetooth couldn’t be read right now.” with **Try again**. | §2.2: no realtime, poll every second while looking or pairing. |
| AP-3707 | **The found list**: strongest first, devices that don't say their signal last, each address once, the paired ones left out; each with its kind's icon, name and the Wi-Fi's bars (four rising bars and their words, AP-3209); labelled “MX Master 3S, mouse, strong signal”. | §2.1's order, defended; the bars say which one is close. |
| AP-3708 | **A pairing in words**: phases `pairing` and `connecting` are both “Pairing with {name}…” (the difference is the box's); `code` is “Type {code} on {name}, then press Enter.” with the code's digits, the ones typed so far marked; `confirm` is “Does {name} show {code}?” with **Yes** / **No**; `done` “{name} is ready.”; `failed` BT-11's sentence for `rejected`, `timeout`, `not_found` and `failed`, and “Pairing with {name} stopped. | AP-3709 | **Refusals by their Bluetooth code**: `error.details.code` before the error's own code, plus `forbidden`: “Another device is pairing. ## The web

| ID | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-3711 | **Where Bluetooth is asked**: `AppServices.bluetooth` — the session's ops on a managed Whombat that advertises `host.bluetooth`, fixture mode's machine on the HTTP transport, null elsewhere; only owners get the section (the session account's owner flag on a managed Whombat, the burrow role elsewhere, as NETWORK, AP-3222). **BLUETOOTH's place**: the Overview, right after NETWORK and before AT A GLANCE (`ThisWhombatTab.tsx` gains one line). | BT-02: owners, beside the network. |
| AP-3712 | **A device's row**: its kind's Lucide icon in an 18 px slot (ink while connected, muted while not; the missing and pairing lines put their dot in the same slot so the names line up), the name over “Connected · Battery 80 %” or “Not connected”, and a ⋯ icon button (`Actions for Keychron K3`) opening the app's menu with Connect or Disconnect, then Remove… (red, on a line of its own). While it connects the meta says “Connecting…”/“Disconnecting…” and the menu waits; a refusal or a device that didn't come is said under the meta, in red, on that row. | One action that fits, the destructive one apart and asked (BT-05); the problem where it happened. |
| AP-3713 | **Remove… asks once**: “Remove {name}?” over “You’ll need to pair it again to use it.”, **Cancel** / **Remove** (“Removing…”), a refusal as the dialog's note. | BT-11's words; removing can't be undone from here. |
| AP-3714 | **Add a device** (the dialog): titled “Add a device” over BT-11's hint; it looks at once (`host.bluetooth.scan`) and lists what the box finds as buttons (icon, name, kind, bars) as they come, with “Looking for devices…” and a breathing dot under the list while the box looks (in the list's place while nothing is found yet). When the minute is over the list stays (choosable) with **Look again**; nothing found says “No devices nearby.”. Choosing one pairs it; a refusal is the dialog's note (a device no longer nearby leaves the list). | BT-03, BT-07: the person chooses from what the box found; a device found a moment ago may still be paired. |
| AP-3715 | **The pairing in the dialog**: the device's name becomes the title over its kind (as the Wi-Fi form takes the network's name), then one line in the outcome panel with its dot (breathing while the box works): “Pairing with {name}…”; the code — the sentence, then the six digits large in DM Mono boxes, the typed ones dimmed and the next one ringed in the brand colour (hidden from screen readers, the sentence says the code); the question with **No** and **Yes**; “{name} is ready.” with **Done**; a failure with **Close** and **Try again** (back to the list, looking again). **Cancel** (and Esc, the close button, closing in any way) cancels a pairing that still runs; **No** goes back to the list and looks again. | BT-04: codes shown, not typed; the brief: closing cancels. |
| AP-3716 | **Visual check and baselines**: fixture mode at 1440×900, light and dark, against NETWORK and the Wi-Fi dialog. The fixture machine's Bluetooth runs on its own clock, `window.__WHOMBAT_BLUETOOTH__.advance(ms)` in development, because the visual suite's clock stands still. Baselines (`visual/machine.spec.ts`): BLUETOOTH on the laptop and switched off; Add a device's list, Pairing with…, the code with two digits typed, the question, ready, refused; Remove's question. | Every state on screen without a box, and the shots stay still. |
| AP-3717 | **The fake managed world's Bluetooth**: each box's machine answers `host.bluetooth*` with `createFakeBluetooth` (its paired devices kept in `ApplianceState.machine` across reloads, fault `machineBluetooth(device)`); a box without a machine answers `not_managed`. The journey `e2e-managed/machine.spec.ts` pairs a mouse and a keyboard that shows a code, connects the headphones and removes a keyboard; the PC without a Wi-Fi driver shows Bluetooth switched off; a box without a machine shows no BLUETOOTH. | Every path over the session without a box. |
