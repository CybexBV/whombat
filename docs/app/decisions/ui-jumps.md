# The screen holds still (AP-9010…AP-9016)

John (2026-11-09): “In the iOS app and web UI (possibly also Android) the UI sometimes jumps around during scrolling or
during a chat. Also when I hover my mouse over certain elements the UI changes.” What was found, measured in Chromium in
fixture mode (a pointer swept over every screen with the browser's layout-shift records on, a long thread and room
scrolled with the wheel) and read in the phone's list code. Before and after: `docs/evidence/ui-jumps/`.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-9010 | **A thread moves to its end for a new reply or someone starting to work only when the reader is at the end, or the reply is their own.** Further up they stay where they read and “Scroll to the end” shows (`useThreadScroll`'s `newBelow`), until they reach the end or press it, as the room's “New messages ↓” (WX-07) and the phone's MX-01 already do. | Every reply, typing line and hand-over pulled a reader who had scrolled up back to the end, so during a busy chat (a council's notes, a moved card) the thread jumped away from what they were reading. |
| AP-9011 | **Hover changes paint only.** A `.x, .x:hover` rule (written to beat the global `a:hover`) keeps no layout a modifier of the same element changes: the reply row's hover twin is gone (a row with a work line took the twin's `gap` and grew 4px under the pointer, so Chat, held at its end, moved 4px each time the pointer crossed it), and `.bold` and `.waiting` carry their own `:hover` (the open or unread chat in the sidebar lost its bold under the pointer; a server waiting to be added lost its dashed frame). | The modifier is one class, the hover twin two: the twin wins while the pointer is over it. |
| AP-9012 | **A row keeps its element when something beside it comes or goes.** A thread's reply is always one fragment (the model divider, the replies line and “Load earlier replies” inside it or not), and a room's row always sits in one with the “New” line. | Swapping the row for a fragment built it again: its card, pictures and previews started over at their first size and the list jumped. |
| AP-9013 | **The phone's list waits while the reader's finger or fling moves it.** `followAction` answers `later` instead of `end` while they scroll; when they let go (and the fling's grace has passed) it is asked again, and follows if they are still at the end or shows the pill if they left it. | Within the end's 80dp slack, rows drawn above as the reader nears them and the thinking row's words changed the content's size, and each change scrolled to the end under the finger: starting to scroll up snapped back, sometimes several times. |
| AP-9014 | **A message just sent keeps its row on the phone**: rows are keyed by the client's id while they have one (`ChatItem.key`), as the web does. | The server's copy has another id, so the row was drawn again when it came back. |
| AP-9015 | **The phone's thinking line is one line, shortened in the middle**: the agent's name and its clock stay. | Its words change with each step and every second, and moving between one and two lines moved the list held at its end. |
| AP-9016 | **Left as they are, for now**: the phone's keyboard moves the list from JavaScript frame by frame (react-native-keyboard-controller's `KeyboardChatScrollView` would move it on the UI thread: try it on a real phone first); a thread on the phone that shows only its root while its replies load opens a second list at the end; bars that appear above a phone thread after it opens (the T3 Code job, the settled line); web pages, HTML and SVG files in phone messages that grow once loaded; the room on the web still judges the end by position (within 120px), where the thread uses the reader's own input (AP-6451); Safari before 27 has no scroll anchoring, so rows above that take their real size while one scrolls up move what is on screen there. | Each needs a real device or a larger change; none was the cause of what John saw on a desktop browser. |
