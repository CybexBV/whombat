# Mobile reach: navigation and notifications (MN-01…MN-19)

Stream MN of the improvement-review build (Whombat `docs/implementation/improvement-review/assignment.md` §6.2, report
section D items 5–8 and 15), in `apps/mobile` on contracts 0.55.0 (`notification.read`, CT-300). Android first; the
native pieces are in `modules/whombat-live` (Kotlin, compiled with `./gradlew :whombat-live:compileReleaseKotlin` after
a prebuild; no emulator in this stream — the lead's one emulator pass checks the list at the end).

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| MN-01 | **A screen a link or notification opens has the tabs beneath it, and the app reopens where it was after a kill.** The linking config's `initialRouteName` is `Tabs` (React Navigation then navigates to the screen on top of the tabs, and a cold start builds `[Tabs, screen]`). The root navigation state is saved in the `SecureBox` under the storage scope (`server:<url>`, `managed:<org>/<Whombat>`, file `navigation`; sealed like the outbox and the cached reads, wiped with them) a second after it changes and when the app goes to the background, and restored once per process before the first screen (at most 1 s): not older than 24 hours, the same burrow, the tabs at the bottom (on the open tab; Search keeps its query), the screens above them as far as each may be restored (`navigation/persist.ts`), at most 8, action params stripped (`stripLinkedActionParams`). The demo world keeps nothing. | The report's D5: a notification-opened screen had nothing beneath it (back left the app) and a killed process forgot where the person was. The box is the one the outbox already trusts with message text. |
| MN-02 | **Ongoing live notifications time out 10 minutes after their last update**, every update starting the 10 minutes again (`setTimeoutAfter`, both builders; ended ones keep their 30 minutes), and the app reconciles them on every push that reaches it (`onGenericPush`, and `onPush` for anything but a live push, which carries its own state), on coming back to the foreground (runs and approvals reload, at most every 10 s; reconciling follows each load) and, while in the foreground, every 5 minutes for what is on screen (so the timeout doesn't take one the app is still keeping up). Reconciling ends or finishes the ones whose run or approval ended, as before. The same `LiveActivityBridge` and native module serve self-hosted and managed. | D6: on a managed Whombat (no live pushes, NT-13) and with a killed app nothing ever ended an ongoing notification. A timeout is the only thing that works without the app running; 10 minutes is long enough that a working run's updates keep it, short enough that a stale one goes. |
| MN-03 | **`notification.read` reads the inbox rows on every device, with the web's reducer.** WA's `reduceNotificationRead` (`packages/client/src/query/reducers.ts`, applied in `query/apply.ts` to every cached `notifications.inbox` of the socket's burrow; ir-shell a01a4e9, cherry-picked here so both branches carry the same commit): named rows, or with `ids: null` every row created up to `before`, get `readAt` (the earliest an event read them), and the count is the newest event's `unread`, else drops by the rows that turned read. The phone's inbox rows, the Inbox tab's badge, the app icon's badge and the unread count follow the cache; the inbox model now uses the shared `inboxItemShown` / `INBOX_GONE_WHEN_READ` (`@whombat/client`) instead of its own copy. Closes android-inbox.md's “Open”. | Contracts 0.55 has the event; the placeholder refetched the whole inbox. One reducer for web and phone (the coordinator's call), so nothing to reconcile at merge. |
| MN-04 | **Reading in the app takes the notification out of the shade.** Native `shade()` lists the inbox (`whombat.inbox:`) and managed (`whombat.push:`) notifications showing, with what each was built from (now kept in its extras: kind, an inbox push's burrow, row and path, a managed push's ref), and `cancel(tags)` removes them (other tags are ignored). `NotificationShadeBridge` (`push/ShadeBridge.tsx`, mounted in `RootNavigator`) looks a moment after any change of a cached inbox, on `notification.read`, on foreground and on pushes, and cancels what `readInShade` (`push/shade.ts`, pure) says was read. | D8. Android had `setAutoCancel` only, so a thread read in the app (or on the laptop) stayed in the shade. |
| MN-05 | Superseded by MN-16. **Notifications are asked for after a reason, not cold.** Neither bridge asks on start any more (`registerPush(…, ask)` / `registerManagedPush({ask: false})`; an undecided permission is `off`). While the system hasn't asked, the inbox shows a card under its title: “Know when something needs you” / “Turn on notifications for approvals to give, mentions and agents’ answers. Your phone asks you next.” with “Not now” (quiet) and “Turn on” (secondary), the grouped-list card and 44dp buttons. “Turn on” asks the system and registers; allowed or refused, the card goes. | D15: the system dialog came cold on first open, with no word on what Whombat would send. |
| MN-06 | **The shade is matched through the cache, not wired into each mark-read path.** Opening an inbox row, reading a conversation or a thread, opening a notification, `conversation.unread` with 0 from another device and `notification.read` all end in the cached inbox (optimistically or by the reducer), so one observer covers them; the event's ids are kept too (bounded, 500) for rows the cache doesn't hold, and its `ids: null` time covers a self-hosted notification posted before it (that burrow only). | Five call sites (inbox, chat, thread, the navigator's notification open, the managed tap fallback) would each need a native call and would still miss reads on other devices. |
| MN-07 | **A managed notification is matched by its resolved link.** Its tag is NT-15's keyed collapse hash, which the phone can't compute, and its ref is a keyed hash of the row: so the app resolves each ref once per process through the session (`push.resolve`, read-only) and cancels the notification when every cached row with exactly that link (`linkKey`, keys sorted) is read — the newest row of its thread, or its approval's NEEDS YOU row. A ref the Whombat answers `not_found` (gone, or no longer visible) is cancelled: its tap would open nothing. A self-hosted push is matched by its row id. A NEEDS YOU row whose approval is decided counts as read (NT-16 does the same for managed pushes on the server). | The group key NT-15 uses is deliberately opaque on the wire; resolving through the session is how a tap already finds its row. |
| MN-08 | **The app doesn't bring back a live notification that went.** Natively each shown key is remembered for 24 hours (`recent()`, cleared by `endAll`); reconciling shows what is on screen (refresh) and what is new, and leaves one that timed out or was swiped away (Android 14 lets people dismiss ongoing notifications) until its run or approval changes (`run.updated` / `approval.updated` render it again). | Re-creating an approval's notification on the “Needs you” channel (high importance) alerts again: every foreground after a timeout would have buzzed while the person was looking at the app. |
| MN-09 | **Restoring happens once per process and yields to links.** A cold start opened by a link or a notification (`Linking.getInitialURL()`, iOS's last tapped notification) doesn't restore: the link decides, with the tabs beneath. A managed tap or app link the managed root holds is applied on top of the restored place (back returns there). A burrow switch or a new sign-in doesn't restore. | The person who taps a notification wants that screen; the person who reopens the app wants their place. |
| MN-10 | **Opened from Recents after a kill, the old link is dropped.** Android relaunches the task with the intent that started it; `LatestIntentListener.onCreate` (whombat-live) clears the data and FCM tray extras of an intent with `FLAG_ACTIVITY_LAUNCHED_FROM_HISTORY`, so the app restores where the person was instead of reopening a notification from hours ago. | Without it MN-01's restore would be skipped (MN-09) and the stale screen opened, every time the app came back from Recents after Android killed it. |
| MN-11 | **What may be restored, and “data gone”.** Conversations, Pins, a conversation's Files and Memory must still be in the sidebar (by id, channel slug, or branch room by number and repo); DMs, agents, an agent's settings and packs, and a DM's Files must name an active agent; lists (Work, Plans, Automations, Vault, Connections, Files, Browse channels) always; Whombat and Devices on a managed build. A run, approval, automation run, thread, plan, pull request (and file), Vault entry or connection can't be checked without reading it: it is restored, and if its read (`RESTORE_WATCH`) answers 404, 410 or 403 within a minute while the person is still on it, the app pops to the tabs. Share, New message, Hire and any screen not on the list aren't restored; the stack is cut at the first screen that may not be. | Never open a screen whose data is gone, without delaying the start with requests. New screens (stream MQ's) restore once they are added to `persist.ts`. |
| MN-12 | Superseded by MN-17. **“Not now” is kept on this phone, and the card doesn't come back by itself.** Settings → Notifications keeps the “Turn on” the managed row already had, and a self-hosted server gets the same row (`serverPush` from `PushBridge`, “On” saying “You get a notification when something needs you.”: its pushes carry the text, so not the managed line about it staying on the Whombat). Both bridges try again without asking when the app comes back to the foreground (allowed in the system settings since). | The person said no to the reason; asking again unprompted is the nagging MN-05 removes. |
| MN-13 | **iOS is unchanged.** `recent`, `shade` and `cancel` are Android-only native functions (optional in `index.ts`, no-ops elsewhere); the Swift module wasn't touched (it is built on macOS). The permission card and the navigation restore are shared JavaScript. | Android first; iOS notifications are APNs alerts the system shows, and nothing here was built or run on a Mac. |
| MN-14 | **Self-hosted inbox notifications stay one per row.** They keep `whombat.inbox:<notification id>`; the server's `PushPayload` carries no group (NT-15 changed only managed pushes), and the shade now clears them by row. | Grouping them would need the group in the payload (a contract change). |
| MN-15 | **No Kotlin unit tests; the logic stays in tested JavaScript.** The native side only lists, cancels, times out and remembers keys; what to cancel and what to show is `readInShade` / `reconcileActivities` (unit-tested). The module compiles (`:whombat-live:compileReleaseKotlin`, offline, after `expo prebuild` in the worktree). | The module has no Kotlin test setup (AP-807), and nothing here needs one. |
| MN-16 | **Notifications come on by themselves wherever the phone lets them** (John, 2026-11-27; the web's account menu does the same). At the first open of a session that can register, `pushStart` (`push/autoOn.ts`, pure) reads the platform, its version, the permission, whether the system can still ask, an iPhone's quiet (provisional) authorization and a kept Not now: allowed already (Android 12 and older, where apps may notify unless the person turned it off, or allowed earlier) registers at once with no question; an iPhone never asked asks iOS for quiet notifications (`allowProvisional` with alert, badge and sound; no prompt shows) and registers (ios-push.md AP-6460); Android 13 and later never asked shows one sheet with the reason; refused, or Not now on this phone, does nothing. The sheet (`PushAskSheet`, mounted with the navigator so it shows over whatever the first screen is) is the bell, “Get a ping when {Agent} needs you?” and “Only for things that need you, like an approval or a backup that failed. You can change this in Settings.” (the web's words, the assistant by its name), with Turn on (Android's question, then registering) and Not now as 52dp buttons. The Overview's card is gone: nobody is asked twice. | The card waited on the Overview for a tap and the iPhone never asked at all, so most phones stayed silent; the phone asks only where it must, once, with one reason. |
| MN-17 | **Not now is kept on this phone and the sheet never comes back by itself**; closing the sheet (back, a tap beside it, a swipe down) is Not now too, except while the system's question shows. | MN-18 | **The deciding is one pure function, tested** (`push/autoOn.test.ts`): every platform, version, permission and Not now case, and reading expo-notifications' answer (a quiet iPhone is `undetermined` and not `granted`, with `ios.status` PROVISIONAL; `allowedQuietly`). ## For the emulator pass

- A notification tapped with the app killed opens its screen with the tabs beneath; back goes to the inbox (MN-01).
- Open a conversation or run, `adb shell am kill com.whombat.mobile` (or swipe from Recents after background kill),
  reopen from the launcher and from Recents: the same screen, the tab underneath as it was. A deleted run's screen
  falls back to the tabs.
- A run's live notification disappears 10 minutes after the app was killed (`dumpsys notification` shows
  `timeoutAfter`); with the app open it stays and updates.
- Read a thread on the web: its notification leaves the phone's shade while the app runs (managed: after `push.resolve`).
- Fresh install on Android 13 or later: the sheet at the first open after sign-in, no system dialog until “Turn on”;
  “Not now” (or closing the sheet) hides it for good, Settings → Notifications shows “Off · Turn on”. On Android 12 or
  older the app registers without a word.
- Fresh install on an iPhone: no question; Settings → Notifications says “On, quietly”, the first notification lands in
  Notification Center with iOS's Keep and Turn off, and Turn on alerts shows iOS's question.

## Left open

- With the app not running, reading elsewhere can't clear the shade: the server would need a `clear` per read thread
  (like NT-16 for decided approvals) — a Whombat follow-up, not built here.
