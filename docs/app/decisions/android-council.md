# The Android app, from the council (2026-11-21)

John, 2026-11-21: “Focus on the Android app and let the council decide how we can improve the Android app.” Four
reviewers (GPT 6.2 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5, Muse Wonder Full 1.0, Gemini 4 Neon) read the app's code, decisions and
history independently; no Android device was reachable, so the review was of the code. What three or more of them found
came first: Android's crashes left no trace, Chat could look old after coming back or on a Wi-Fi without internet, the
Notifications row could say “On” while Android showed nothing, and three native modules had races. Left for a round
with a real phone: dragging sheets down, predictive back, a run's live progress while away, Mermaid outside the
bundle, and the folding phone's two-page width.

## Chat stays current on Android (2026-11-21)

The Android council (2026-11-21) found four ways Chat looked old or quiet on an Android phone: a Wi-Fi without internet
paused every box read although the box was reachable; a socket that died while the app was away still counted as open
for 20–40 s after coming back; a reply within a minute of a quick visit got no notification (tried on the phone and withdrawn, AP-9743); and a cold start showed
skeletons on today's tabs because the saved copy held only pre-redesign reads. This extends live-data-calm.md; it
replaces none of it.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-9740 | **Box reads run while the connection is open, whatever the phone thinks of the internet** (`createBoxReach` in `managed/background.ts` feeds TanStack's `onlineManager`: open, or NetInfo says online). With neither, reads wait as before. Which reads are refetched doesn't change (AP-6560): the online flip goes through the same `refetchOnReconnect` rule. The outbox follows the same signal. The reach listener is registered before the one that asks the owed reads as the connection opens, so those reads find the phone online. | NetInfo's `isInternetReachable` is false on a LAN without internet, yet the box answers on its direct way. Every read paused there, and the catch-up on opening (`refetchOwedReads`) only asks idle reads, so a saved Chat list stayed old for as long as the phone was on that Wi-Fi. |
| AP-9741 | **NetInfo is only a hint for the connection.** While the connection isn’t open, it dials now when the phone is connected again after none, onto another network (its kind and address, `networkKey`), or online again after its internet check said no (a hotel’s sign-in page passed). While it is open, another network checks it (AP-9742). Nothing else counts: a repeated NetInfo state, or an internet check that was unknown and comes in as yes. Dialing is a no-op while open or dialing. | A LAN without internet must still dial at once on joining it. A phone that moves from Wi-Fi to mobile data keeps a socket tied to the old network. Behind a sign-in page the box may be out of reach until it is passed, and the backoff could otherwise hold the next try for up to 30 s. Only a real change may dial, or NetInfo’s frequent repeats would reconnect over and over. |
| AP-9742 | **An open connection is checked with one ping after 30 s or more away, and when the network changes** (`session.probe()`, `WAKE_CHECK_AFTER_MS`): no pong within the stall check’s current wait (2.5 s at first, `stallWait()`) and it is lost and dialed again at once, without the backoff; subscriptions resume from their cursors. Its own pong ends the check at once. A new check while one is out replaces it with a fresh ping and its own wait; back in the foreground, a check begun in the background pings again (`onlyIfChecking`). A ping that can’t be sent is a lost connection, dialed at once. A check whose wait ends far later than set (the app’s JavaScript was frozen) gets one more short wait on a fresh ping. A check over a connection that brought something in during its wait waits again, up to the stall check’s longest wait (20 s); silence, or no pong by then, is lost. A failed check doesn’t start a stuck spell. | Android stops the app’s timers in the background and mobile networks drop quiet sockets silently. On return the session said “open” while the socket was dead; `foreground()` did nothing and the heartbeat found out 20–40 s later. A network change right after a healthy answer, or during a check whose ping went out on the old network, must still get its own check. A timer that fires late after a freeze says nothing about the connection: the answer may be queued behind it. After a stay away a slow link may have a backlog to work through before the pong; frames still arriving show it is alive. A dead socket after a stay away says nothing about the network now, so it shouldn’t slow the next stall checks. |
| AP-9743 | **Tried and withdrawn: letting a connection opened in the last minute go as soon as the app goes to the background.** Every connection keeps the two-minute grace (AP-925). The fix for the push held after a quick visit belongs on the box: a visible/away signal from the app that ends its “at a screen” hold. Not in this change. | The box holds a push for a minute after one of the person’s connections opens, so a reply soon after a quick visit gives no notification. The phone-side release misfired both ways: in the common case (send, then leave) the send is still under way, and the retry is a JS timer Android doesn’t fire while the app is paused, so it never ran; and Android’s own screens (photo and document pickers, the microphone permission, Credential Manager, the share sheet, Custom Tabs) pause the app too, so it forced a new handshake on return from them. The box also renews its hold on later subscriptions, which the phone can’t see. |
| AP-9744 | **The saved copy holds what today’s tabs open with**: `chats.list`, `chats.threads` (a room’s open threads only — not settled ones, not a server’s or connection’s — newest page), `chats.unread` and `home.overview`. A pinned widget’s line keeps at most 96 readings spread over its day (`KEPT_WIDGET_POINTS`). These reads never push out what the copy held before: when it is too large they go first (the Overview, then the threads, then what is new, then the rooms), then conversations as before. A save is asked for only by a change to what is saved (`shouldPersistQuery`); a change to the Overview alone waits for the next other change or for the app going to the background. None carries a secret. | A cold start showed skeletons and empty badges on Chat and Overview until the session opened. A day of widget readings is thousands of points a widget and could have pushed the copy over its cap, which drops all of it. Pages opened on the way aren’t what a tab opens with, and their refetches shouldn’t seal the copy again. The Overview changes every minute (its time, the readings); saving it each time would seal the whole copy every minute. |
| AP-9745 | **Chat’s pull to refresh spins until the reads it started have answered or failed, or until they wait to get online** (`data/pullRefresh.ts`: TanStack pauses a read while offline and its promise stays open); only the reads the pull asked for count (by query hash), and the read goes on once online. **Chat’s inbox shows the “Updating…” / “Couldn’t update · Try again” pill** over its list, as a conversation does (`ReadStatePill` over `useChatThreads().read`); the pill hides while the pull’s own spinner shows, and while an empty list shows its own error. | The spinner snapped shut at once, so a pull looked like it did nothing; waiting on the read’s promise alone, it spun for ever offline and hid the pill. Another list of the same kind left waiting says nothing about this pull. The inbox replaced the conversation view before its pill, so Chat never said it was showing old threads, against live-data-calm’s rule that Chat does. |

## Android tells the truth (2026-11-21)

The Android app's crashes in native code, freezes and memory endings left no note, its source maps were thrown away at
each build, and Settings → Notifications could say “On” while Android showed nothing. From the Android council.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-9750 | **How Android says the app's earlier runs ended becomes error notes** (`modules/whombat-diagnostics` Android, `ApplicationExitInfo` on Android 11+; `errors/androidExits.ts`): a Java/Kotlin crash is `Crash`, a native crash `NativeCrash`, an app Android closed for not answering `Anr`, an ending for memory `LowMemory`, all `kind: 'app'`. User requests, force stops, updates (the app's own updater), the app's own exit and the rest are skipped. The module hands over only the app's own process's endings (`applicationInfo.processName`; a preview's WebView renderer is filed under the package too), newer than the newest one taken (its own SharedPreferences), in iOS's `pending` / `remove` shape; on Android they are read after the app's first frame (`noteEndingsAfterFirstFrame` from the root), not while its code loads; nothing below Android 11. “Ended while open” stays iPhone only. No contract change: the name is free text within `AppErrorNote`'s bounds. | The only place a native crash, a freeze or a memory ending is named on Android; the app's own code is gone before it could write anything, and Google's console names neither the build's JavaScript nor reaches the box. |
| AP-9751 | **An ending for memory is noted only while the app was on screen** (`importance <= IMPORTANCE_VISIBLE`). The module itself passes over a background one, and one older than a week, without reading its trace, and takes it when it is older than every ending handed over. | Android ends apps in the background for memory all the time, which is routine and not the app's fault (as iOS's background memory counts, AP-9410); one while the person is using it is a real problem. |
| AP-9752 | **Only structured facts are kept, bounded**: the reason, an ANR's main thread (its state, the lock it waits for rebuilt as its class and the holding thread, at most four native frames on top, then the rest, 20 frames), a native crash's signal and code and the crashed thread's name (only a known one: React Native's, Android's and the app's own names and forms like `binder:N_M`, else “another thread”) and 20 frames from Android's tombstone (protocol buffer, parsed in JavaScript without BigInt, base64 through a lookup table), the memory used, the build. **Android's free text is never kept**: an ending's description, the abort message and the causes are dropped; an ANR's description only gives its kind, matched against the start of Android's fixed words and said in ours (“input not handled in time”). Without the crashed thread's id the first thread is taken; with it, a crashed thread past the part read gives no frames and says “crashed thread not in the part read”. A frame's file is kept by name only when it is a code file (`.so`, `.apk`, `.oat`…, `base.apk!lib.so`); one in the app's or the person's data, an anonymous mapping or any other name is `(file)`. The module reads traces only for the newest three ANRs and native crashes (the other endings have none), at most 128 KB of an ANR's and 1 MB of a tombstone; a file is its name only. | The note goes to the box's log and the phone's shareable sync log, and free text from a library can carry an address, a link or a token. Another thread's frames would blame the wrong code. Reading traces costs start-up time. |
| AP-9753 | **A crash that a JavaScript error noted in that very run explains is not noted again**: each run has a random id, kept by Android with its ending (`setProcessStateSummary`, `… run=<id>`), and a fatal JavaScript note also writes its run's id into a small file beside the notes (`whombat-fatal-runs.json`, the newest 10); only a crash of a run in it is let go. A run of a build before run ids is always noted. and **an ending older than a week when first read is let go** (the first start of a build that reads them). Either counts as taken. | A fatal JavaScript error ends the app as a Java crash: the JavaScript note says far more. Matching by time alone would also swallow a separate Java crash in the next run moments later. The run id needs no contract change. A burst of old endings would push the recent ones out of the five-note file. |
| AP-9754 | **Each run labels itself for its ending** (`setProcessStateSummary`, `3.141.0 (3141000)`): the note's build is the build that ended, and its `at` the moment it ended. | Endings are read at the next start, often a newer build after an update. |
| AP-9755 | **`publish:apk` keeps each build's bundle source map and R8 mapping** beside its output, `<dir>/sourcemaps/<versionCode>-<rev>.map` and `.mapping.txt`, the newest 30 builds; never stops a publish. | A note's frames are lines of the minified bundle and R8's short names (release builds minify, AP-892). `<dir>/sourcemaps/` is outside `android/`, control serves only the one APK `latest.json` names, and `deploy.sh downloads` uploads only the APK and `latest.json`, so a map is never a download. Only a bundle map this run's build wrote (newer than its start) is kept, named after the revision it was made from (`-dirty` with local changes); `--skip-build` keeps none, as the map on disk may be another build's. R8's mapping is kept whenever it is there (Gradle leaves it as it was when R8 had nothing new to do, and it still matches). A publish that kept no bundle map ends with a loud warning. |
| AP-9756 | **Settings → Notifications says what Android holds back although notifications are allowed** (`WhombatLive.deliveryStatus`, `managed/pushView.ts`), most important first: the app-wide switch off (`enabled`, `areNotificationsEnabled`) or every Whombat channel off is “Not allowed” (as a denied permission) with *Open settings* to the app's notifications page; some off is “Partly on” · “Needs you” notifications are off in this phone’s settings.” with *Open settings* to that channel's page (the app's notifications page for several); the app's battery use restricted is “Held back” · “Battery use for Whombat is restricted, so notifications can’t arrive while it’s closed.” with *Open settings* to the app's page. The last-arrival time isn't shown then. | The row said “On” with a recent “Last one” while Android showed nothing: a channel turned off (a long press on one notification does it) or background restriction (data pushes don't reach a restricted app) were never checked, and on Android 8 to 12 turning the app-wide switch off neither ends the app nor marks its channels off, so a registered app never noticed. |
| AP-9757 | **At most one calm line while what matters arrives**, the dot staying green: “Runs in progress” turned off says so with *Open settings* to that channel (not “Partly on”); else Live Updates off (Android 16 QPR1+, looked up by name, and only where this phone has the Live Updates settings page for the app, a `<queries>` entry letting the app see it) with *Open settings* to its page; else battery optimisation says “This phone may hold Whombat’s notifications back to save battery.” with no button, and the dot stays green. The app never asks for a battery exemption. | Runs in progress is the low-priority progress channel, and turning it off is usually on purpose; the rest still arrives. Live Updates off is a choice with a way back, and a line about a setting the phone has no page for would be a dead end. Battery optimisation is Android's default for nearly every app and high-priority pushes come through it; a button would send people off to change a setting that is rarely the cause. |
| AP-9758 | **The status is read when the row shows and when the app comes back to the foreground**, locally (`managed/useDelivery.ts`); it never touches push registration, and iOS is as it was. | Coming back to the foreground is the person returning from Android's settings; registration has its own state (assignment §12). |

## Native fixes and a smaller app (Android council, 2026-11-21)

The council's review of the Android app (GPT 6.2 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5, Muse Wonder Full, Gemini 4 Neon) found races in
three native modules, a self-updater check that passed when it couldn't check, previews and sheets that waited or went
blank for nothing, a side tab out of reach, and two things in every download that no phone uses.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-9760 | **The voice recorder's reader thread owns the microphone and the file, and every recording has a number** (`modules/whombat-voice`): start, stop and cancel run one at a time (one lock); the JavaScript side numbers each recording (`gate.ts`), Android puts the number on every `onLevel` and `onEnd`, and the app drops events of any recording but the current one (iOS events carry none and count as the current one's). The reader alone releases the `AudioRecord` and closes the file, outside the recording's lock (a flag under it keeps a stop from touching a microphone being released), in a `finally` that also marks it released and wakes the waiters. Stop and cancel stop the microphone (which wakes a waiting `read()`) and wait up to 2 s; a reader still stuck after that deletes the file itself later, stop says “The microphone didn’t stop.”, cancel answers at once, and a later start waits for it once more and fails with that recorder error rather than open a second `AudioRecord`. The app closing lets go on a thread of its own, waiting for nothing. The file (a unique name in the cache) is made before the microphone opens; a WAV header that can't be written deletes it and is a recorder error, as is any file that can't be stored (never a microphone refusal). A microphone that gives nothing for 1 s ends the recording as an error. Events from background threads (here and in the fold module) can't throw. | Two quick starts left the first `AudioRecord` running with nothing pointing at it; an earlier recording's `onEnd` (cancel near the time limit, then start again) could submit or drop the next one; a failed header write skipped `release()`; a fast stop could release the microphone while the reader was still inside `read()`; a slow release or flush under the lock a stop needs could hold the stop without limit; zero reads during an audio route change spun a core; a `sendEvent` while the JavaScript runtime went away could end the reader before it let go. |
| AP-9761 | **The self-updater accepts only this app's signers, or a key rotated from ours, and fails closed** (`modules/whombat-updater`): it asks for `GET_SIGNING_CERTIFICATES` and `GET_SIGNATURES` together (only `GET_SIGNATURES` below Android 9), for the download and for this app. Several signers (from `signingInfo`, else the older `signatures`) must equal this app's set. One signer must be ours, or (Android 9+) a newer key whose own `signingCertificateHistory` holds our current key. Signers Android can't read are a `verify` failure (“The download didn’t check out…”), never a pass. | Below Android 9 (minSdk 24) the check never ran, and an empty signer list skipped it. Android 9 reads an archive's certificates only when `GET_SIGNATURES` is among the flags. A later release signed with a rotated key must pass, as Android installs it; one signed with an older key of ours must not (rotation only goes forward). Some phones fill only the older field for an archive. The SHA-256 from `latest.json` still comes first. |
| AP-9762 | **When Android can't open the per-app “Install unknown apps” page, the app opens its own app info page** (`openAppDetails`), and the note under “Allow installs” says where to look: the setting, the app info instead, or Settings → Apps → Whombat when neither opened (`COPY.update.allowWhere`, the `allow` step's `opened`). | Some phones can't open that page; the app ignored the `false` and left the person on “Allow installs” with nothing on screen. |
| AP-9763 | **The fold listener registers and unregisters only on the main thread, in call order** (`modules/whombat-fold`): `listen` and `stop` both post to the main looper, and a stop bumps a generation that outdates a `listen` asked for before it. | `listen` posted its registration while `stop` changed the tracker on the caller's thread: a quick leave and return could register after the stop (a listener left running in the background) or lose it. |
| AP-9764 | **A preview whose WebView browser went starts again** (`ui/htmlPreview.tsx` `PreviewPage`, `afterPreviewGone`): when Android let it go for memory while the app was in the background (`didCrash: false`, not `active`) it comes back uncounted once the app is in front (`AppState`); anything else — a crash, a release for memory while the app is in front, iOS's ended page process (which doesn't say why) — restarts it twice by itself, then “Couldn’t open this page.” with *Try again*, the page set viewer's way. One death reported twice is handled once (by the WebView's mount). A new page clears the count, the failure view, a waiting remount and the steady-load timer; a load that stays up 10 s clears the count. The failure view is at least the card's height and grows to fit its words and button. | Android kills a WebView's renderer to free memory (or it crashes); the card stayed blank until the row left the screen and came back. A page released while on screen may itself be what fills the memory, so it counts, or it would loop. Counting starts again only after a load stays up a while, so a page that crashes as it loads still stops. A short card (80 dp) would have clipped “Try again”. |
| AP-9765 | **On Android what a sheet opens comes on the next frame** (`whenSheetDown` in `ui/sheet.tsx`, used by `useAfterSheet`, which cancels it on unmount; Places, the Inbox's item sheet and the sheets that open sheets use it); iOS keeps waiting for the sheet to go down (600 ms at most). | The wait exists because iOS shows nothing new under a sheet still leaving. React Native's `Modal` never calls `onDismiss` on Android (iOS only, RN 0.86 `Modal.js`), so Android always waited the full 600 ms; there each sheet is a dialog of its own, and a new sheet or screen can open while one leaves. |
| AP-9766 | **The side tabs scroll when the window is shorter than they are** (`SideBar` in `navigation/TabBar.tsx`); when they fit they stay centred and nothing moves. A tap on a tab with the keyboard up switches tabs (`keyboardShouldPersistTaps="handled"`), and the tab list's role is on the view that holds the tabs. | Five 60 dp tabs under the clock didn't fit a short split-screen window, and the last tab (Settings) was out of reach. TalkBack counts tabs (“Tab 2 of 5”) only under their own tab list. |
| AP-9767 | **`@expo/ui` is left out of the Android app** (`expo.autolinking.android.exclude` in `apps/mobile/package.json`). Only the iPhone's `.ios.tsx` controls (`Segmented`, `ClockField`, `BackGlyph`) import it. | It linked Jetpack Compose and Material 3 (~1,500 classes, and the `compose` core feature) into every APK for nothing. Metro never resolves a `.ios.tsx` file for Android, so no Android code path reaches it. |
| AP-9768 | **The production APK is for `arm64-v8a` only**: `publish:apk`'s default, now also what the README and the scripts' instructions say; `--arch all` (x86_64 too) is for emulators. | The instructions said `--arch all`, which put the emulator's x86_64 libraries into every phone's download. No decision on architectures changes. |
