# The Android app, from the council (2026-11-21)

John, 2026-11-21: “Focus on the Android app and let the council decide how we can improve the Android app.” Four
reviewers (GPT 6.2 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5, Muse Wonder Full 1.0, Gemini 4 Neon) read the app's code, decisions and
history independently; no Android device was reachable, so the review was of the code. What three or more of them found
came first: Android's crashes left no trace, Chat could look old after coming back or on a Wi-Fi without internet, the
Notifications row could say “On” while Android showed nothing, and three native modules had races. Left for a round
with a real phone: dragging sheets down, predictive back, a run's live progress while away, Mermaid outside the
bundle, and the folding phone's two-page width.

## Chat stays current on Android (2026-11-21)

The Android council (2026-11-21) found four ways Chat looked old or quiet on an Android phone: a Wi-Fi without internet
paused every box read although the box was reachable; a socket that died while the app was away still counted as open
for 20–40 s after coming back; a reply within a minute of a quick visit got no notification; and a cold start showed
skeletons on today's tabs because the saved copy held only pre-redesign reads. This extends live-data-calm.md; it
replaces none of it.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-9740 | **Box reads run while the connection is open, whatever the phone thinks of the internet** (`createBoxReach` in `managed/background.ts` feeds TanStack's `onlineManager`: open, or NetInfo says online). With neither, reads wait as before. Which reads are refetched doesn't change (AP-6560): the online flip goes through the same `refetchOnReconnect` rule. The outbox follows the same signal. | NetInfo's `isInternetReachable` is false on a LAN without internet, yet the box answers on its direct way. Every read paused there, and the catch-up on opening (`refetchOwedReads`) only asks idle reads, so a saved Chat list stayed old for as long as the phone was on that Wi-Fi. |
| AP-9741 | **NetInfo is only a hint for the connection**: getting online, or onto another network (its kind and address, `networkKey`), dials now when the connection isn't open; another network while it is open checks it (AP-9742). A repeated NetInfo state (its internet check arriving) does nothing. | A LAN without internet must still dial at once on joining it. A phone that moves from Wi-Fi to mobile data keeps a socket tied to the old network. Only a real change may dial, or NetInfo's frequent repeats would reconnect over and over. |
| AP-9742 | **An open connection is checked with one ping after 30 s or more away, and when the network changes** (`session.probe()`, `WAKE_CHECK_AFTER_MS`): no pong within the stall check's first wait (2.5 s) and it is lost and dialed again at once, without the backoff; subscriptions resume from their cursors. A healthy one answers and stays. The probe's failure doesn't start a stuck spell (the stall check's wait isn't doubled). One check at a time. | Android stops the app's timers in the background and mobile networks drop quiet sockets silently. On return the session said “open” while the socket was dead; `foreground()` did nothing and the heartbeat found out 20–40 s later. A dead socket after a stay away says nothing about the network now, so it shouldn't slow the next stall checks. |
| AP-9743 | **A connection opened less than a minute ago is let go as soon as the app goes to the background** (`PUSH_HOLD_MS`), when nothing holds it (the same `keep` check: messages waiting, a live activity). Something holding it gets the usual two-minute grace; work under way is asked again after 30 s as before. An older connection keeps the grace (a quick look at another app). A connection opened while in the background (a push needing a read) keeps its grace too. Phone only; no server or contract change. | The box holds a push for a minute after one of the person's connections opens (they are at a screen). Open the app, ask, switch away: a reply within that minute gave no notification and nothing on the phone showed it. Releasing the fresh connection ends that hold. The background-opened case was left alone: releasing at once could drop the connection before the read it was opened for is sent. |
| AP-9744 | **The saved copy holds what today's tabs open with**: `chats.list`, `chats.threads` (the room's own lists only, newest page), `chats.unread` and `home.overview`. A pinned widget's line keeps at most 96 readings spread over its day (`KEPT_WIDGET_POINTS`). When the copy is too large the Overview goes first, then conversations as before; so it never holds less than before. None carries a secret. | A cold start showed skeletons and empty badges on Chat and Overview until the session opened. A day of widget readings is thousands of points a widget and could have pushed the copy over its cap, which drops all of it. A server's own threads list, read on its page on the way, isn't what a tab opens with. |
| AP-9745 | **Chat's pull to refresh spins until the reads it started have answered** (or failed), and **Chat's inbox shows the “Updating…” / “Couldn’t update · Try again” pill** over its list, as a conversation does (`ReadStatePill` over `useChatThreads().read`); the pill hides while the pull's own spinner shows. | The spinner snapped shut at once, so a pull looked like it did nothing. The inbox replaced the conversation view before its pill, so Chat never said it was showing old threads, against live-data-calm's rule that Chat does. |

## Android tells the truth (2026-11-21)

The Android app's crashes in native code, freezes and memory endings left no note, its source maps were thrown away at
each build, and Settings → Notifications could say “On” while Android showed nothing. From the Android council.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-9750 | **How Android says the app's earlier runs ended becomes error notes** (`modules/whombat-diagnostics` Android, `ApplicationExitInfo` on Android 11+; `errors/androidExits.ts`): a Java/Kotlin crash is `Crash`, a native crash `NativeCrash`, an app Android closed for not answering `Anr`, an ending for memory `LowMemory`, all `kind: 'app'`. User requests, force stops, updates (the app's own updater), the app's own exit and the rest are skipped. The module hands over the endings newer than the newest one taken (its own SharedPreferences), in iOS's `pending` / `remove` shape, at the start; nothing below Android 11. “Ended while open” stays iPhone only. No contract change: the name is free text within `AppErrorNote`'s bounds. | The only place a native crash, a freeze or a memory ending is named on Android; the app's own code is gone before it could write anything, and Google's console names neither the build's JavaScript nor reaches the box. |
| AP-9751 | **An ending for memory is noted only while the app was on screen** (`importance <= IMPORTANCE_VISIBLE`). | Android ends apps in the background for memory all the time, which is routine and not the app's fault (as iOS's background memory counts, AP-9410); one while the person is using it is a real problem. |
| AP-9752 | **Only the thread that mattered is kept, bounded**: an ANR's main thread (its state, the lock it waits for, at most four native frames on top, then the rest, 20 frames), a native crash's signal, abort message, causes, crashed thread name and its 20 frames read from Android's tombstone (protocol buffer, parsed in JavaScript without BigInt). The module reads at most 128 KB of an ANR's trace and 1 MB of a tombstone; a file is its name only; the command line, other threads, memory maps, log buffers and memory are never read into a note. | Enough to find the cause with the build's symbols, never the person's data; the parsing is pure and tested, the native side stays small. |
| AP-9753 | **A crash that a JavaScript error noted in that run explains is not noted again** (an `app` note within 30 s before it), and **an ending older than a week when first read is let go** (the first start of a build that reads them). Either counts as taken. | A fatal JavaScript error ends the app as a Java crash: the JavaScript note says far more. A burst of old endings would push the recent ones out of the five-note file. |
| AP-9754 | **Each run labels itself for its ending** (`setProcessStateSummary`, `3.141.0 (3141000)`): the note's build is the build that ended, and its `at` the moment it ended. | Endings are read at the next start, often a newer build after an update. |
| AP-9755 | **`publish:apk` keeps each build's bundle source map and R8 mapping** beside its output, `<dir>/sourcemaps/<versionCode>-<rev>.map` and `.mapping.txt`, the newest 30 builds; never stops a publish. | A note's frames are lines of the minified bundle and R8's short names (release builds minify, AP-892). `<dir>/sourcemaps/` is outside `android/`, control serves only the one APK `latest.json` names, and `deploy.sh downloads` uploads only the APK and `latest.json`, so a map is never a download. |
| AP-9756 | **Settings → Notifications says what Android holds back although notifications are allowed** (`WhombatLive.deliveryStatus`, `managed/pushView.ts`), most important first: every Whombat channel off is “Not allowed” (as a denied permission); some off is “Partly on” · “Needs you” notifications are off in this phone’s settings.” with *Open settings* to that channel's page (the app's notifications page for several); the app's battery use restricted is “Held back” · “Battery use for Whombat is restricted, so notifications can’t arrive while it’s closed.” with *Open settings* to the app's page. The last-arrival time isn't shown then. | The row said “On” with a recent “Last one” while Android showed nothing: a channel turned off (a long press on one notification does it) or background restriction (data pushes don't reach a restricted app) were never checked. |
| AP-9757 | **At most one calm line while everything arrives**: Live Updates off (Android 16 QPR1+, looked up by name) says so with *Open settings* to its page; else battery optimisation says “This phone may hold Whombat’s notifications back to save battery.” with no button, and the dot stays green. The app never asks for a battery exemption. | Live Updates off is a choice with a way back. Battery optimisation is Android's default for nearly every app and high-priority pushes come through it; a button would send people off to change a setting that is rarely the cause. |
| AP-9758 | **The status is read when the row shows and when the app comes back to the foreground**, locally (`managed/useDelivery.ts`); it never touches push registration, and iOS is as it was. | Coming back to the foreground is the person returning from Android's settings; registration has its own state (assignment §12). |

## Native fixes and a smaller app (Android council, 2026-11-21)

The council's review of the Android app (GPT 6.2 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5, Muse Wonder Full, Gemini 4 Neon) found races in
three native modules, a self-updater check that passed when it couldn't check, previews and sheets that waited or went
blank for nothing, a side tab out of reach, and two things in every download that no phone uses.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-9760 | **The voice recorder's reader thread owns the microphone and the file** (`modules/whombat-voice`): start, stop and cancel run one at a time (one lock, start's cancel-and-create inside it); the reader alone releases the `AudioRecord` and closes the file when it leaves its loop; stop and cancel stop the microphone (which wakes a waiting `read()`) and wait up to 2 s for it; a reader still stuck after that deletes the file itself later and stop says “The microphone didn’t stop.” Only the recording still current sends `onEnd`. A microphone that gives nothing for 1 s ends the recording as an error. A failure while starting always lets go of the microphone; one storing the file is a recorder error, not a microphone refusal. | Two quick starts left the first `AudioRecord` running with nothing pointing at it (the mic stayed on, and its `onEnd` could end the second recording); a failed header write skipped `release()`; a fast stop could miss the reader and release the microphone while it was still inside `read()`; zero reads during an audio route change spun a core. |
| AP-9761 | **The self-updater's signer check fails closed** (`modules/whombat-updater`): below Android 9 it compares `GET_SIGNATURES` with this app's own (they must be the same set); on 9 and later it asks for `GET_SIGNING_CERTIFICATES` and `GET_SIGNATURES` together; signers Android can't read are a `verify` failure (“The download didn’t check out…”), never a pass. | Below Android 9 (minSdk 24) the check never ran, and an empty signer list skipped it. Android 9 reads an archive's certificates only when `GET_SIGNATURES` is among the flags, so with `GET_SIGNING_CERTIFICATES` alone every update there would have failed once the check fails closed. The SHA-256 from `latest.json` still comes first. |
| AP-9762 | **When Android can't open the per-app “Install unknown apps” page, the app opens its own app info page** (`openAppDetails`), and the note under “Allow installs” says where to look: the setting, the app info instead, or Settings → Apps → Whombat when neither opened (`COPY.update.allowWhere`, the `allow` step's `opened`). | Some phones can't open that page; the app ignored the `false` and left the person on “Allow installs” with nothing on screen. |
| AP-9763 | **The fold listener registers and unregisters only on the main thread, in call order** (`modules/whombat-fold`): `listen` and `stop` both post to the main looper, and a stop bumps a generation that outdates a `listen` asked for before it. | `listen` posted its registration while `stop` changed the tracker on the caller's thread: a quick leave and return could register after the stop (a listener left running in the background) or lose it. |
| AP-9764 | **A preview whose WebView browser was stopped starts again** (`ui/htmlPreview.tsx` `PreviewPage`, `afterPreviewGone`): a new WebView twice by itself, then “Couldn’t open this page.” with *Try again*, the page set viewer's way. | Android kills a WebView's renderer to free memory (or it crashes); the card stayed blank until the row left the screen and came back. The page set viewer already recovered. |
| AP-9765 | **On Android what a sheet opens comes on the next frame** (`whenSheetDown` in `ui/sheet.tsx`, used by `useAfterSheet`, Places and the Inbox's item sheet); iOS keeps waiting for the sheet to go down (600 ms at most). | The wait exists because iOS shows nothing new under a sheet still leaving. React Native's `Modal` never calls `onDismiss` on Android (iOS only, RN 0.86 `Modal.js`), so Android always waited the full 600 ms; there each sheet is a dialog of its own, and a new sheet or screen can open while one leaves. |
| AP-9766 | **The side tabs scroll when the window is shorter than they are** (`SideBar` in `navigation/TabBar.tsx`); when they fit they stay centred and nothing moves. | Five 60 dp tabs under the clock didn't fit a short split-screen window, and the last tab (Settings) was out of reach. |
| AP-9767 | **`@expo/ui` is left out of the Android app** (`expo.autolinking.android.exclude` in `apps/mobile/package.json`). Only the iPhone's `.ios.tsx` controls (`Segmented`, `ClockField`, `BackGlyph`) import it. | It linked Jetpack Compose and Material 3 (~1,500 classes, and the `compose` core feature) into every APK for nothing. Metro never resolves a `.ios.tsx` file for Android, so no Android code path reaches it. |
| AP-9768 | **The production APK is for `arm64-v8a` only**: `publish:apk`'s default, now also what the README and the scripts' instructions say; `--arch all` (x86_64 too) is for emulators. | The instructions said `--arch all`, which put the emulator's x86_64 libraries into every phone's download. No decision on architectures changes. |
