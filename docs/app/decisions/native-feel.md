# The phone app feels native, as T3 Code's does (2026-10-31)

John, 2026-10-31, in the main checkout, after the swipe to Settle (chat-settle.md AP-6390) stuttered when a thread left
Chat: “I’m a big fan of native components … We could also use what T3 code uses, this is very smooth as well.” Then:
“Let’s do T3 code approach fully.” That is: gestures and animations on the display thread (react-native-gesture-handler
and Reanimated, as T3 Code's app), iOS's own tab bar on iPhone, and iOS's own switches and pickers in Settings (SwiftUI
through `@expo/ui`). One app for both phones stays: a rebuild in Swift and Kotlin would write every screen twice and
lose what the app shares with the web (the encrypted session, the data, the words), while Chat's messages are our own
drawing on any platform.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-6400 | **The app has a gesture library and runs animations on the display thread**: `react-native-gesture-handler` (its root round the whole app), `react-native-reanimated` with `react-native-worklets`, and `@expo/ui` for SwiftUI controls, at the versions Expo 57 lists (`expo install`). `@react-native/metro-config` is pinned to the app's React Native (0.86.3), which worklets asks for as a peer, so no second Metro comes along. Expo's Babel preset adds the worklets plugin by itself. Supersedes AP-6182's “the app has no gesture library” (chat-settle.md). | A gesture or animation run by JavaScript stutters whenever the app is busy drawing, which a swipe that changes the list always is. These are T3 Code's own choices. |

## Swipes and the list closing up (AP-6401…AP-6409)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-6401 | **A swipe row runs on the display thread** (`ui/swipeRow.tsx`): the gesture handler's pan follows the finger, Reanimated moves the row and its panels, slides it away and folds it; JavaScript only hears that an action runs, a row opened or closed, and the ticks. What it does is unchanged (ios-native.md AP-6274, chat-settle.md AP-6390…AP-6392); it settles with T3 Code's spring, and draws its panels from the first touch on. | The JavaScript swipe and fold stuttered while the list redrew as a thread left it. |
| AP-6402 | **From rest a row takes only a sideways drag towards a side with actions**; a drag that goes 10 dp up or down first is the list's scroll. A pull to the right on a row with only trailing actions goes on to the iPhone's way back. | The gesture handler decides which gesture gets a touch before it moves anything. |
| AP-6403 | **Chat doesn't scroll to its end while its list only gets shorter** (`followAction`'s `shrank`): the scroll view keeps its end by itself, so the messages above slide down with the fold. A new message at the same time is followed as before. | Chat scrolled to its end on every frame of the fold, fighting it: the stutter John saw. |
| AP-6404 | **A table, a code block or a page in a message keeps a sideways drag** by blocking the row's swipe the gesture handler's way (`KeepsSideways` in `ui/sideways.tsx`: its own native gesture blocks the row's pan while it scrolls). Selected words need no guard: iOS gives the drag of a selection's handles to the text. Supersedes how chat-settle.md AP-6393 did it. | One gesture system decides; a mark on the touch could lose a race with the display thread. |
| AP-6405 | **On a row taller than 112 dp the panels' icon and word sit level with the finger** (a 112 dp box round where the drag began, kept inside the row). Supersedes chat-settle.md AP-6391's “in its top 112”. | On Android a long message scrolled half under Chat's header hid its panels' words; the finger is always on the screen. |



## The tab bar (AP-6410…AP-6419)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-6410 | **On an iPhone the tabs are iOS's own tab bar** (`navigation/GlassTabs.tsx`): UIKit's tab bar controller through react-native-screens' `Tabs`, the Liquid Glass bar of iOS 26 and later, with the same five tabs and words (`TAB_WORDS`). It is a navigator of our own on React Navigation's tab router, so the routes, the links and pushes into a tab and the place kept across a killed process are unchanged. iOS picks the tab on a tap and says so; a tab the app opens (a link, “See them”, a push) goes to iOS as a request based on the last selection iOS confirmed, and one older than a tap is turned down. Which bar a window gets is `tabBarKind` (`systemTabs.ts`, unit-tested): iOS's along the bottom of an iPhone, ours on Android and down the side. | John: “Let’s do T3 code approach fully”; T3 Code's iPhone app puts UIKit's bar over its screens the same way, giving it only icons, titles and a tint. React Navigation's own native tab view (`createNativeBottomTabNavigator`) sets its theme's font, colours and a blur on every tab, which makes the labels 14 points and draws a blur in place of the glass. |
| AP-6411 | **The icons are filled SF Symbols** close to our bar's: `message.fill`, `tray.fill`, `powerplug.fill`, `bolt.fill`, `gearshape.fill`. The open tab is in `brand`, the others in iOS's own label colour; the bar doesn't shrink while a list scrolls. | Apple's own apps fill their tab icons. `brand` is the colour the app taps in (back links), as iOS's tint. Five tabs with words stay readable at full size. |
| AP-6412 | **Superseded by docs/decisions/phone-shell-final.md PSH-06 (badges in the unread pill's colours).** **The badges are iOS's**: Chat's unread and Needs you's count as numbers (“99+” past 99), red, as iOS draws them. Settings' dot is iOS's badge without a number (a zero-width space: react-native-screens passes an empty value on as no badge, and UIKit's own empty badge looks the same, checked). VoiceOver hears iOS's reading of a count (“Chat, 1 item”) and, for the dot, the tab's name with what it means (“Settings, a newer app is ready”). The tones (the `btn` pill for unread, `warn`/`bad` on Needs you, the Settings dot's `ok`/`warn`/`bad`) stay on our bar. | UIKit takes a badge's colour from the bar's look, which is the selected tab's, so a colour per tab would change with the selection. UIKit has no small dot; the words carry what the dot means. |
| AP-6413 | **Superseded in part by AP-6543** (the room is counted by `useBottomRoom`, which also counts the window's edge). **A tab's screen runs under the glass.** How much of it the bar covers is measured in the tab (`SafeAreaListener` inside the tab, whose safe area includes the bar) and given as `useGlassBarRoom()`; every screen keeps the window's own insets, so a sheet opened from a tab sits as before. Needs you, Connections, Automations and Settings add the room under their last row: they scroll on under the bar and end above it. Chat's reply box sits 10 points above the bar (`composerBottomPadding`, `glass-bar`) with its background running under the glass, and on the keyboard while typing. react-native-screens' scroll-view inset change is off. | iOS sets the bar's height and the room under it; the safe area follows whatever iOS draws. Changing the screens' insets instead would lift every sheet by the bar's height. |
| AP-6414 | **Tabs out of view under iOS's bar aren't frozen**; a page over the tabs still freezes all of them, and our bar still freezes its tabs (AP-904, android-slim.md). A tab draws its screen the first time it shows and keeps it (Chat alone at the start, AP-2644). A new badge count or iOS's selection draws only the bar: the screens come in drawn. | Freezing hides a tab's views until JavaScript catches up, while iOS shows the tab the moment it is tapped, so every switch would flash an empty page. |
| AP-6415 | **On the iPhone Duo and wide windows the side tabs stay** (iphone-duo.md AP-5804). iOS 27.1 draws its own bar there as a glass rail of icons in the strip under the clock (checked on the Duo simulator, folded and open), but only inside the tab controller's own view. Here the tabs are one route of the stack (AP-5805): with two pages they are the left page, so the rail would sit in the middle of the open Duo, and under a thread the strip would go empty where our side tabs stay. Both poses of the Duo get the side tabs, so folding never changes the bar; a window that changes between the two bars (none on iPhone today: portrait only, no iPad) draws the other on the tab it showed (AP-6340). | iOS's rail fits an app whose tab controller holds everything; ours holds the list. The rail also drops the tabs' words. |
| AP-6416 | **Tapping the open tab again does nothing**, as on our bar. | react-native-screens finds a tab's list only when it is the first view all the way down, and ours sit under a header (only its experimental marker would reach them); Chat would jump to its first message. |

AP-6410…AP-6416 were checked with `pnpm --filter @whombat/mobile typecheck` and `test` and the demo world's Release build
on an iPhone 18 Pro simulator (iOS 27.0) and an iPhone Duo simulator (iOS 27.1), light and dark: every tab, the
badges, a tab opened by a link and by “See them”, Settings → Advanced over the tabs and back, Chat's box at rest and with
the keyboard, and the Duo folded and open with Automations kept across the fold.



## Switches and pickers (AP-6420…AP-6429)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-6420 | **On iPhone the segmented control is iOS's own**: `Segmented.ios.tsx` draws SwiftUI's `Picker` in its segmented style through `@expo/ui`, in a `Host` as wide as its place and as tall as the control, for the same props as ours (`SegmentedProps`); Android keeps `Segmented.tsx`. The `Host` takes the app's light or dark (the in-app choice, not only the phone's), the large control size (44pt, the touch target ours padded to) and the caller's `label` as the group's name for VoiceOver; each segment keeps its words, `disabled` dims it and keeps the chosen one legible. iOS paints the segments in its own greys: SwiftUI gives a segmented picker no tint, and we don't paint over iOS's control. | John: “Let’s do T3 code approach fully.” T3 Code's iPhone app draws iOS's segmented control too (a UIKit view of its own); `@expo/ui`'s SwiftUI picker is the same control without a native module of ours. |
| AP-6421 | **A segmented choice whose words don't fit becomes iOS's menu picker**: the chosen one's words and ⌃⌄ in `brand`, opening iOS's menu with a check on the chosen one; VoiceOver reads the caller's `label` and the choice. `segmentedLook` (pure) decides from the labels alone: each must fit an equal share of the narrowest iPhone iOS 27 runs on (the SE, 375pt, less a page's and a part's 16pt sides: 311pt) at 13pt semibold, with an estimate that errs wide; more than five choices is always a menu. Today that makes menus of What pops up, Pages turn and the top bar's Always · When it matters · Off, among others, and segments of the theme, the keys, the ranges, Size and At night. The menu is pulled back by the 12pt iOS keeps each side of its words, so they line up with a card's text. `lines` and `fit` stay for Android. | iOS's segments are as wide as each other and cut a long label with “…”; they can't wrap as ours do. Apple's own Settings put longer choices in a menu, and a menu shows every word. Decided by the narrowest phone, not the one in hand, so one choice looks the same on every iPhone. Falling back to ours would leave the one non-native control on the page. |
| AP-6422 | **The `HH:MM` field is iOS's compact time picker on iPhone** (`ClockField.ios.tsx`, SwiftUI `DatePicker`, hour and minute; the quiet hours in Settings → Notifications and on the desk screen): tapping it opens iOS's wheels, and each time picked saves the same `HH:MM` as before. The picker holds 1 January 2000 at that time in UTC and is told to read it in UTC (`clockDate`); iOS shows 24 or 12 hours as the phone is set. The label beside it stays ours. | A typed time on a phone is a keyboard, a format to get right and a value put back when it was wrong; the wheels can't be wrong. UTC keeps a time zone or a summer-time night from moving the hour between the app and the picker. |
| AP-6423 | **Do not disturb's “Until” is the same picker on iPhone**, starting at the next whole hour on the person's clock (`nextWholeHour`), with Set beside it as before; VoiceOver still calls it “Do not disturb until this time”. Android keeps the typed time. | It sits right above the quiet hours' pickers: one way to give a time on one page. |
| AP-6424 | **iOS's pickers show what their caller says** (`useHeldPick`): SwiftUI moves a picker as soon as it is touched, so a pick is held until the caller's value changes, and when it hasn't within 4 s (a question first, a refusal, a failed save) the picker is drawn again from the value. | Callers ask first (a server's “Let Whombat go ahead?”) or take a pick only once it's saved; the picker mustn't keep showing a choice nobody took. 4 s covers a save over the session. |
| AP-6425 | **Switches stay React Native's `Switch`**, colours from `switchColors`. | On iPhone it already is iOS's own switch (UISwitch), the same control SwiftUI's `Toggle` draws; a SwiftUI host per switch adds a view and a layout pass for no difference. T3 Code's iPhone app keeps React Native's switch too. |
| AP-6426 | **Richer choices stay ours**: radio cards with a line under each choice (Remote help's How much, Add a server's and Connect's keys), a backup's kinds, the desk's voices with Listen, and a widget's servers and parts (rows with a mark and a check, already in iOS's own list shape). The thing pages' range control (`RangeControl`, outside Settings) stays ours for now, with its spoken “Last 24 hours”. | A menu would drop the words or the buttons that explain each choice. |
