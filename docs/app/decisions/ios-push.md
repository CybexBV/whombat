# Pushes on the iPhone (2026-11-01)

John, 2026-11-01: “We need to add support for push notifications for iOS”. The app already asked for permission,
registered its APNs token with control and opened a tapped notification's screen; control already had an APNs sender.
DEV control had no APNs key, so every push to the iPhone failed (appliance IN-518 adds the key). Reading the app against
expo-notifications' iOS code showed three gaps against Android, closed here. Phone only: no contract or runtime change.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-6440 | **A push's Whombat keys are read from the trigger's payload** (`pushPayload`, `push/iosPayload.ts`): for a remote notification expo-notifications puts only the Expo push service's `body` key in `content.data`, and the whole APNs payload in `trigger.payload`. A tap (`push/taps.ts`), the shade check and the inbox refresh all read it there; `content.data` stays the fallback for a local notification. | A managed Whombat's alert carries `w: {kind, ref}` at the payload's top level, so a tapped notification found no data and opened nothing. |
| AP-6441 | **`phoneNotifications` is the one place the app reads, removes and hears its notifications on both systems** (`push/phoneNotifications.ts`): Android through `modules/whombat-live`, iOS through expo-notifications (`getPresentedNotificationsAsync`, `dismissNotificationAsync`, `addNotificationReceivedListener`, `dismissAllNotificationsAsync`). The shade check, `useManagedPush`, the live-activity bridge and sign-out use it. On iOS a notification's tag is its request identifier, which iOS sets to the push's `apns-collapse-id` (the thread or approval, as Android's tag), and its date comes in seconds. | Read in the app, gone from the phone (MN-04) and an inbox that refreshes when a push arrives worked on Android only; sign-out left the iPhone's notifications on screen. |
| AP-6442 | **A Whombat alert shows while the app is open on the iPhone too** (`showWhileOpen`: banner, list and sound; other notifications keep the system's default), as Android shows it. The shade check removes it once read. | iOS hides a notification that arrives while the app is in front unless the app says otherwise, so a mention or an approval went unseen while the person was elsewhere in the app. |
| AP-6443 | **No background task for control's `clear` pushes on iOS.** iOS hands a background push to the app only through a registered background task (expo-task-manager and the `remote-notification` background mode), and not at all after the person swiped the app away. A notification read elsewhere goes when the app is next open or comes back to the front, and a newer one for the same thread or approval replaces it (`apns-collapse-id`). | One more native dependency and background mode for a clean-up the shade check already does when the app opens; revisit if read notifications piling up on the iPhone bother anyone. |
| AP-6444 | **An iPhone's notifications come on quietly, with no question** (John, 2026-11-27; mobile-reach.md MN-16). The first open of a session that can register asks iOS for provisional authorization (`requestPermissionsAsync({ ios: { allowProvisional: true, allowAlert: true, allowBadge: true, allowSound: true } })`): no prompt shows, iOS grants it at once, and the app registers its APNs token as for full permission. Notifications then arrive quietly: in Notification Center only, without a sound, a banner, the lock screen or a badge, and the first carries iOS's own Keep and Turn off. Keep → Deliver Immediately gives the alert, badge and sound asked for; Turn off denies. Asking for full alerts later (Settings → Notifications' Turn on alerts: the default request without `allowProvisional`) shows iOS's question once and upgrades it. expo-notifications reports quiet as `status: 'undetermined'`, `granted: false`, `ios.status` PROVISIONAL, so the app reads that itself (`allowedQuietly`). A Not now kept from before isn't overridden. | Turned on by themselves wherever possible, without a cold system question; iOS's quiet delivery was made for this. |
