# Who looks after a connection, and what an agent looks after (AP-863…AP-870)

John, 2026-10-08: “I'd like each agent to have their own responsibilities just like in real life.” Whombat
`docs/decisions/agent-responsibilities.md` (OW-01…OW-12) gives every agent its own area and lets agents pass a
person's message on to the teammate whose area it is (`pass_on`, OW-04). A shared connection is **looked after by** a team
agent (OW-09: by default the first-hired active team agent of the entry's category role — homelab and infrastructure the
Operator, developer services the Software Developer, business, productivity and files the Associate), or by nobody; people
who may manage the connection change it. OW-09 and OW-10 are the clients' part; contracts 0.62.0 (CT-315).

| ID | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AP-863 | **Contracts 0.62.0, and one set of words and choices for web and phone.** The vendored contracts move to 0.62.0 (`SESSION_API_LEVEL` 36, window 34…37), so the app's minor goes to 0.52.0. `FEATURE_NAMES` names `agents.pass_on` (“passing a message on to a teammate”) and `connections.looked_after` (“who looks after a connection”). `@whombat/client`'s `lookedAfter.ts` holds the copy — “Looked after by”, “Nobody”, “Looks after” verbatim, the lines “Other agents pass questions about it on to Dusty.” / “No agent looks after it, so whoever is asked answers.”, Nobody's choice line “Whoever is asked answers.”, the toast “Dusty looks after PVE-01 now.” / “Nobody looks after PVE-01 now.” — and the rules: `showsLookedAfter` (feature and shared, never a personal connection), `lookedAfterLine` (“Looked after by Dusty”, null for nobody), `caretakerChoices` (= `pickableAgents`: the burrow's active team agents by name; no people, personal assistants, system principals or retired agents, as the server refuses them) and `connectionsLookedAfterBy`. The new trigger `passed` needs nothing in the app: a pass is ordinary messages (OW-04), and the app has no exhaustive map over `DispatchTrigger`. | The spec's words are John's product language; one module keeps the web and Android saying the same thing, and the rules that decide whether it shows at all are tested once. |
| AP-864 | **A shared connection's CONNECTED row says “Looked after by Dusty”.** In the row's meta line after “Connected by Mara”: “Connected by Mara Okafor · Looked after by Dusty · 6 tools”. Nothing when nobody looks after it, on a personal connection, on a runtime without `connections.looked_after`, and on a row that needs connecting again (that line says why and what to do). | The row is where people scan who does what; the meta line already carries the connection's facts in that order, so it reads as one more fact rather than a new element. A reauth row's sentence stays the only thing on its line. |
| AP-865 | **The connection page's CONNECTION card has LOOKED AFTER BY.** A block between the facts (CONNECTED BY … LAST USED) and WHAT AGENTS MAY DO, built like the latter: the mono caps label (the DOM text is “Looked after by”, uppercased by CSS), the agent's 24px head avatar and name — or Nobody with an empty dashed ring — and the line under it. People who may manage the connection (`canManage`) get **Change** (accessible name “Change who looks after it”): radio cards in WHAT AGENTS MAY DO's style, two to a row, each team agent with its avatar and role (“Operator”, “Software Developer”), then Nobody, with Save and Cancel; it saves `connections.update` `{lookedAfterById}` (null for Nobody) and toasts. Everyone else reads it. Shared connections on runtimes with `connections.looked_after` only. | The choice needs avatars (a native `<select>` can't show them) and must stay a real, labelled form control: a fieldset of radios with a legend does both and matches the page's other choice. An explicit Save keeps it in line with WHAT AGENTS MAY DO and Rename. |
| AP-866 | **The agent profile (web) lists LOOKS AFTER.** After CHANNELS, a team agent's profile pane lists the labels of the shared connections whose `lookedAfterBy.id` is that agent, by label, each a link to the connection's page (`/settings?tab=connections&connection=<entry>&id=<id>`), styled like the Vault's profile rows. Read from `connections.list` (members may read shared connections, CX-25); not asked for personal or system agents; hidden when it looks after none or the runtime lacks the feature. | The profile is where a person learns what an agent is for; its area includes what it looks after (OW-01). |
| AP-867 | **Android: the row line, and LOOKED AFTER BY on the connection's screen, changed on the phone.** The CONNECTED row gets the same “Looked after by Dusty” line (mono meta under the state, in its TalkBack label). The connection screen gets a LOOKED AFTER BY section after CONNECTION: the agent's avatar (36dp) or Nobody's dashed ring, its name and the line. For people who may manage it the row opens `CaretakerSheet`: a bottom sheet in the plan OwnerPicker's language, 56dp rows (radios for TalkBack, the current one checked and marked ✓), the team agents with avatar, role dot and role, then Nobody; a tap saves at once (`connections.update`), with Android's toast. This is the one thing about a connection the phone changes; connecting, access levels, tools and removing stay on the web (CX-74). | Who looks after a connection is a light, everyday choice, not a credential or a risk change, so asking people to go to the web for it would be friction for nothing. A tap that saves matches the phone's other one-choice sheets (Reassign). |
| AP-868 | **Android's agent screen lists LOOKS AFTER.** After CHANNELS, a team agent's screen lists the shared connections it looks after, each with its logo and opening the connection's screen; hidden when none or without the feature. | Parity with the web's profile (AP-866). |
| AP-869 | **Fixtures and what is not covered.** The web's catalog fixtures give a new shared connection the first fixture agent of `caretakerRoleFor` (Dusty looks after Home Assistant) and take `lookedAfterById` (a fixture agent or null; refused on a personal connection), so `VITE_FIXTURE_CONNECTORS=connected` shows all of it. Verified visually on the web at 1440×900, light and dark (overview row, connection page reading and changing, Nobody, the agent profile). Not done: no screenshot of the read-only (`canManage: false`) web state (the fixtures always manage; the branch is a plain condition), and Android was checked by unit tests and the type checker only, not on an emulator. | DEV is fast (no emulator builds for a field and a sheet in existing patterns); the remaining risk is layout on the phone, which uses the screens' existing rows and the OwnerPicker sheet unchanged. |
| AP-870 | **Released as whombat-app 0.52.0** (minor: contracts 0.62.0 raises the session API level): version bump, tag `app-v0.52.0`, `pnpm release:dist` from the tag. | Like 0.51.0. |
