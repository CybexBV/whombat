# UI refresh: Inbox and approval cards (UIR)

From the 2026-11-04 design council (its final spec, §2 Inbox and §3 Approval card) and John's “implement this fully”.
The coordinator settled beforehand that Settled stays pinned to the bottom of the screen (inbox.md IBX-09) as a compact
bar, and that the assistant's name comes from the name helpers. Ids UIR-30…UIR-59; the other slices of the refresh have
their own files.

Changes inbox.md IBX-05 (its words only: *Ask {assistant} to fix it* becomes *Ask {assistant} to handle it*) and IBX-09
(the empty bar now opens), and needs-you-list.md NYL-08's words for an approval's buttons.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| UIR-30 | **Superseded in part by approval-card.md ACD-13 (no line limit on a description).** **An Inbox row**: its tile (32px), the title 15.5/600, its description 14.5 `--ink2` on a line of its own (no leading “·”), at most two lines on a desktop and four on a phone, then a mono 12 `--mut` line “source · time” (the source only when the title doesn't already name it), padding 14px 20px. What the assistant says sits under them. The Inbox has its own row styles; Connections' slim row stays as it is. | The council's calmer rows: title, then reason, then where and when, so a row reads in one pass. |
| UIR-31 | **Times read relative first**: the clock today (“10:12”), else “1 day ago”, “3 days ago” (`needsYouWhen`, shared by the web, the phone and Home). | “3 d ago” is a developer's abbreviation. |
| UIR-32 | **Not working is the tile alone**: an item that isn't working (severity `bad`, in Needs you) gets a `--bad` tile at 12% with the alert icon; every other row a calm `--sf2` tile with its own icon (the warn tint is gone); no left rule, no coloured title. | One loud cue per event. |
| UIR-33 | **What isn't working comes first in Needs you**, by a stable sort in `needsYouListView`; otherwise the server's order. No contract change. | The worst thing first, on the web and the phone alike. |
| UIR-34 | **The buttons sit in a right column only when the list itself is at least 1000px wide** (a container query on the group's card), at most 340px wide; narrower they wrap under the words, aligned with them; at phone width (≤600px) they span the row at 44px, the way forward grows, ⋯ (44×44, “More for {title}”) last. The Inbox's page is up to 1104px wide so a desktop list reaches 1000px. 340px, not the spec's 300, is the approved mockup's: at 300 “Ask Dusty to handle it · Settle · ⋯” and an approval's three answers with ⋯ wrap. | The mockup wins on layout; a column that wraps defeats its purpose. |
| UIR-35 | **A problem has one verb, “Ask {assistant} to handle it”**: an only-you line the assistant can do keeps its own action (it asks the assistant to fix it, `askFixText`) under the same words. | Two verbs for the same tap made the person choose between words. |
| UIR-36 | **Every Settle shows its Undo for 8 s**, paused while the pointer is on the toast or focus is in it (the toast region, `role="status"`); Undo is the same call with `ignored: false`, and the row comes back. Focus moves to the first button of the next row (the one before it at the end). The words stay `needsYouSettledText`'s: a line Whombat watches says “Settled. It comes back if it happens again.”, a suggestion, which settles for good (IBX-03), says “Settled.” | Settling one row after another from the keyboard; a toast that doesn't promise what won't happen. |
| UIR-37 | **Superseded in part by approval-card.md ACD-14 (no “· 0”).** **Settled is a flush 44px bar under the list**, “Settled · 3”, with no card or shadow; the list scrolls above it, so it never covers a row. It always opens: into its rows (their own scroll, at most 45% of the screen), or, with nothing settled, into the line that says what it is for. | IBX-09's place, the council's calm; the explanation only when someone asks for it. |
| UIR-38 | **Superseded in part by approval-card.md ACD-04 (the details use the card's answers; none is filled).** **An approval's answers keep one order and words everywhere**: “Yes, now · Yes, tonight · No” where a later time is offered, “Yes, do it · No” where it isn't (core `APPROVAL_ASK_WORDS`). The server's Inbox items use them; the client orders and names an approval's actions by kind, so an older Whombat's “Yes, do it · Tonight” reads the same. The approval's details show “Yes, do it · No” in that order. The yes is the one filled button. | One order means no misreading between the thread, the Inbox and the details. |
| UIR-39 | **No suggestion on an ask, for now**: an approval carries none, and most everyday asks (a server's or connection's call) are made by the server from the agent's tool call, not by an ask tool. A suggestion would need a field in the contract, the tool's schema, a column and a release, for a label. So the filled button stays the yes with no “… SUGGESTS” label. Revisit when the agent can say when it would rather do something. | Not worth a contracts release while nothing would set it. |
| UIR-40 | **No consequence line**: nothing on an approval says what follows (its explainer is about the key, its summary repeats the title), so none is shown and no field is added now (UIR-39's reasons). | A line only when the data gives one. |
| UIR-41 | **The lasting yes sits apart and asks first**: under a `--line` divider, a text button “Don’t ask again for {server}…” (a risk row's “Always allow {assistant} to …”) opens “Let {assistant} do this on {server} without asking?” · “Next time it does it and tells you after. You can change this in Settings.” · “Let {assistant} do it” / “Keep asking”. Confirming is the old button's decision (`approvals.decide {scope: 'agent'}`), on the card and in the approval's details (`LastingYes`). | A quick tap never gives a standing permission. |
| UIR-42 | **An answered ask says where it is** (`approvalAnswer` in core, from the approval's state, `executeAt`, execution and decider): “Scheduled for tonight, 03:00” (the real time; today, tomorrow or the weekday otherwise), “Running”, “Done · 09:46”, “It didn’t work”, “You said no · 09:46” (someone else's: “Mara said no”). An answered approval is still a line that opens its card (card-settle.md AP-730): an everyday ask's line leads with this state before its title, and the card keeps its title with the state under it. Undo stays on the line. | The person sees what their answer did, in the same words in both places. |
| UIR-43 | **The card's frame**: `--warn` 1.5px while it waits, `--line` 1px once answered. On a phone its answers are a grid of two columns of 44px buttons in the same order, a third (No) across the second row. | Waiting is the only state that asks for attention. |
