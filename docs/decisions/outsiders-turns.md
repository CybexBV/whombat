# Outsiders' turns are deny by default and run one at a time (OT-01…OT-20)

The lead, 2026-10-26: a Whombat is one person's device (one-person.md OP-01). Outsiders (website feedback reporters,
The tracker customers, people who mail the person) only send input in, and what they send runs as an outsiders' turn, queued
for nobody (`runtime/person.ts` `outsidersTurn`, `DispatchContext.outside`), in Hermes's `_feedback` profile (OP-17,
OP-S41, OP-S81). Prompt injection in what strangers send must not reach the person's things or put words or actions
anywhere else. Membership's removal confined the tools that name a conversation to the turn's own (membership.md MB-04),
but such a turn was still served nearly every tool: a `TurnShape` had no notion of “outside”, only connections, the
person's accounts and the person-only tools were left out, and a tool left out of a turn's list still ran when called
by name.

**What an outsiders' turn is today** (2026-10-26, `apps/server/src`):

- a **Feedback assessment** (`feedback/assessment.ts`, trigger `feedback`): its own five tools (feedback.md FB-21), in the
  report's thread in the person's Feedback room;
- the **agent steps of a run a tracker report or a customer's reply starts** (`automations/runs.ts` `outsidersRun`, trigger
  `automation`): the assessment step (`report_assessment`), a coding step in the checkout its step made (the runner pushes
  after the turn; the automation opens the pull request itself when its recipe has that step), a review step that
  answers with a verdict, an answer step whose final reply the automation sends to the customer; `tracker_item` and,
  when granted, `tracker_read`. Each runs in its own thread of the run's room, which `automations/rooms.ts` makes the
  publisher's Feedback room (an assessing recipe) or their **Chat**;
- **incoming mail** (`automations/runs.ts` `outsidersRun`, trigger `automation`): a mail-triggered run's agent step
  gets the matched email fenced (`[[mail …]] … [[/mail]]`) and its attachments handed over onto the prompt message
  (`read_attachment`), with no direct access to the person's mailbox, files or connections; proposed writes go through
  `propose_connection_actions` and a `connection_actions` step that always asks the person on a card first.

**What such a turn reached before** (each was served, or ran when called by name): the rest of the person's Chat or
Feedback room (`read_conversation` of the room, top-level posts, replies and cards in any thread there), files on the
person's recent Chat messages and the Chat's Files (`read_attachment`, `list_files`, the context's `files` and the
runner's file fetch), the person's schedules made in that room (`schedule` list and cancel), Home (`report_change`,
whose Undo wakes a person's turn with the outsider-influenced words; `set_status`), plans and canvases of the room,
approval cards and actions under the agent's keys (`request_approval`, `perform_action`: merges, deploys, refunds,
outside messages), runs and pull requests on any repository (`start_run`, `open_pull_request`), reviews and merges of
any pull request (`review_pull_request`, `merge_pull_request`), deploy records and automation drafts.

## One at a time (OT-13…OT-18)

John, 2026-11-09, after a council review of the tracker bug-report flow: “The whole tracker run must work without my
input. Keep full access and the flow as it is.” Every outsiders' turn runs in Hermes's one `_feedback` profile, behind
one host lock that a turn waited at most ten minutes for on the runner before failing. A tracker step that hands work to
T3 Code (a look up to 120 minutes, a fix up to 360, a review up to 180) keeps its turn, and so the lock, for the whole
wait, so any other report or customer reply meanwhile failed and its run with it (“Dusty couldn't finish”), with nothing
trying again: Acme dev report #20's “Nevermind, you can close this ticket” (automation run 16) failed after about 20
minutes behind report #21's fix.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| OT-13 | **Outsiders' turns are queued at the claim** (`runtime/service.ts` `claimInTx`, `runtime/places.ts` `outsidersTurnsInFlight`): an outsiders' turn isn't claimed while another outsiders' turn anywhere on the box holds its place — claimed, running, or stopped while its runner still holds it (`holdsItsPlace`, the rule of a turn's own place; as there, a run's turn isn't held back by the turn that started the run). It stays `queued`, never fails for the wait, and is claimed oldest first once none is left. A person's turn never waits for them. Website Feedback assessments and incoming mail queue the same way. The runner keeps its ten-minute wait for the profile's lock as a fallback. | The profile takes one Hermes at a time, so the box runs one outsiders' turn at a time; waiting in the queue is free and durable, waiting on the runner's lock failed after ten minutes. A pool of outsiders' profiles was not chosen: it ripples into the Hermes-home contracts, backups, session views and the one-person-files migration, which reads unknown `profiles/*` folders as old accounts. |
| OT-14 | **An agent step's time counts from its turn's claim** (`automations/engine/observe.ts`): a `dispatch_agent` step whose turn is still queued doesn't time out on its step deadline (the run's own `runTimeoutMinutes` still bounds the wait); once its turn is claimed, the step's deadline is the claim (`dispatches.claimed_at`) plus the step's timeout. A turn claimed again (after a crash, or OT-16) starts its time again. Every agent step, not only outsiders'. | A reply queued behind a six-hour fix would otherwise be out of time before it began. Website Feedback assessments already count from the claim (feedback.md, review-2 F6). `claimed_at` is the latest claim: a first-claim column would need a migration, for a case (a crash late in the turn) where a fresh full time is fair anyway. |
| OT-15 | **The step says why it waits, and why it failed** (`automations/steps/turn-words.ts`). While its turn is queued behind another outsiders' turn, the step's summary (`inputSummary`: the run page, the phone) reads “Asked Dusty · Waiting for another customer report to finish” (“a website feedback report” when that one is an assessment): the step op `turn_queue` (`automations/steps.ts`) sets it and keeps the summary it had in the step's output (`queuedBehind`), which comes back once the turn is claimed or the step settles. A failed turn's line says the reason its turn recorded (`dispatches.failure`), else what its error says: “Dusty couldn’t finish because the AI service had a problem.” (took too long, the usage limit, the AI service, the model wouldn't answer, the sign-in, the workspace, the code project, interrupted, other reports kept it waiting too long); with no reason only “couldn’t finish”. | One short plain sentence (concise-replies.md), through the step's existing summary and error, no new UI. |
| OT-16 | **A busy profile queues an outsiders' turn again** (`runtime/service.ts` `finalizeIn`): an outsiders' turn that ends with the runner's busy-profile error (`HERMES_PROFILE_BUSY` in `@whombat/agent-runtime/failures`; older runners send the same words) is queued again without counting the attempt, after the retry backoff, up to a day after it was queued; later it fails like any turn. A person's turn is retried and fails as before. | With OT-13 only something outside the claim can still hold the profile for long (a Hermes-home step, a sign-in); that must not fail a customer's run. The day bounds a profile that never comes free; a run's deadline and the Feedback queue deadline are shorter anyway. Not a general retry rule. |
| OT-17 | **A step's turn doesn't hold the profile while T3 Code works and another outsiders' turn waits** (`automations/turn-waits.ts`, `automations/engine/handed-wait.ts`, migration 0065 `dispatches.waits_on`). An automation step's outsiders' turn waits for a T3 Code thread (`t3_thread_wait` through `connection_call`) 25 seconds a call at most, inside a call's own 30 s (`CONNECTION_CALL_DEADLINE_MS`; a longer wait fails there and drops the pooled session), as T3 Code's guide asks. When it asks to wait while another outsiders' turn is queued and due, and a one-second look says the thread is still at work, the call doesn't go to T3 Code: the turn records the thread (`automation.turn_waits`, the step's agent, only its own turn at work, once) and hears T3 Code's own end-your-turn signal (`next: end_turn`) with why; nothing more runs in that turn — every further tool call it makes is refused with a line to end it (`mcp/server.ts`), so it can't act alongside the turn that goes on — and one that hasn't ended two minutes later is stopped (`handed_over`); ending by itself, failing as it winds down (not queued again) or stopped so (its usage still kept), the step waits for its thread all the same (`handedTurnEnded`). Its end doesn't settle the step: the engine asks T3 Code about the thread every 20 seconds (a one-second wait, with the turn's `untilDelegatedWorkSettled`) and, once the thread finished or asks something, queues the step's next turn (step op `turn_resume`): the same agent and person, in reply to the same request, so it gets the step's instructions with one line naming the thread to read and go on from. The step's result is what that newest turn says (`output.handedWait.afterOrd`); its time still counts from its first turn's claim (OT-14), what the handed turns used counts in its tokens and the run's and day's limits (`output.handedWait.tokens`), and it may hand over 20 times an attempt. Anything uncertain (no step, T3 Code not answering) waits as before. | Both real tracker runs on 2026-11-09 kept a customer waiting behind another report's fix: #24's first reply came after 62 minutes; in the second, #23's “you can close this” and the notice of John's decision on #26 waited 2 h 18 min, until #25's fix (three reviews) ended. A fix turn mostly waits; the profile can't be shared (OT-13). Handing over only while someone waits keeps the usual run as it was; the next turn is fresh (an automation step resumes no session, OA-15), so it is told which thread to read. The short calls bound how long a newly queued customer waits for the turn at work to notice; a model asked to end may not, so the server ends its part.
