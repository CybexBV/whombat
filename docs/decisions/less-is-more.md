# Less is more, the server's part: feedback in a chat, a short skill catalog, an honest out-of-memory alert (2026-10-18)

whombat-app 0.83.0 (`docs/decisions/less-is-more.md` AP-1749…AP-1826) removed the retired crew from every screen and
made the pages calmer. Five points needed the server, the packs or the hosts service. John, 2026-10-18, after the
lead's recommendations: “Proceed with all your recommendations”.

What the lead found on John's DEV box first (read-only): his one active feedback integration posts reports into the
channel `#reports`, which the one-assistant shell doesn't show; only Assistant productivity is attached (to Dusty), no
whole-burrow attachment; the crew is retired, so Vault key counts are exact; The Titan's “Out of memory” warning at
10:39 was a test's python3 killed inside its own memory cgroup (`CONSTRAINT_MEMCG`) while the machine had 98 GB free.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| LM-01 | **Website feedback lands in a chat with the person's assistant**, not a channel, in the assistant shell: one chat per integration (“Website feedback · <site>”, the person who set it up and their assistant), where the reports, the assistant's assessment and the decision buttons appear. An existing integration that points at a channel moves to its chat on its own (boot step through the pipeline, idempotent; the channel and its history stay). The crew shell keeps channels. The form loses its channel step and the “Add Dusty to #channel” offer there. | Channels aren't shown in the one-assistant shell, so reports landed where nobody looks. |
| LM-02 | **The shipped skill catalog is short and speaks of the assistant.** Ten packs are for the assistant (`roles` gains `assistant`): Assistant productivity, Documents, Homelab, Operator craft, Developer craft, Code review, Security craft, UI review, Game servers, Researcher craft; their descriptions say what they teach the assistant (“Teaches Dusty to…” in the app's words: the manifest says “Teaches your assistant to…”), no crew roles. The other eleven stay shipped and installable under “More packs” (collapsed), descriptions also without crew roles. Pack edits bump the pack's patch version; packs already installed somewhere get the queued upgrade (AE-13's pattern) only where their skills changed. | 16 of 21 descriptions named retired roles; an office crew's packs (sales, legal, marketing…) don't fit one assistant that looks after servers and a home. Nothing is removed. |
| LM-03 | **No whole-burrow pack attachments in the assistant shell.** A one-time boot step turns each whole-burrow attachment into one attachment per person's assistant (same version, same skills off), through the pipeline, idempotent; the server refuses a new whole-burrow attachment there with words the app shows. | A whole-burrow attachment never reaches a personal assistant (SL-D14), so it silently did nothing. |
| LM-04 | **The hosts service's out-of-memory alert is about the machine.** A kill inside a container's, VM's or sandbox's own memory limit (cgroup-constrained) is not “Out of memory” on the server: it shows on that guest when the cgroup is a guest, else as a quiet note; only a machine-wide OOM (no cgroup limit hit) warns on the server. Agents that don't report the difference keep today's behaviour. | The Titan was flagged while 98 GB were free; an alert that cries wolf teaches people to ignore it. |
| LM-05 | **Vault key counts stay as they are.** With the crew retired the counts are exact; listing holders waits until the Vault changes for another reason. | Nothing to fix on a retired box; less work, same truth. |
| LM-06 | **Charts show fewer time labels when narrow** (whombat-app `components/charts`), so they never overlap at phone width. | Seen at 390 px in 0.83.0's check. |

## Feedback in a chat (stream F)

LM-01 built (contracts 0.87.0, CT-383, CT-384; whombat-app `docs/decisions/feedback-chat.md` AP-1827…). On John's box
the one active integration (“Whombat Website”, site `fbs_q4DiqtWpk2Ce0BFY28gGaVRs`, `#reports`, assessed and developed
by Dusty, saved by John) moves at the first start of the release to John's chat with Dusty, “Website feedback · Whombat
Website” (read on the box, read-only); `tracker-reports` (`moved_to` set) stays as it is.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| LM-F01 | **The integration names its chat; no new chat subject, no migration.** `feedback_integrations.channel_id` (and `settings.channelId`) hold the conversation reports go to: a channel, or the integration's chat. That chat is an ordinary task chat whose task key is the integration's (`fb-<id>`, `feedbackChatKey`): one per integration, person and assistant, found again on every save and made when missing (`feedback/chat.ts`). Contracts add `FeedbackIntegration.chat {id, personId, agentId}` and make `settings.channelId` optional; `FeedbackIntegration.channelId` stays set (CT-383). | A `feedback` `ChatSubject` kind would make an older client's strict enum refuse the whole chat list. The unique task-chat index already makes find-or-create safe, so no column is needed. |
| LM-F02 | **The chat is with the configuration's authority** (whoever last saved the settings, FB-F06) **and that person's assistant.** The boot move takes the authority, else the creator, else the first owner in order who is still an owner with an active assistant; that person becomes the authority. | The assessment runs on that person's authority and only their own assistant may assess (AN-06). Only the two of them are in the chat, so nobody else reads the reports. |
| LM-F03 | **Another owner's save moves new reports to their own chat**, with their assistant; the earlier reports stay in the first chat. The `feedback.integration_updated` event carries `movedFrom`. | Their save makes them the authority, and their assistant can't be a member of someone else's chat. |
| LM-F04 | **In a chat only its assistant assesses and develops.** Another agent as assessor or developer is refused on its field (“Reports come to your chat with Dusty, so only Dusty can assess them.”); a person without an active assistant can't save one in the assistant shell (“Your assistant isn’t ready yet. Try again in a moment.”). The channel sent with the settings is ignored there. | The assistant is the only agent in the chat; refusing up front beats a report that is never assessed. |
| LM-F05 | **The assistant opens the chat with one plain line**: “Reports from “Whombat Website” will show up here from now on, instead of #reports. I’ll look at each one and tell you what I find.” (the integration by its name; “from now on, instead of #…” only when it moved from a channel) (the last sentence only when it assesses). No push when the person just saved the settings; a push when the boot step moved it. The chat is titled already (“Website feedback · <name>”, `chatRetitledAt` set), so the assistant doesn't retitle it; the person may rename or archive it. | An empty chat says nothing; the line tells the person where reports went, in the assistant's own words (concise-replies.md). |
| LM-F06 | **A new report brings an archived feedback chat back** to the person's list (a signed `chat.unarchived`, `byFeedback`), then bumps it and marks it unread as any message does. | Otherwise reports would land where nobody looks again. |
| LM-F07 | **The assessment starts in a chat with “Dusty is having a look.”** (no `@` mention, no reference; the dispatch is anchored to it as before). A channel keeps “@Banjo Please assess FB-… (read-only).” The development handoff keeps its approved fixed text (FB-24). | Instructions go to the agent through its turn, not into the chat. The handoff's words are what the owner approved on the card, word for word, so they stay. |
| LM-F08 | **Health keeps its codes, with chat words** for `no_agent`, `agent_missing`, `agent_not_member`, `channel_missing` (“The chat reports go to is closed …”) and `owner_not_member`; audit events name the chat by its title. A chat whose assistant retired or was removed is closed: reports are refused as unavailable until an owner saves the settings again. | Clients show the server's texts; “channel” would be wrong there. |
| LM-F09 | **The boot step `moveFeedbackToChats`** runs in the assistant shell only, after the crew retired and the assistants got their names (AN-06, AN-07), one commit per integration signed by the chat's person: the chat made, `channel_id` and the settings pointing at it, an assessor or developer that isn't the assistant becoming it (none stays none), a new revision and config hash, a signed `feedback.integration_moved` (Keys: “Whombat moved feedback from Website Feedback (“Whombat Website”) to John’s chat with Dusty: new reports go there, and #reports keeps the earlier ones.”), and LM-F05's line. Idempotent: an integration already in its chat is skipped. Integrations moved to automations (TA-20), seeded burrows and the crew shell are left alone; one without anyone to take it waits for a later start. | The AN-S boot steps' pattern: audited, restart-safe, nothing to do by hand. |
| LM-F10 | **The crew shell keeps channels**: there settings without a channel are refused (“Choose the channel reports go to.”). The runtime advertises `feedback.chat` in both shells; apps drop the channel step only in the assistant shell. | One rule per shell; the feature says what the runtime can do, the shell what the app draws. |
| LM-F11 | **The chat's title follows the integration's name** while it is still the one Whombat gave it (“Website feedback · <name>”); once the person renames the chat, their title stays. A save that moves reports from a channel to the chat makes the assistant's first line say where they went before. | The title is the integration's until the person makes it theirs. |
