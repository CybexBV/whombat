# Getting one thing back from a backup (BKO-40…BKO-49)

John, 2026-11-04 (backup and restore v2, section 6): get back one thing — a chat, a file, an automation — from a backup's
page, or by asking the assistant (“bring back yesterday’s chat about the roof”). It only adds and never overwrites. It
reuses the backup file's restorer ([backup-file.md](backup-file.md)) on a backup the backup service opens for reading
(the appliance's peek: `POST /v1/peeks`, a `whombat_peek_<12 hex>` database the runtime may only read and a
read-only folder). Part of [backup-options.md](backup-options.md); the apps' side is app
[backup-look.md](../app/decisions/backup-look.md) (AP-6940…).

Ops (session minor 7, the same release as the rest of backups v2): `backup.look {checkpointId}`, `backup.lookContents
{lookId, category?, query?, cursor?}`, `backup.lookBring {lookId, items? | categories?}`, `backup.lookClose {lookId}`
(`@whombat/protocol` `backup-look.ts`). The assistant's tool is `backup_bring_back` (`@whombat/core` `mcp-backup.ts`).

## Decisions

| ID | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BKO-40 | **Bringing back is the backup file's restore into the Whombat the backup came from, keeping what is here.** The picked things become the backup file's records and go through its restorer with `onDifferent: keep`; the source is this burrow, so messages and files that are still here are themselves, and the restore map makes a second bring-back add nothing (“already here”). Every write goes through the restore's batches in the commit pipeline. | The restorer already knows how to add without overwriting and how to recognise what came back before; a second path would drift from it. |
| BKO-41 | **One look at a time, in memory.** The backup service opens one peek at a time. Opening the same backup again answers the same look; another closes the open one first (a bring-back still running refuses it as busy). A look closes when the person closes it, after two hours unused (checked every minute, never while it brings something back), when the box stops, and at the next start when a stop left one open (its peek's id in `$WHOMBAT_HOME/backup-look.json`). No table: a look lives as long as the process. | The service closes peeks two hours after they are ready and at boot anyway; nothing about a look needs to survive a restart. |
| BKO-42 | **The backup is read, never written, and never through a symlink.** Its database through a pool of its own (two connections, read-only from the start, the same server and role as Whombat's own with only the database name changed, which must be `whombat_peek_<12 hex>`), one repeatable-read, read-only snapshot per bring-back. Its folders: every step from the root a real directory (`lstat`), every file opened with `O_NOFOLLOW`; attachments from `home/files/sha256/…`, canvases from their stored state, Hermes homes packed from `hermes/` the way the runner packs a running one (the file rule is shared: agent-runtime `hermesArchiveIncludes`). | The peek keeps the backup's symlinks as they were, and they may point anywhere on the box. |
| BKO-43 | **The collectors take their source, not a fork.** Beside the scope's database they read bytes through a `CollectSource` (blobs, a canvas's state, a Hermes home's archive, the skills' count): the running Whombat's (`liveSource`) when a backup file is made, the backup's (`peekSource`) when something is brought back. What was picked is a filter on the records (`PickFilter`); the messages collector reads only the picked chats' conversations. | One collector per category keeps the backup file and the look in step. |
| BKO-44 | **What a look lists, by the backup file's categories:** chats (each thread of the person's Chat and Feedback rooms on its own, every other chat whole), files in Files, automations, connections (the person's connections except built-in ones, their Google and Microsoft accounts, GitHub), their Vault entries, memories (the assistant's notes; what it remembers about the person, and from Feedback, each filled in), and the skills (filled in). The agent's and Whombat's settings, the servers and the repositories come back only with the whole Whombat: they are settings or whole trees, not things a person loses one of. Each thing has a plain title (a thread's first line), when it last changed, a few words of where it was, and whether it is here already (its own id, or what a restore made of it; automations by name; memories and skills can't tell). Search: every word in the title, or for chats in their messages. Pages of 50. | People look for “the chat about the roof”, not a record type. |
| BKO-45 | **What comes with a picked thing:** a thread's replies, its choice of what answers and its room; a chat's canvases and their comments; a message's files (whether or not Files was picked); a Google or Microsoft account's sign-in app; an automation's approved destinations. Nothing else. | Each is part of the thing; anything more would bring back what wasn't asked for. |
| BKO-46 | **Each picked thing is told back as back, here already, or couldn't come back,** from its records' outcomes (the restore keeps each record's outcome when asked: `RestoreJob.onOutcome`, the batch's `outcomes`). The line (“3 things are back. 1 was already here.”) is `backupLookDoneLine` in `@whombat/core`, shared by the box's `say` and the apps. `backup.lookBring` waits up to 20 seconds (within a device's 30-second wait for an answer); after that the look's `bring` follows it. | The person asked about things, not records. |
| BKO-47 | **A look's restore is a `backup_restores` row that names the look** in `upload_id` (`bkl_…`): the one-running index keeps it from overlapping a backup file's restore, it signs `backup.restored` as a file's restore does, and it is never listed with the backup files' restores. | One restore at a time per burrow, without a migration. |
| BKO-48 | **Refusals in plain words, as the look's problem:** not enough room with the sizes (`no_space`: needed and free), busy (another backup open, or a backup or check running), not supported yet (a backup service from before peeks: its router's 404 or 405, or 503 when peeks aren't set up), not there any more, couldn't open. `backup.look` answers a failed look rather than an error, so the screens and the assistant say the sentence; the backup service not answering is the usual plain refusal. | Everything the service can refuse has a sentence people understand. |
| BKO-49 | **The assistant brings things back on the person's yes.** `backup_bring_back` (personal, no key): `find` opens the newest backup from before the person's time hint and lists up to eight matches (titles, when, where, whether here), telling the agent what to say; `bring` puts them on the settings card (`backup.lookBring` is `ask` in the settings map: it always asks), which names them by their titles in the backup (`change_setting.lines`: Whombat's words, never ids), and the yes runs it as the person and wakes the assistant with how it went. Looking and listing are reads, closing is a change; nothing goes on the outsiders' list. | Losing a chat is a moment to ask the assistant; adding things back is still the person's to say yes to. |
