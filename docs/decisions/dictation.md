# Voice typing

The person speaks instead of typing in any composer, the box turns the recording into text with speech models of its
own, and the text goes into the draft. John asked for it on 2026-10-26 after trying T3 Code's iPhone dictation: box-side
transcription for English and Dutch, on the processor and on a graphics chip when the box has one, no phone or browser
speech recognition, the models downloaded after the box is installed (not in the image), and settings for it in
Settings. Contracts 3.1 (`@whombat/core/domain` `voice.ts`, `@whombat/protocol/session` `voice.ts`,
`@whombat/control-contracts/local` `voice.ts` and `voice-models.ts`); the box's side is the appliance's
(`docs/decisions/nixos.md`, the voice service and the model fetcher).

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| DT-01 | **A mic in every composer**: Chat and a thread's reply box, the Ask box and the phone's composers. Pressing it records; *Done* sends the recording to the box and puts the text into the draft at the cursor; *Cancel* drops it. The text is never sent by itself. The browser's own speech recognition (the Ask box's mic until now, which sent the audio to Google in Chrome) is gone, and the phones' built-in recognition isn't used (John). | One way that works the same in every browser and on every phone, and what is said stays on the box. |
| DT-02 | **The box hears with whisper.cpp 1.9.5 and two models**: Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 (NVIDIA, CC-BY-4.0; `q8_0`, 669 MB) and Whisper large-v3-turbo (OpenAI, MIT; `q5_0`, 574 MB). Measured on The Titan pinned to 4 cores, 25 Dutch (FLEURS) and 25 English (LibriSpeech) clips of about 9 s, fair-normalised: Parakeet 6.5 % / 2.4 % wrong words, 0.6 s a clip, 0.8 GB; Whisper turbo 3.8 % / 2.8 %, 8.5–8.9 s a clip on 4 cores. Canary 1B v2 (5.2 % / 2.0 %, 2.4 s, sherpa-onnx, community export), Qwen3-ASR 0.6B (17.6 % Dutch), Whisper small and medium (15.9 % and 10.7 % Dutch) and sherpa-onnx's int8 Parakeet (10.7 % Dutch) were measured and not chosen. | Parakeet is the only one fast on any processor, and hears English and Dutch without being told which; Whisper is the most accurate in Dutch and, on a graphics chip, quick (about 0.3 s for 10 s of speech reported on a Radeon 8050S through Vulkan). One engine runs both, on the processor or the chip. |
| DT-03 | ***Speech model***: *Automatic* (the default), *Fast* (Parakeet) or *Most accurate* (Whisper). Automatic is Whisper when it is downloaded and the box has a graphics chip it can use, else Parakeet, else Whisper on the processor. | The person needn't know about chips; whoever wants Whisper on a box without one may have it, warned it is slow. |
| DT-04 | ***Language***: *Automatic* (the default), *English* or *Nederlands*: Whisper's `--language`. Parakeet always hears the language itself. | English and Dutch for now (John). Automatic is right for someone who speaks both. |
| DT-05 | **The models are downloaded after the box is installed, never in the image**, by the box's model fetcher (`whombat-voice-models`; the speech service has no network). The runtime asks for what the settings need when it starts and when they change: Parakeet while voice typing or the desk's Talk is on; Whisper when chosen, or in Automatic on a box with a usable graphics chip. Nothing is removed by itself. The sources are pinned in the release (Hugging Face revision, size, sha256). | John: "download the models after the appliance is installed (not during)". The image stays 1.2 GB smaller, and a box without a chip never fetches Whisper. |
| DT-06 | **Settings → This Whombat → Overview, VOICE TYPING** (and the phone's This Whombat): *Voice typing* on/off (on by default), *Speech model*, *Language*, the two models with their size and state (*Ready*, *Downloading · 40%*, *Waiting to download*, *Couldn’t download* with *Try again*, *Download*, *Remove*) and a line about the graphics chip. *Remove* only for a model the settings don't need (`inUse`). A Whombat is one person's, so the settings are the box's. Hidden where the box has no speech service (`available: false`). | Everything voice typing does is visible and reversible in one place, beside the desk's Talk, which uses the same models. |
| DT-07 | **A recording is a 16 kHz mono 16-bit WAV of at most five minutes**, uploaded through the session's upload stream with the target `dictation` (no reservation): kept in memory only, never resumed, never stored or logged, and answered in `stream_done.output` (`DictationOutput {text, seconds, model}`). The service cuts it at pauses into pieces of at most 30 s and joins their text. | A CONTROL message is 64 KiB; the upload stream already pages and checks bytes. WAV keeps decoding off the box: no codecs to parse what a client sends. |
| DT-08 | **The composer's controller is a port of T3 Code's voice input** (MIT, `packages/client-runtime/src/voice-input/controller.ts`): the draft's text, selection and revision are captured when recording starts; a transcript that comes back to a changed draft is dropped; the text goes in at the cursor with a space where the words need one; one recording at a time. | It is proven and handles the cases that lose text (a late answer, an edited draft, a second composer). |
| DT-09 | **Whisper is told the names the person may say** (the agent's name, the servers', the things'), as the desk's Talk does (desk-display-talk.md DK-T03). Parakeet takes no prompt. | Whisper writes "pve-02" for pve-02 only when told. |
| DT-10 | **The desk's Talk uses the same settings and models** (`VoiceSttRequest.model`, `language`), still 30 s. | One way speech is heard on the box, and Dutch on the desk too. |
| DT-11 | **`voice.changed` (topic `me`)** on every change of the settings or a model's state, at most every 2 s while one downloads. | Settings and the composers follow a download without polling. |
| DT-12 | **No speech service, no mic**: a development box without `WHOMBAT_VOICE=fake`, an older box (`unknown_op`), or `available: false`. `WHOMBAT_VOICE=fake` brings a fake speech service and a fake model fetcher (downloads that take a few seconds), for development and tests. | The composers never offer what can't work; development shows the whole flow without models. |
| DT-13 | **Voice typing is `available` when the speech service answers and the box has its model fetcher** (`WHOMBAT_VOICE_MODELS_SOCKET`; or the health names its models). A service from before 3.1 has no `transcribe`, so the composers show no mic there; a new box before its first model is downloaded is available, not ready, and Settings shows the download. | A service that can't take a five-minute recording would fail every one; a box that is downloading its first model must show it. |
| DT-14 | **Nothing is downloaded while neither voice typing nor the desk's Talk is on**, Whisper in Automatic included. *In use* (no *Remove*) is what the settings want downloaded, plus the model recordings go to now while voice typing or the Talk is on. The server asks for the wanted models at its start (again every 15 s, doubling to 5 minutes, until the fetcher and the speech service answered) and after every change of voice typing's settings or the desk's (`desk.changed`). | Turning both off must free the person to remove both models; a model that would come back by itself after *Remove* reads as a bug. |
| DT-15 | **At most four recordings are held in memory at once on the box** (up to 9.6 MB each); one more is refused at `open` as `busy`. A silent recording (no sample louder than about −40 dBFS, the desk's rule) is answered as nothing heard without asking the speech service, and the service's notes about the sound (“[BLANK_AUDIO]”, “(music)”) are dropped from the text. | Bounded memory whatever the clients send; Whisper makes words up for silence. |
| DT-16 | **The server waits for a transcription at least 90 s and three times the recording's length**, and nothing else times the upload stream out: the session stream has no idle timer, the client's stall check is answered by the box's `pong`, and the relay's and the direct connection's pings keep the connection alive. A stream that goes (the person cancelled, the session closed) stops the wait. | Whisper on a processor takes about as long as the person spoke, behind up to two other jobs; a timer shorter than that would drop a five-minute recording. |
| DT-30 | **One controller for the web and the phone** (`@whombat/client/voice`, the port of DT-08): a space goes before or after the words only at a plain cursor and only next to a word (as T3 Code does, for any letters, so Dutch's too); never inside a word or over a selection. A transcript for a changed draft (another text, another edit count, another composer) is dropped with “The message changed while you spoke, so the words weren’t added.” | Both apps keep the same rules and words, and the tests run once. |
| DT-31 | **While voice typing records or works, the composer's row is the recording bar** (Cancel · waveform · “Recording 0:12” · Done, then “Turning speech into text…”): the field waits hidden with its text and cursor and can't be typed in, Send waits, Escape cancels on the web, and screen readers hear the phase once. A failure shows in the composer's notice line for 5 seconds. The mic is the Ask box's ghost round (one-whombat mock 1) at each composer's size, Done is filled as Send. | Typing during a recording would only make its words stale (DT-30); the bar in the same place keeps the composer's height. |
| DT-32 | **No mic where it can't record**, beside DT-12: a browser page without `getUserMedia` (the box's page over plain HTTP), a phone build without `modules/whombat-voice`. | DT-12's rule: the composers never offer what can't work. |
| DT-33 | **The browser records at the microphone's rate** in an AudioWorklet (a ScriptProcessor where there is none) and brings it to 16 kHz itself, averaging each new sample's span; the hosted app allows the microphone on its own pages (`Permissions-Policy: microphone=(self)`, the camera stays off). | An AudioContext at 16 kHz refuses a microphone's stream in some browsers; the averaging keeps speech and drops what would fold back. |
| DT-34 | **The phone records with a module of its own** (`modules/whombat-voice`: Android `AudioRecord` at 16 kHz from the speech recogniser's source, iOS `AVAudioEngine` and `AVAudioConverter`), writing the WAV straight to the cache; the file is read and deleted at Done. The microphone is asked for on the first press of a mic (the image picker and camera plugins no longer remove it); going to the background drops a recording. | No recording library in the APK, and a recording never stays on the phone. |
| DT-35 | **VOICE TYPING's rows**: a model shows “669 MB · Ready” (or its download's state and bar) with the reason under it when it waits or failed; *Try again* is filled, *Download* and *Remove* are not; *Remove* doesn't ask first. Automatic says what it uses now; a chosen model says it only when another is used. | *Download* brings a removed model back; what Automatic does is the one thing the person can't see otherwise. |
| DT-36 | **Fixture worlds have voice typing only when asked** (`VITE_FIXTURE_VOICE`, `whombat:fixture-voice` for the visual suite, the phone's demo `?voice=`): the fake speech service (`@whombat/client/voice/fake`) answers on the fake box's session, so `voiceApi` and the `dictation` upload run as against a box. | The mocked screens stay as drawn, and fixture mode tests the real path. |
| DT-37 | **Talk's mic on the desk screen is voice typing** (contracts 3.3, the desk socket's `hear`): tap the mic, speak, *Done*, and the box turns the recording into text (`DeskHeard`) that goes into Talk's box at its end, where the person reads it, may edit it on the keyboard cover and sends it with *Send* or Enter; *Cancel* drops it. Nothing is posted until *Send*. Up to five minutes, *Done* by itself at the cap. Hold to talk and the space bar are gone (DK-06, DK-07 in part). | John, 2026-10-27: the apps had voice typing and the screen still had the old way; one way everywhere, and the words are seen before they go, as in every composer. |
| DT-38 | **The screen's recording bar is the composers'** (DT-31) at the desk's size: *Cancel* · the live waveform · “Recording 0:12” · *Done*, then “Turning speech into text…”; a failure shows on the mic's line for five seconds in the composers' words. The words live in `DESK_WORDS` written out (`tapToTalk`, `wordsFirst`, `dictation`), checked equal to `VOICE_WORDS` by a test, because the console copies the contracts' words and can't follow a reference. | The same words and order wherever someone dictates; the console's copy check needs literals. |
| DT-39 | **Talk's switch alone allows the screen's mic**: `hear` needs a paired, unlocked screen with Talk's voice on and a speech service that hears and has a microphone, one recording at a time (`busy`); it runs `dictate` with `desk: true`, so voice typing's own switch doesn't matter there while its model, language, Whisper's names, the silence rule and the dropped notes do. Refusals: `voice_unavailable` (Talk off, no model yet), `busy`, `bad_audio`, `too_large` (past five minutes), `unavailable` (the service failed). Words that come back to a screen that locked meanwhile are refused (`locked`). | Turning voice typing off for the composers shouldn't silence the desk the person set up to talk to; DT-10 already made both hear the same way. A locked screen shows no words (DK-03). |
| DT-40 | **A question from the screen is at most 1000 code points** (`DESK_TALK_TEXT_MAX`, RE2's largest count, about a minute of speech; 500 before), typed or heard; what `hear` heard past it is cut after the last whole word, with no ellipsis, since the person sees it before sending. `DeskTalk.question` still shows 500. | A dictated question is longer than a typed one; the desk's text patterns carry their length in the pattern, and RE2 counts to 1000. |
| DT-41 | **Words from the mic go as a typed question with `spoken: true`**: the turn still hears it came by voice (`desk:<talk>:v`, DK-T05), even when the person edited them. The answer is spoken as before (DK-T07). A talk's `audioWav` stays for an older screen. | The answer should still read well aloud; editing a word doesn't make the question typed. |
