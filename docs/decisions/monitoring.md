# Monitoring servers, networks and services (MON-01…MON-99)

John, 2026-11-24: “I would like to use Whombat for server, network and service monitoring also. What is missing?” A
council of four (GPT 6.2 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5, Muse Wonder Full 1.0, Gemini 4 Neon) reviewed the ideas and added their
own; John chose to build everything they agreed on except checks from outside the house. This file is the one design
all parts follow. Rows MON-01…MON-19 are the design; each part records its own detail decisions in its range:
uptime checks MON-20…MON-39, incidents and history MON-40…MON-59, quiet and causes MON-60…MON-79, the hosts service
MON-80…MON-89, automations and watches MON-90…MON-99.

Everything here keeps “What Whombat watches starts calm” (`watch-defaults.md`) and plain words (`plain-words.md`,
`concise-replies.md`).

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| MON-01 | **Your own checks are connections.** A catalog entry “Website or address” (`uptime`, no sign-in) watches one website (HTTP/HTTPS, an optional word the page must contain), a port (TCP), a ping, a name lookup (DNS) or a check-in (a script or job that must call Whombat in time). The person picks how often (1, 5 or 15 minutes) and where it runs from: this Whombat, or a server Whombat watches. The check itself runs in the hosts service (its `checks`, made at Connect, changed when the connection is edited, removed at Disconnect); the connection's health pass reads the hub check's latest result instead of asking the address itself. | The hosts service has run these checks for a long time with nobody able to reach them. As a connection, a check gets everything a connection has at once: a page and tile, Worth a look, the assistant's research, the Inbox, Ignore and Stop watching, history, automations and the assistant's connect-by-talking. |
| MON-02 | **An uptime check's lines.** `uptime.down` (it doesn't answer, or answers wrong; bad, offline, a problem), `uptime.checkin_late` (a check-in didn't come; bad, only you), `uptime.cert_expiring` (the certificate ends within 14 days: amber; within 3: bad) and `uptime.unseen` (Whombat can't run the check now, because the server it runs from isn't reporting; a heads-up). The first three start on: the person asked for exactly this. The hub's own `check.*` alerts of a check that belongs to a connection never reach the Inbox; the connection's lines do. | One source of truth per check: the connection. A person-made check is the clearest case of “very important and the person wants to hear”. |
| MON-03 | **A check-in's address** is shown on its page with a line ready to paste (`curl` with the hosts service's pinned key) and the words “Works from your home network.” Calls from the internet aren't offered. | The agents' listener uses Whombat's own certificate and isn't on the internet; checks from outside the house were left out on purpose. |
| MON-04 | **Keep quiet for a while, on anything.** Any server, connection or device can be kept quiet until a time (“for an hour”, “until tonight”, “until tomorrow morning”, a time the person picks), with an optional note, from its page's ⋯ and by the assistant on the person's word. While it holds, the thing's lines don't colour it and make no look, research, Inbox item, incident or push; its page says “Quiet until 23:00” with End now. A server's window is also a maintenance window in the hosts service, so its alerts are muted there too, and things that run on a quiet server are quiet with it (MON-07). When it ends, what is still wrong counts again from the start. | “Keep quiet while I work on the NAS tonight.” Maintenance windows existed only for servers and only in the hosts service. |
| MON-05 | **Whombat's own planned work is quiet by itself.** When Whombat updates or restarts a server or machine (the care plan's updates, an approved restart, a step of an automation), it keeps that server or machine quiet for the work and 15 minutes after, noted “Whombat is updating it”. | A planned restart at 03:00 shouldn't start a research or a push. |
| MON-06 | **One push when something important goes wrong.** Whombat keeps an incident for a thing while its dot is bad or offline from lines that count (watched, not quiet, confirmed); it opens after two passes in a row and ends after two passes without. When no research finding has pushed about it within 10 minutes, Whombat pushes once (“Plex is down”, “The NAS: a disk is failing”). When the incident ends the push is withdrawn quietly; nothing pushes “it's back”. Several incidents opening within the same few minutes are one push. Amber never pushes. Do not disturb and quiet hours hold pushes back as for every notification. | Today the phone only buzzes when a research ends with a step to approve: a NAS that is switched off, an AI that is signed out or a day's researches used up meant silence. |
| MON-07 | **One cause, one message.** A connection or device whose machine (what it runs on) is down — its server offline, its machine stopped — is down because of that: it makes no look, Inbox item or incident of its own, its page says “Not answering while pve-02 is down”, and the cause's item names what it takes with it (“Plex and 4 more are down with it”). The same for the internet: while Whombat can't reach the internet (MON-08), connections on internet addresses that fail are grouped under one “The internet is down” item. Things still failing after their cause is back get their own lines again, after the usual confirmation. | One dead host or router should be one message and one research, not ten, and shouldn't use up the day's researches. |
| MON-08 | **Whombat checks its own way out.** Every minute the box checks its router, its name lookups and the internet (two independent addresses, one of them Whombat's own service), from its own server process. It says plainly which step fails (“The internet is down since 14:02”, “Name lookups don't work”, “The router doesn't answer”); this starts on (Whombat can't reach things any more). While the internet is down no push can leave the house, so its incident is told when the internet is back, as history. | The cause for MON-07, and the first question of every outage: is it the internet, or the service? |
| MON-09 | **Uptime that is true, and a list of outages.** Every health pass also stores whether the thing answered (an `up` point, 1 or 0), so the hourly roll-up keeps an hour's share and short outages don't disappear (the response-time average skipped failed checks). Incidents (MON-06) are kept 90 days with when, how long, what and their cause. Every thing's page, of every kind, shows one line: “Up 99.6 % this week · down 3 times, 41 min” (or “Up all week”); a server's page lists its past alerts from the hosts service's 90 days. | The council's shared finding: the data was mostly there, but the figure was wrong and the sentence missing. No monthly report: history is something the person opens or asks for (`brief-removed.md`). |
| MON-10 | **The assistant reads status and history.** Read tools answer what needs the person now (the Inbox, things not fine, quiet windows, open incidents) and what happened (a thing's or every thing's incidents, uptime and past server alerts over a period); `alerts_list` takes resolved alerts and a period. | “Is everything OK?” and “What broke while I was away?” in one call instead of probing every connection. |
| MON-11 | **Automations start on something going down or coming back**, for a thing or every thing of a kind, optionally only after it has been down for some minutes; once per incident. And a one-time watch: “tell me when pve-02 is back” (or “…if Plex goes down tonight”) posts once in that thread and ends, with a card and Cancel, like Find My's one-time watch. | “When the UPS has been on battery for 10 minutes, shut down the NAS” runs without an AI turn. |
| MON-12 | **Backups per service.** A connection's machine (what it runs on) says when it was last backed up, or that nothing backs it up, as a fact on its page. A line `machine.backup_old` (a job covers the machine but its last backup is much older than the job's rhythm) starts on: a backup isn't happening. “Nothing backs this up” stays a fact, not a line: often it is meant. | The most regretted monitoring answer, from data Whombat already reads. |
| MON-13 | **Say when Whombat can't see something.** A check whose server stopped reporting says “Not checked since 14:02” (MON-02 `uptime.unseen`) and that time counts neither as up nor as down; a connection that answers but whose details Whombat couldn't read for an hour has a heads-up saying so; the health pass picks only connections it looks at before its per-pass limit. | A green dot should mean it was checked. |
| MON-14 | **Machines that stopped by themselves.** A container or machine that is set to start by itself and stopped without anyone stopping it (out of memory, or a crash's exit) is a critical alert of its own that starts on. A stop by Whombat, by the person, or a normal stop never counts; when the hosts service can't tell, no alert. | The most common silent outage. Something stopped on purpose is not a problem (`on-purpose.md`). |
| MON-15 | **Limits per server.** A server's rules that have a limit (disk use, SSD wear, temperatures, inodes) take a limit of the server's own or of one item (a disk, a mount), set from the line's ⋯ “Change the limit” on the server's page and by the assistant on the person's word; the hosts service's defaults stay for the rest. | A backup disk that is always 93 % full shouldn't alarm, and switching the check off would leave it blind. |
| MON-16 | **Left out:** checks from outside the house (John, 2026-11-24), a monthly or weekly report, pushes for every blip or for “it's back”, a separate monitoring dashboard, new-device and slow-answer lines. | Calm by default; the person opens history or asks for it. |

## Your own checks, backups per service and blind spots (MON-20…MON-39)

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| MON-20 | The connection owns the settings. Connect saves them and creates a hub check with the connection as owner. Edit keeps the check id and changes its settings. If the hub cannot take them now, the health pass reconciles them and recreates a missing check. Disconnect removes the hub check first. | A retry can finish a saved change. A failed disconnect leaves a connection the person can still remove. |
| MON-21 | Whombat reads the hub's current result and latency. A missing, stale, paused or pending reading counts as neither up nor down. After a probe changes its target, runner or required word, a reading from before the change describes the old probe and is left out. | A first failure, a server that can’t run the check and an old answer do not describe the new check's status. |
| MON-22 | Probes run every 1, 5 or 15 minutes. A check-in expects the same periods, with 0, 1, 5, 15 or 60 extra minutes. It always reports to this Whombat. The assistant offers the settings on a confirmation card and can read status, and only the person gets the pinned command from the page. | The person agrees to what runs. A script can report from the LAN without giving the assistant its private URL. |
| MON-23 | A known rhythm uses the longest normal gap in the most frequent job Whombat can match to the machine. Its backup is overdue after more than two gaps, or after eight days when the rhythm is unknown. There must be a backup date and a known job covering the machine. | A missed run can be retried without an immediate warning. An unknown schedule gets at least a week before the warning. |
| MON-24 | A connection keeps the time its detail reader first failed while the connection still answered. After an hour the check shows a heads-up, and a successful read clears it. The uptime page always shows when its check cannot run. The optional `uptime.unseen` line starts off. | The status stays honest without adding a problem or a push for a blind spot. |

### Website or address and history fixes

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| MON-25 | The health pass asks for the hub's stored probe results between passes. Any failed result makes that pass down in history. The current result still drives the page and alerts. A hub without the period read uses its current result. This extends MON-21. | A check can fail and recover between two health passes. |
| MON-26 | Check again reports the saved hub check's current result as working, down or not checked. The connection stays active and continues to be watched. | A failed or unseen probe is a result of a working connection to the check. |
| MON-27 | Disconnect saves the removal first and tries to delete the hub check. The health pass sweeps checks with a connection owner that no longer exists. This replaces the removal order in MON-20. | Disconnect works while the hosts service is unreachable. |
| MON-28 | The connections catalog leaves Website or address out below session minor 9. | Those apps cannot draw the form's conditional fields. |
| MON-30 | A live uptime point weighs at most seven and a half minutes. A rolled-up point weighs at most an hour. A last point weighs up to its usual interval. This replaces the weighting in MON-48. | Time without readings contributes neither up nor down. |
| MON-31 | Server uptime starts at the first retained report when it falls inside the period. When there is no report date or the offline alerts could not be read, Whombat shows only known outages. This refines MON-49. | A server first seen today has no week of uptime yet. |
| MON-32 | All-things `watch_history` includes the saved router, name lookup and internet outages. This Whombat has no history page for itself, so its outage history is available by asking. This extends MON-52. | The box's connection to the internet is separate from any watched server's history. |
| MON-33 | A failed backup attempt remains the machine's latest backup fact. For a covered machine, overdue uses the newest usable backup that is still listed on its storage. A known failed attempt with no successful backup on record raises the line. This extends MON-23. | A fresh failure cannot make the machine look recently backed up. |

## Incidents, uptime and history (MON-40…MON-59)

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| MON-40 | **An incident's watcher is a home job of its own** (`home.incidents`, every minute) that reads the looks as the look watcher does (every look, up to 200), rather than a hook inside the look watcher. What it saw on the pass before stays in memory: after a restart nothing opens or ends until the second pass. | The look watcher stops for a burrow without an agent or account; outages shouldn't. The second read costs one read of Home's things a minute and the fleet the hosts service already caches. |
| MON-41 | **An incident starts at the first pass that saw its look**, or earlier when Whombat knows: an offline server's last report, or the start of the assistant's research of a look as bad or worse. It ends at the first of the two passes without it. | History says when the outage began, not when Whombat confirmed it. |
| MON-42 | **One open incident per thing; it keeps its worst.** A problem that goes down becomes a `down` incident with the down words and signs `thing.down` once more (same incident); a cause learned while it lasts is kept. Amber ends nothing and opens nothing. | Automations that start on “down” must see a thing that went from broken to down; one row per outage keeps the history readable. |
| MON-43 | **The push waits 10 minutes from the incident's start, then names together everything that started within 5 minutes of the first due one.** Never for an incident with a cause, one a research finding pushed about since it started, one older than an hour (it was down before Whombat watched, or while it couldn't push), nor past 6 pushes a day or 2 of one thing. | One message for one outage; an old outage found after an update isn't news; a flapping service can't buzz all day. |
| MON-44 | **The push is a “Needs you” notification by the Servers signer** (“Whombat”), keyed `incident:<group>` (its inbox row and its group on the phone). Its words: “Plex is down”, “The NAS: a disk is failing” (the line's reason, its first word lowered unless it is a name), “3 things are down: Plex, the NAS and Home Assistant”, “2 things aren’t working: …” when some are only broken; past three names “… and 2 more”. Its tap opens the thing's look thread when it has one, else the thing's page (`/things/<id>` on the app's address; the person's Chat on a box without one); several open the Inbox. | “Needs you” always buzzes, Do not disturb and quiet hours still hold it. No link type for a thing's page exists, and an app address is what phones already open as a screen. |
| MON-45 | **Withdrawn when the last incident it named ends:** its inbox row is marked read (the person's devices told) and on a managed box its phone notification is cleared (a content-free clear, as for a decided approval). Nothing says it's back. | MON-06: no “it's back” push; an outage that's over shouldn't wait in the Inbox. |
| MON-46 | **Events.** `thing.down` (opened down, or a problem went down), `thing.problem` (opened as a problem), `thing.back` (ended), all `System` with no text, signed by the Servers signer with `ThingIncidentEvent`: `incidentId`, `thing`, `kind`, `name`, `nature`, `words`, `startedAt`, `endedAt` (back only), `cause` when another thing took it down. Automations are nudged after each. | What a trigger and its words need, nothing more; no text keeps the activity log calm. |
| MON-47 | **`up` is 1 when the check answered (also when it refused the credential: the service is up), 0 when it didn't or answered with an error, and nothing when the check couldn't run (`unseen`) or the credential couldn't be read here.** An unseen pass records no response time either. | Uptime is whether the service answers; a sign-in problem is a line of its own. |
| MON-48 | **Uptime weighs each point by the time until the next one, at most an hour; the last one as long as the one before it.** Before a thing's first `up` point, its response time stands in (answered when it has one). | A rolled-up hour then weighs twelve 5-minute points, and a gap (the box was off) never counts as up. |
| MON-49 | **The week's line**: “Up 99.6 % this week · down 3 times, 41 min”, “Up all week”, “Down twice this week, 12 min” when Whombat has no checks of it (a server without the hosts service), “Up since 21 Nov” for a thing watched less than a week; never “100 %” while it missed something, whole percents under 95. It is the page's `uptime` and one of its facts (after the first), so apps without `uptime` show it. Down counts only `down` incidents (a server's offline alerts too, overlaps once); a server's share is the week minus its down time. | One line on every page from data Whombat has; the facts already reach every app. |
| MON-50 | **“Answers” on a page without a kind** says the week's weighted share and “Didn’t answer for 2 h” instead of a count of checks. | A rolled-up hour is many checks; time is what a person reads. |
| MON-51 | **`things.history`** (`GET /burrows/:burrow/things/:thing/history?days=30|90`, read, session minor 9, `THING_HISTORY_MINOR`): the incidents overlapping the period newest first (when, how long in minutes, words, cause), the uptime over it (its points are kept 30 days, so 90 days says “since …”), and for a server every alert the hosts service had in it. | One read for the list the apps will draw. |
| MON-52 | **`watch_status`** answers the Inbox's counted items (the `you` group, open) as one sentence of their titles, the looks not fine (down, bad, look; reason; cause), the open incidents and the quiet windows; **`watch_history`** (one thing by page id or handle, or all; 1–90 days, 7 by default) the incidents, the uptime line of each thing that had one (or of the thing asked about), the servers' alerts in the period and a note when nothing happened. Times read as the person's clock (“Today 07:31”, “Mon 17 Nov 07:31”). Read-only, for the person's turns, never in an outsiders' turn. `quietWindowsFor` answers none until quiet windows are kept. | One call for “Is everything OK?” and “What broke while I was away?”. |
| MON-53 | **`alerts_list` takes `state` (open by default, as before; resolved or all: newest first with when each opened and resolved) and `since`**; `hosts.listAlerts` passes `since`, `until`, `rule` and `q` to the hosts service. | The hosts service keeps 90 days of alerts; the assistant and the apps couldn't reach them. |
| MON-54 | **Held or unseen isn't back.** An open incident whose thing left the looks because it is held (a window, a cause) or not seen (Home's thing `unknown`: its check couldn't run, it isn't checked yet) stays open and counts no pass; it ends, with `thing.back`, only after two passes that see the thing fine. A thing gone from Home altogether still ends as before. | `thing.back` starts automations and tells watchers; a hold started mid-outage or a probe runner that stopped reporting says nothing about recovery. |
| MON-55 | **A push only for what is still bad.** The watcher pushes an incident only while its thing has a look on that pass and isn't held; the push's commit checks each again (still open, not pushed, no cause, not held) and makes its words from the incidents left. | Keeping Plex quiet at 14:09 must stop the 14:10 push, and a batch whose other half ended meanwhile mustn't name it. |
| MON-56 | **The update pushes nothing old.** While a Whombat has no incidents kept, what the watcher's first pass after its start already saw opens as told: `pushed_at` set, no push group, so it counts in no day's pushes and has nothing to withdraw. Chosen over starting it at the line's `notice_states.first_seen`, which also counts time a line was only amber. | On boxes without an AI account, or with the day's researches used up, everything red for days would buzz ten minutes after the update. |
| MON-57 | **Pages hear of outages.** Opening, worsening or ending an incident publishes `home.changed {things}` to the person; the client reads `things.history` again with `things.page` on it. | An open “What happened” kept saying “still going”. |
| MON-58 | **An outage ends on what Whombat saw of the thing itself, not on its dot.** Each pass reads every open incident's thing without a look as `fine`, still `bad` underneath, `unread` or `gone`: a server is fine when its fleet row is online (offline stays bad while its alert is quiet or held back by its offline parent); a connection by its last check's outcome and stored lines, before the quiet rows (not answering, an error, a refused sign-in or a bad line someone ignored or snoozed are all still bad); a device by its last read. A check made before a hold over it ended (a window, or a cause the watcher saw lift) is `unread`, as is a fleet that wasn't read, a check that couldn't run and a thing not checked. Two `fine` passes in a row end it at the first; `bad` starts the count again; `unread` counts neither way, in both directions (bad, unread, bad opens from the first bad pass). A check the person switches off drops out of the stored lines: its problem can end, a thing that doesn't answer can't. The same reading settles a `back` watch: answering, also with an error or a bad line, after any hold. | Ignoring the down line, a hold that lifted while the line counted again from the start, a failed fleet read and a parent's suppression each signed `thing.back` (“Plex is back”, “back” automations) while the thing was still down. |
| MON-59 | **A thing that was removed ends its incident without `thing.back`.** Gone means not there any more: no connection row, no switched-on device, a fleet read without the server. The incident ends at that pass, its push is withdrawn as usual, nothing starts, and every watch still waiting on the thing stops (`cancelled`) with “Plex was removed, so I stopped watching it.” in its thread, without a notification. A thing only missing from Home's list (past its 200) counts as unread. | Disconnecting a broken Plex said it was back and ran “back” automations. |
| MON-59a | **The push's clock starts at the incident's start, or when a hold over it ended when that is later**: the latest end of a window over it (kept a day) or the last pass that saw it held (a cause leaves no row once it lifts). Its 10 minutes, its hour of freshness and its group of 5 minutes all count from then. | A server still down after a 2-hour window was older than an hour and never pushed; after 15–59 minutes it pushed at once, before a research could. |
| MON-59b | **One push takes up to 200 incidents**, the same limit the planner and `incidents.push` use (the pass's own limit); its words name the first three and “and N more”. | The planner could group 51 while the command took 50, so a large outage failed every minute and was never pushed. |
| MON-59c | **The week's line never says 100 % beside an outage**: with any down time it says 99.9 % at most (also `upPct`), even when every reading said it answered (an outage between two checks, or one Whombat knows only from its incident). | “Up 100 % this week · down once, 12 min” contradicted itself. |
## Quiet and causes (MON-60…MON-79)

Keep quiet for a while (MON-04), Whombat's own planned work (MON-05), one cause (MON-07) and the way out (MON-08), as
built.

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| MON-60 | **A window or a cause holds the whole thing inside `applyQuiet`.** The thing's quiet read (`ThingQuiet`) brings its windows and its cause, `notWatched` passes them on, and while one holds every watched line goes to `quiet` with a row of its own kind: a window as snoozed until its end, a cause as ignored with its words (“while pve-02 is down”), both marked `held`. A line's own Ignore keeps its own row. A pass under a hold writes no line state and forgets the old ones. | Every reader already treats quiet lines as quiet (pages, dots, the desk's looks, the Inbox, the incidents built on `deskLooks`), so one place makes them all obey; forgetting the counts is what makes “counts again from the start” true. |
| MON-61 | **One window per thing, the person's, signed.** `things.keepQuiet` (until a time or for 5 minutes to a month, an optional note of 120 characters) changes a window that holds already instead of adding one; `things.endQuiet` ends it. Keeping quiet is a change with Undo (Undo ends it); ending is an everyday change in the thing's Activity. Both are session minor 9 (`KEEP_QUIET_MINOR`), resent by no client (a server's touches the hosts service). | One window to explain and to end. Undo of “Kept the NAS quiet until 23:00” is the natural way back. |
| MON-62 | **A server's window is a maintenance window in the hosts service** (scope server, now until the end, the note as its note), made before the commit and removed if the commit fails, on End now, and by the minute's sweep once a window is over. When the hub refuses, the window still holds in Whombat. A server's page carries `keptQuiet`, hides its banner, and its dot shows nothing while it holds. | The hub's own alerts are muted at the source, and Whombat never waits on the hub to keep its own word. |
| MON-63 | **What the apps get.** A page (`ThingPage.keptQuiet`, `HostServerDetail.keptQuiet`) has the words, the end, the note, who (none: Whombat, `whombat`) and `canEnd`; Home's thing has `quietUntil`; held lines' `NoticeQuiet.held`. Worth a look's first line is a heads-up with the same words, for an app that doesn't draw them yet; an older app reads held lines as snoozed or ignored, and Undo on one of them ends the window. | The next wave draws End now from contracts that already carry it; nothing an app has today breaks. |
| MON-64 | **`quiet_until` acts only when the turn's message is the person's own**, as `watch_checks`; without until, minutes or end it says how the thing is kept quiet. Never in an automation's own step, and the two session ops are no assistant setting. | Keeping something quiet is the person's word. |
| MON-65 | **Whombat's own planned work** is the three tools that clearly restart or update: `guest_power` reboot or restart (the machine, about 5 minutes), `server_power` reboot (the server, about 10 minutes), `server_update` install (the server, until its followed task ends; at most 2 hours), each plus 15 minutes, noted “Whombat is updating it”. The care plan's updates, approved restarts and automation steps all reach these tools. A shutdown or stop isn't planned to come back, so it isn't kept quiet; the person's own buttons on a server's page aren't Whombat's work. A window over one machine (`guest_id`) has no maintenance window (the hub has none per guest) and quiets only what runs on that machine. | Only paths that clearly come back are worth a window; nothing else tells when a restart is over, so its window is a fixed span. |
| MON-66 | **Causes are found by a job every minute and kept** (`thing_causes`, with when it was last found): from the machine each connection's last health pass found it on (kept while it doesn't answer, since a stopped machine isn't found by its address), the fleet's servers and the machines' states. A cause the job hasn't confirmed for 5 minutes counts for nothing. | Readers are pure and many; a stored cause reaches them with the quiet rows they already read. |
| MON-67 | **Which cause:** its server offline, then its machine stopped (both only while the connection doesn't answer), then its server or machine kept quiet (whatever it answers), then the way out failing while it talks to the internet and doesn't answer. A cause is `down` (it doesn't answer because of it) or `quiet` (it is quiet with a server or machine kept quiet). | A wrongly found machine never hides a connection that answers; a quiet server quiets all it carries. |
| MON-68 | **A thing down because of something else is grey, not fine**: its dot `unknown`, its line and badge “Not answering while pve-02 is down”, `causedBy` on its page and Home's thing, the cause's page linked. One only quiet with its server keeps its dot and line and says “Quiet while pve-02 is kept quiet” (`causedBy.kind` `quiet`). It makes no look; `DeskLook.causedBy` is filled for one that still shows. Devices get no cause in this part: Whombat doesn't know what machine a device is on. | Green would be a lie and red a second message; something that answers isn't “not answering”. |
| MON-69 | **The cause names what it takes with it** in its Inbox item (the server's “isn't checking in” alert) and its desk look's reason: “Plex is down with it”, “Plex and Sonarr are down with it”, “Plex and 4 more are down with it”; a machine's things count for its server, things only quiet with it don't. | One message that says the whole story. |
| MON-70 | *Superseded in part by MON-74 (the route table, which step is said, the names looked up).* **The way out's probes**: the router is the default gateway from `/proc/net/route`, answering on 53, 80 or 443 (a refusal answers); the internet is a TCP connect to Whombat's own service (the host of `WHOMBAT_APP_URL`, port 443) or `1.1.1.1:443`, by the address each last resolved to (a box without the service: `1.1.1.1` and `9.9.9.9`; `WHOMBAT_WAY_OUT_TARGETS` overrides); name lookups are the system resolver finding those names. The first failing step in that order is the one said. | No root, no outside service of our own to run, and a failing resolver doesn't make the internet look down. |
| MON-71 | **An outage opens after one step has failed for two minutes**, from when it first failed, and ends at the first pass with every step working; another step failing for two minutes replaces it. Outages are kept in their own table (`way_out_outages`, `wayOutHistory`, “The internet was down 14:02–14:31”) for history and the incidents' part. | One lost answer is nothing; the incidents table belongs to its own part. |
| MON-72 | **The way out's item** is an alert of the person's (`box.way_out`, starts on, severity bad): “The internet is down since 14:02”, “Name lookups don’t work since …”, “The router doesn’t answer since …”, with what it takes (“GitHub, Google and 3 more can’t be reached until it’s back”). A connection is on the internet when it is a fixed public service, a public address or a name that isn't a local one, and doesn't run on a server Whombat watches. | Whombat can't reach things any more: very important, and Whombat can say which step. |
| MON-73 | **`WHOMBAT_WAY_OUT`** is `on` in production and `off` on a development box and in tests unless set. Ended windows are kept 90 days. | Development and tests never probe the internet by themselves. |
| MON-74 | **Which step of the way out is said.** The gateway comes from the process's own route table (`/proc/self/net/route`; the system's as a fallback). The router and the internet are probed at the same time: the internet is down when no target answers, and it is the router only when a gateway is known and doesn't answer either; without a gateway the router isn't judged. Name lookups always include one fixed name (`one.one.one.one`) beside the targets' names, and fail when none resolves while the internet answers. | The server's unit runs with `ProcSubset=pid`, which hides `/proc/net` (checked: with `subset=pid,hidepid=invisible` `/proc/net/route` is missing and `/proc/self/net/route` is there), so the old read found no gateway and said “The router doesn't answer” for ever. A router that drops these ports, an IPv6-only route or a point-to-point link said the same while the internet worked; a box with bare-address targets never checked its lookups. |
| MON-75 | **End now ends every window that holds the whole thing**, the person's and Whombat's for its own work alike, and answers with nothing holding. Undo of keeping it quiet ends only its own window and answers with the window still holding, if any. | Planned work adds a window beside the person's; “Whombat watches it again” must be true, and Undo takes back one change. |
| MON-76 | **A server kept quiet holds its open alerts with the window**, whether or not the hub muted them or made its maintenance window: `NoticeQuiet` snoozed until the window's end with its words and who kept it quiet, `held: 'window'`, no longer asking for attention, so it is out of the Inbox and snoozed on Watch until the end. Its own Ignore or snooze stays its own; the hub's checks aren't the server's. Home's server thing has `quietUntil` (a device's too). Undo on a held server line, from an app without End now, ends the window. | The maintenance window mutes at the source when the hub takes it; Whombat keeps its own word without it, and a muted alert shouldn't read “while it’s in maintenance” with an Undo that can't work. |
| MON-77 | **A fleet that can't be read leaves the kept causes alone**: when the fleet or a server's machines can't be read, no kept cause is removed or replaced, and each stops counting once nobody confirmed it for 5 minutes; things without one still get theirs (the way out, a quiet window). | An unread fleet says nothing about servers, as the incidents treat it; one bad read shouldn't end every cause and make every thing count again from the start. |
| MON-78 | **A “Website or address” check takes part in one cause.** Its host is its own address field (a website's host, a port's host, the name or address of a ping or name lookup; none for a check-in). Its glance keeps the machine it runs on (also while it is down or can't be checked) and says `outcome: offline` while its probe is down, so the causes job still sees it down while a cause quiets its line; its page keeps its own `uptime.down` line and no second “can’t reach it”. | Its base address only names the hosts service, so it was never on the internet, and with the line quieted its glance read fine, so the cause ended the next minute and came back: a flap. |
| MON-79 | **Small rules of windows.** Every window write outside a command (a work's end, the sweep, a maintenance window given back) goes through the commit pipeline per Whombat; Home hears when a work's end moves, when a maintenance window goes back, and once when a window runs out by itself (the first sweep after its end). Keeping quiet takes a time at least 5 minutes ahead, as for minutes, and refuses a server the hosts service doesn't list (a fleet that can't be read doesn't stop it). A line held by a cause reads “On hold while media is stopped”; “Quiet until …” and “Kept quiet …” are for windows. | An open page dropped a stale “Quiet until …” only on its own refresh; nobody kept a thing quiet that is only down because of something else. |

## The hosts service (MON-80…MON-89)

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| MON-80 | `guest.stopped` starts on. It is critical per Unraid Docker container with a dated OOM exit, or libvirt VM with an explicit crash event. A fresh report must show the guest set to start automatically and stopped. The hub checks its guest and host power actions before opening it. It resolves when the guest runs, is removed or no longer starts automatically. | Unraid reports Docker autostart and the OOM flag. Libvirt reports autostart and sends a separate crash event. Other reports lack either autostart or a clear cause. A nonzero exit alone can come from a deliberate stop. |
| MON-81 | Check reads keep `last.at` and report `stale: true` when the agent's reports or check results go missing. Stale probes have pending status and leave their open alerts alone. | Losing the runner says nothing about the address it was checking. |
| MON-82 | `GET /checks/{id}` reads the result without querying the chart series. A check can have a fixed `owner` set at creation. A `whombat:connection:` owner leaves alerting to the connection. | Whombat can match and read one check for each connection and has one alert source. |
| MON-83 | `disk.space_high`, `fs.inodes_high` and both SSD wear rules take 1 to 100 %. Both temperature rules take 1 to 150 °C. An item overrides its server, which overrides the rule's default. Each limit can be cleared on its own. | The check can keep watching a disk or sensor at the point that matters there. |
| MON-84 | `things.setWatchLimit` and `watch_checks` change a limit as the person. An Undo compares the current limit before restoring the old value. The session sends the new check and limit fields and accepts the new operations from minor 9. | People can see who changed the limit and take it back without overwriting a later edit. |
| MON-85 | The hub stores when a probe's current settings took effect. Until the first result, freshness counts from that time and only newer report gaps count. A probe revision closes alerts for the previous settings at once. The migration uses creation time for existing checks. | A new check gets time to run and the previous target's alerts cannot describe the new one. |
| MON-86 | The hub applies the agent's clock offset to the result time when it records it, in either direction. The original timestamp identifies repeated reports. | A cached result from before a report gap stays stale even when the agent clock is fast. |
| MON-87 | The first check-in deadline counts its period plus grace from when it was set up or its schedule changed. After the first successful ping, the schedule counts from that ping. | Turning an old website check into a check-in gives the job a full period to report. |
| MON-88 | The hub evaluates its cached report as soon as a limit changes. Alerts that fall below the new effective limit resolve at once, and the usual opening delay still applies. Server-wide edits reach only items that inherit the changed limit. | A settings change is deliberate. The clear delay still covers readings that hover near an unchanged limit. |
| MON-89 | A custom temperature or wear warning must be below the effective critical limit at its scope. Edits that would reverse that order return `invalid_limit` with the applicable bound. If a hardware change makes a stored warning invalid, the hub uses the built-in warning or lowers it below the critical limit. | Crossing the warning limit should produce a warning before the reading reaches critical. |

## Automations and one-time watches (MON-90…MON-99)

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| MON-90 | A `thing` trigger selects one page id or kinds (`server`, `device`, `connection`, or `connection:<catalogId>`). It can name the check behind the incident. It listens for down, problem or back, only from the Servers signer. | The same rule can cover one service or every service of its kind. |
| MON-91 | Each automation claims an incident once, even across edits and when a problem turns into down. The first matching transition claims it. If it is still waiting and another matching transition comes due earlier, the earlier time wins. | The same outage only starts one run. A down-only rule can still see a problem become down. |
| MON-92 | Delay counts from the incident's start. A delayed start is kept in the database and rechecks the incident and its quiet or cause before running. Closing the incident, turning the automation off or replacing its version ends the wait. | An old timer shouldn't start work on something that recovered or was taken out of service. |
| MON-93 | The assistant's `thing_watch` starts in Chat for the person. It tells once for down or back, right away if Whombat already sees that state. Otherwise it waits up to 24 hours unless asked for a different limit, at most seven days. Cancel settles the card silently. | A one-time request belongs in the conversation that asked for it. |
| MON-94 | A signed event claims matching watches in its own commit. Whombat posts the reply in the same thread as the assistant and retries an unposted reply on the next incident pass. The same pass ends watches past their deadline and says so. | A restart between the event and the reply keeps the request. |
| MON-95 | Session minor 9 reads the trigger, its run and card, and can call `things.cancelWatch`. Older apps get the watch's message text and the automation's plain summary. | Older apps can still read the conversation and the automation list. |
| MON-96 | The engine uses the monitor’s confirmed down and problem incidents. A ten-minute UPS rule needs a check that opens a problem when it goes on battery power. The built-in server UPS check opens a problem at low battery. | The incident trigger carries the status the monitor actually reports. |
| MON-97 | **A delayed start rechecks its check.** At the deadline the check it waited on — the trigger's own, else the one that opened or worsened the incident — must still count on the thing, as the incident watcher reads the looks (watched, not quiet, confirmed). A thing Whombat can't read then is asked again a minute later; a check that cleared ends the wait. | A UPS back on mains power must not shut the NAS down because a disk line keeps the server's incident open. |
| MON-98 | **Thing automations wait behind the rollback gate.** A definition or run started by a `thing` trigger is newer: it isn't made, turned on or run until the release the box would go back to recorded `0027_incident_automation_watches` or later in `runtime_releases` (as Find My's with `0017`), and is refused with “Automations that start when something goes down or comes back come with the next update.” | The release before can't read such a definition, yet would advance a run it started, shutdown steps and all. |
| MON-99 | **“Tell me when Plex is back” while Plex is down because of its server** happens when the watcher sees Plex answering again, not held and without an open incident of its own (one with its own incident still waits for that incident's `thing.back`). It starts with “Plex is down while pve-02 is down. I’ll tell you when Plex answers again.” | A thing down because of something else has no incident of its own, so no `thing.back` ever came and the watch expired. |
| MON-99a | **A trigger that names a check starts on any bad check counting on the incident, not only its worst.** The incident keeps the checks it signed for (`incidents.checks`): opening signs its own event and `thing.problem` for each other bad check counting then; a bad check that begins counting on an open incident later signs `thing.problem` once, carrying that check, its line's words and, as `startedAt`, when it began counting, so a delay counts from then. Not for an incident with a cause. A trigger without a check may start on such an event too, once per incident as always. | “When the UPS has been reporting low battery for 10 minutes” never started while a disk problem kept the server's incident open, and would have counted its ten minutes from the disk's. |
| MON-99b | **A delayed `down` start needs the thing still down at its deadline** (its look is `down`, not answering) as well as its check counting; a delayed `problem` start needs its check still bad on the thing, not only amber. | A connection that went from unreachable to answering with an HTTP error kept the same check, so “down for 10 minutes” ran on something that answered. |
| MON-99c | **While thing automations wait for the next update, nothing claims or starts for them.** The trigger scan claims no incident and starts no run from `thing.*` events, and keeps the delayed starts already waiting; the run advancer holds runs of them like Find My's; `automation_read`'s guide leads with “Automations that start when something goes down or comes back come with the next update.” | A box whose release to go back to had Find My's migrations but not these advanced such runs and claimed incidents. |
| MON-99d | **A thing whose dot is bad but answers counts as answering** for a watch waiting for it (`observedOf('bad')` is `up`). | Waiting for a return, “bad” (a critical line on a thing that answers) said nothing, so a watch on a thing down because of its server waited past its return. |
