# Monitoring servers, networks and services (MON-01…MON-99)

John, 2026-11-24: “I would like to use Whombat for server, network and service monitoring also. What is missing?” A
council of four (GPT 6.2 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5, Muse Wonder Full 1.0, Gemini 4 Neon) reviewed the ideas and added their
own; John chose to build everything they agreed on except checks from outside the house. This file is the one design
all parts follow. Rows MON-01…MON-19 are the design; each part records its own detail decisions in its range:
uptime checks MON-20…MON-39, incidents and history MON-40…MON-59, quiet and causes MON-60…MON-79, the hosts service
MON-80…MON-89, automations and watches MON-90…MON-99.

Everything here keeps “What Whombat watches starts calm” (`watch-defaults.md`) and plain words (`plain-words.md`,
`concise-replies.md`).

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| MON-01 | **Your own checks are connections.** A catalog entry “Website or address” (`uptime`, no sign-in) watches one website (HTTP/HTTPS, an optional word the page must contain), a port (TCP), a ping, a name lookup (DNS) or a check-in (a script or job that must call Whombat in time). The person picks how often (1, 5 or 15 minutes) and where it runs from: this Whombat, or a server Whombat watches. The check itself runs in the hosts service (its `checks`, made at Connect, changed when the connection is edited, removed at Disconnect); the connection's health pass reads the hub check's latest result instead of asking the address itself. | The hosts service has run these checks for a long time with nobody able to reach them. As a connection, a check gets everything a connection has at once: a page and tile, Worth a look, the assistant's research, the Inbox, Ignore and Stop watching, history, automations and the assistant's connect-by-talking. |
| MON-02 | **An uptime check's lines.** `uptime.down` (it doesn't answer, or answers wrong; bad, offline, a problem), `uptime.checkin_late` (a check-in didn't come; bad, only you), `uptime.cert_expiring` (the certificate ends within 14 days: amber; within 3: bad) and `uptime.unseen` (Whombat can't run the check now, because the server it runs from isn't reporting; a heads-up). The first three start on: the person asked for exactly this. The hub's own `check.*` alerts of a check that belongs to a connection never reach the Inbox; the connection's lines do. | One source of truth per check: the connection. A person-made check is the clearest case of “very important and the person wants to hear”. |
| MON-03 | **A check-in's address** is shown on its page with a line ready to paste (`curl` with the hosts service's pinned key) and the words “Works from your home network.” Calls from the internet aren't offered. | The agents' listener uses Whombat's own certificate and isn't on the internet; checks from outside the house were left out on purpose. |
| MON-04 | **Keep quiet for a while, on anything.** Any server, connection or device can be kept quiet until a time (“for an hour”, “until tonight”, “until tomorrow morning”, a time the person picks), with an optional note, from its page's ⋯ and by the assistant on the person's word. While it holds, the thing's lines don't colour it and make no look, research, Inbox item, incident or push; its page says “Quiet until 23:00” with End now. A server's window is also a maintenance window in the hosts service, so its alerts are muted there too, and things that run on a quiet server are quiet with it (MON-07). When it ends, what is still wrong counts again from the start. | “Keep quiet while I work on the NAS tonight.” Maintenance windows existed only for servers and only in the hosts service. |
| MON-05 | **Whombat's own planned work is quiet by itself.** When Whombat updates or restarts a server or machine (the care plan's updates, an approved restart, a step of an automation), it keeps that server or machine quiet for the work and 15 minutes after, noted “Whombat is updating it”. | A planned restart at 03:00 shouldn't start a research or a push. |
| MON-06 | **One push when something important goes wrong.** Whombat keeps an incident for a thing while its dot is bad or offline from lines that count (watched, not quiet, confirmed); it opens after two passes in a row and ends after two passes without. When no research finding has pushed about it within 10 minutes, Whombat pushes once (“Plex is down”, “The NAS: a disk is failing”). When the incident ends the push is withdrawn quietly; nothing pushes “it's back”. Several incidents opening within the same few minutes are one push. Amber never pushes. Do not disturb and quiet hours hold pushes back as for every notification. | Today the phone only buzzes when a research ends with a step to approve: a NAS that is switched off, an AI that is signed out or a day's researches used up meant silence. |
| MON-07 | **One cause, one message.** A connection or device whose machine (what it runs on) is down — its server offline, its machine stopped — is down because of that: it makes no look, Inbox item or incident of its own, its page says “Not answering while pve-02 is down”, and the cause's item names what it takes with it (“Plex and 4 more are down with it”). The same for the internet: while Whombat can't reach the internet (MON-08), connections on internet addresses that fail are grouped under one “The internet is down” item. Things still failing after their cause is back get their own lines again, after the usual confirmation. | One dead host or router should be one message and one research, not ten, and shouldn't use up the day's researches. |
| MON-08 | **Whombat checks its own way out.** Every minute the box checks its router, its name lookups and the internet (two independent addresses, one of them Whombat's own service), from its own server process. It says plainly which step fails (“The internet is down since 14:02”, “Name lookups don't work”, “The router doesn't answer”); this starts on (Whombat can't reach things any more). While the internet is down no push can leave the house, so its incident is told when the internet is back, as history. | The cause for MON-07, and the first question of every outage: is it the internet, or the service? |
| MON-09 | **Uptime that is true, and a list of outages.** Every health pass also stores whether the thing answered (an `up` point, 1 or 0), so the hourly roll-up keeps an hour's share and short outages don't disappear (the response-time average skipped failed checks). Incidents (MON-06) are kept 90 days with when, how long, what and their cause. Every thing's page, of every kind, shows one line: “Up 99.6 % this week · down 3 times, 41 min” (or “Up all week”); a server's page lists its past alerts from the hosts service's 90 days. | The council's shared finding: the data was mostly there, but the figure was wrong and the sentence missing. No monthly report: history is something the person opens or asks for (`brief-removed.md`). |
| MON-10 | **The assistant reads status and history.** Read tools answer what needs the person now (the Inbox, things not fine, quiet windows, open incidents) and what happened (a thing's or every thing's incidents, uptime and past server alerts over a period); `alerts_list` takes resolved alerts and a period. | “Is everything OK?” and “What broke while I was away?” in one call instead of probing every connection. |
| MON-11 | **Automations start on something going down or coming back**, for a thing or every thing of a kind, optionally only after it has been down for some minutes; once per incident. And a one-time watch: “tell me when pve-02 is back” (or “…if Plex goes down tonight”) posts once in that thread and ends, with a card and Cancel, like Find My's one-time watch. | “When the UPS has been on battery for 10 minutes, shut down the NAS” runs without an AI turn. |
| MON-12 | **Backups per service.** A connection's machine (what it runs on) says when it was last backed up, or that nothing backs it up, as a fact on its page. A line `machine.backup_old` (a job covers the machine but its last backup is much older than the job's rhythm) starts on: a backup isn't happening. “Nothing backs this up” stays a fact, not a line: often it is meant. | The most regretted monitoring answer, from data Whombat already reads. |
| MON-13 | **Say when Whombat can't see something.** A check whose server stopped reporting says “Not checked since 14:02” (MON-02 `uptime.unseen`) and that time counts neither as up nor as down; a connection that answers but whose details Whombat couldn't read for an hour has a heads-up saying so; the health pass picks only connections it looks at before its per-pass limit. | A green dot should mean it was checked. |
| MON-14 | **Machines that stopped by themselves.** A container or machine that is set to start by itself and stopped without anyone stopping it (out of memory, or a crash's exit) is a critical alert of its own that starts on. A stop by Whombat, by the person, or a normal stop never counts; when the hosts service can't tell, no alert. | The most common silent outage. Something stopped on purpose is not a problem (`on-purpose.md`). |
| MON-15 | **Limits per server.** A server's rules that have a limit (disk use, SSD wear, temperatures, inodes) take a limit of the server's own or of one item (a disk, a mount), set from the line's ⋯ “Change the limit” on the server's page and by the assistant on the person's word; the hosts service's defaults stay for the rest. | A backup disk that is always 93 % full shouldn't alarm, and switching the check off would leave it blind. |
| MON-16 | **Left out:** checks from outside the house (John, 2026-11-24), a monthly or weekly report, pushes for every blip or for “it's back”, a separate monitoring dashboard, new-device and slow-answer lines. | Calm by default; the person opens history or asks for it. |

## Quiet and causes (MON-60…MON-79)

Keep quiet for a while (MON-04), Whombat's own planned work (MON-05), one cause (MON-07) and the way out (MON-08), as
built.

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| MON-60 | **A window or a cause holds the whole thing inside `applyQuiet`.** The thing's quiet read (`ThingQuiet`) brings its windows and its cause, `notWatched` passes them on, and while one holds every watched line goes to `quiet` with a row of its own kind: a window as snoozed until its end, a cause as ignored with its words (“while pve-02 is down”), both marked `held`. A line's own Ignore keeps its own row. A pass under a hold writes no line state and forgets the old ones. | Every reader already treats quiet lines as quiet (pages, dots, the desk's looks, the Inbox, the incidents built on `deskLooks`), so one place makes them all obey; forgetting the counts is what makes “counts again from the start” true. |
| MON-61 | **One window per thing, the person's, signed.** `things.keepQuiet` (until a time or for 5 minutes to a month, an optional note of 120 characters) changes a window that holds already instead of adding one; `things.endQuiet` ends it. Keeping quiet is a change with Undo (Undo ends it); ending is an everyday change in the thing's Activity. Both are session minor 9 (`KEEP_QUIET_MINOR`), resent by no client (a server's touches the hosts service). | One window to explain and to end. Undo of “Kept the NAS quiet until 23:00” is the natural way back. |
| MON-62 | **A server's window is a maintenance window in the hosts service** (scope server, now until the end, the note as its note), made before the commit and removed if the commit fails, on End now, and by the minute's sweep once a window is over. When the hub refuses, the window still holds in Whombat. A server's page carries `keptQuiet`, hides its banner, and its dot shows nothing while it holds. | The hub's own alerts are muted at the source, and Whombat never waits on the hub to keep its own word. |
| MON-63 | **What the apps get.** A page (`ThingPage.keptQuiet`, `HostServerDetail.keptQuiet`) has the words, the end, the note, who (none: Whombat, `whombat`) and `canEnd`; Home's thing has `quietUntil`; held lines' `NoticeQuiet.held`. Worth a look's first line is a heads-up with the same words, for an app that doesn't draw them yet; an older app reads held lines as snoozed or ignored, and Undo on one of them ends the window. | The next wave draws End now from contracts that already carry it; nothing an app has today breaks. |
| MON-64 | **`quiet_until` acts only when the turn's message is the person's own**, as `watch_checks`; without until, minutes or end it says how the thing is kept quiet. Never in an automation's own step, and the two session ops are no assistant setting. | Keeping something quiet is the person's word. |
| MON-65 | **Whombat's own planned work** is the three tools that clearly restart or update: `guest_power` reboot or restart (the machine, about 5 minutes), `server_power` reboot (the server, about 10 minutes), `server_update` install (the server, until its followed task ends; at most 2 hours), each plus 15 minutes, noted “Whombat is updating it”. The care plan's updates, approved restarts and automation steps all reach these tools. A shutdown or stop isn't planned to come back, so it isn't kept quiet; the person's own buttons on a server's page aren't Whombat's work. A window over one machine (`guest_id`) has no maintenance window (the hub has none per guest) and quiets only what runs on that machine. | Only paths that clearly come back are worth a window; nothing else tells when a restart is over, so its window is a fixed span. |
| MON-66 | **Causes are found by a job every minute and kept** (`thing_causes`, with when it was last found): from the machine each connection's last health pass found it on (kept while it doesn't answer, since a stopped machine isn't found by its address), the fleet's servers and the machines' states. A cause the job hasn't confirmed for 5 minutes counts for nothing. | Readers are pure and many; a stored cause reaches them with the quiet rows they already read. |
| MON-67 | **Which cause:** its server offline, then its machine stopped (both only while the connection doesn't answer), then its server or machine kept quiet (whatever it answers), then the way out failing while it talks to the internet and doesn't answer. | A wrongly found machine never hides a connection that answers; a quiet server quiets all it carries. |
| MON-68 | **A thing down because of something else is grey, not fine**: its dot `unknown`, its line and badge “Not answering while pve-02 is down”, `causedBy` on its page and Home's thing, the cause's page linked. It makes no look; `DeskLook.causedBy` is filled for one that still shows. Devices get no cause in this part: Whombat doesn't know what machine a device is on. | Green would be a lie and red a second message. |
| MON-69 | **The cause names what it takes with it** in its Inbox item (the server's “isn't checking in” alert) and its desk look's reason: “Plex is down with it”, “Plex and Sonarr are down with it”, “Plex and 4 more are down with it”; a machine's things count for its server. | One message that says the whole story. |
| MON-70 | **The way out's probes**: the router is the default gateway from `/proc/net/route`, answering on 53, 80 or 443 (a refusal answers); the internet is a TCP connect to Whombat's own service (the host of `WHOMBAT_APP_URL`, port 443) or `1.1.1.1:443`, by the address each last resolved to (a box without the service: `1.1.1.1` and `9.9.9.9`; `WHOMBAT_WAY_OUT_TARGETS` overrides); name lookups are the system resolver finding those names. The first failing step in that order is the one said. | No root, no outside service of our own to run, and a failing resolver doesn't make the internet look down. |
| MON-71 | **An outage opens after one step has failed for two minutes**, from when it first failed, and ends at the first pass with every step working; another step failing for two minutes replaces it. Outages are kept in their own table (`way_out_outages`, `wayOutHistory`, “The internet was down 14:02–14:31”) for history and the incidents' part. | One lost answer is nothing; the incidents table belongs to its own part. |
| MON-72 | **The way out's item** is an alert of the person's (`box.way_out`, starts on, severity bad): “The internet is down since 14:02”, “Name lookups don’t work since …”, “The router doesn’t answer since …”, with what it takes (“GitHub, Google and 3 more can’t be reached until it’s back”). A connection is on the internet when it is a fixed public service, a public address or a name that isn't a local one, and doesn't run on a server Whombat watches. | Whombat can't reach things any more: very important, and Whombat can say which step. |
| MON-73 | **`WHOMBAT_WAY_OUT`** is `on` in production and `off` on a development box and in tests unless set. Ended windows are kept 90 days. | Development and tests never probe the internet by themselves. |
