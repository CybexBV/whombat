# A service's settings, changed from its page or by the assistant (SST)

John, 2026-11-04 (main checkout): Whombat should become more of a server and services dashboard, with sub-views (how
the disks are used) and full service pages (Plex) with useful information and settings, and the assistant one click
away for each part. He liked the idea that the assistant makes the changes instead of Whombat building forms for them.
Two other models reviewed that against the code at the highest effort and both came back with the same middle way:
the settings that matter are declared per service as typed changes that a switch, a Change sheet and the assistant all
run through one path, every change is checked with the service and can be put back, and only the long tail goes to the
assistant's free-form calls. John agreed and saw the mockups (`~/.cache/wb-tmp/mockups/service-dash-v2/`), then:
“Implement this fully”.

Builds on service-dashboards.md (SD-*), server-pages.md (SPG-*), assistant-settings.md (ASG-*) and home-redesign.md
(HR-*). It narrows SD-02 and SD-08. No contracts release (the appliance, relay and API use none of it, as ASG-16).

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| SST-01 | **A service declares the dozen settings that matter** (`KindModule.settings`, `things/kinds/settings.ts`): each with its group, name, one plain sentence, its value in words, and either a switch or a list of choices, and how to read it from the service and write it back. The page shows them on a tab of their own as `settings` panels (one card per group), and every switch and Change runs `things.action` with the kind's `setting` action (`target` the setting, `value` the new value). The assistant runs the very same action (SST-04). | One typed path for the person's tap and the assistant: the same checks, the same words, the same record; the page works without the AI. |
| SST-02 | **Done means checked**: the action reads the setting first, refuses a value that isn't one of its choices, writes it, then reads it back from the service. Only when the service reads back the new value does Whombat record “Changed” with Undo and say so (“Plex now allows up to 20 Mbps outside your home. Checked with Plex.”). If the service still reports the old value, it refuses (“Plex didn’t take the change. It still says 8 Mbps.”). If the read-back fails, drops the setting or shows some other unexpected value, Whombat records an uncertain outcome in plain words (“Asked Plex to set …”) with no “Changed” and no Undo, and does not mark a Put-it-back complete. Choosing what it already is says so and changes nothing. | A change the service quietly ignored or Whombat couldn't verify must never look done or offer Undo. |
| SST-03 | **Every declared change can be put back** for 7 days: it is recorded as a change of the thing (kind `thing.setting.<setting>`, title “Changed Quality outside your home in Plex from 8 Mbps to 20 Mbps”) with an undo recipe (`thing.action`: the action, the setting, the old value and the value it set). **Put it back** — in the toast, on the setting's row and in Activity's Undo — runs the same action as the person with the old value, and refuses in plain words when the setting was changed since (“Quality outside your home was changed again since, so Whombat won’t put it back.”). The row says who changed it last and when (“Dusty changed this yesterday from 4 Mbps.”, on a phone “Changed by Dusty yesterday”). Supersedes SD-08's “No Undo” for declared settings; other buttons keep it. | A person tries a setting when they can take it back with one tap. |
| SST-04 | **The assistant changes a declared setting through the same action**: it reads the page (`things.page`), whose settings tab lists each declared setting with its id, value and choices, then `settings_change` with `things.action {action: 'setting', target, value}`. That always asks on a card (`things.action` is an `ask` entry, ASG-06), and the card's words are Whombat's, from the kind (`describe`): “Change a Plex setting”, “Quality outside your home: 8 Mbps → 20 Mbps”. After the yes it runs once as the person and the change records the assistant as the one who asked (`agentId`), so it also shows in Done recently with Undo. A setting that isn't declared goes through the connection's own request tool, which asks first for Plex's `/:/prefs` (unchanged). | The person approves exactly what runs, in words they read, and finds it again on the page. |
| SST-05 | **What stays in the service** (narrows SD-02): updating or upgrading it, deleting anything, accounts, sharing and sign-in, and anything that could lock the person out. Such a row shows its value and “In Plex” with a link to the service's own settings page; it has no switch or Change. Everything else SD-02 lists stays as it was. | Settings are now something a page may change, but only the reversible ones. |
| SST-06 | **The tab names**: the service's settings are a server-built tab after the Summary, named “{Service} settings” (key `service-settings`). The clients' own Settings tab (the connection's sign-in, Look only or Manage, the checks) is now **Connection**; its key stays `settings` so old links still open it. | Two tabs both called Settings would be a guess every time. |
| SST-07 | **Plex's settings** (from `/:/prefs`, written with `PUT /:/prefs?<id>=<value>`): *Watching away from home* — Remote access (`PublishServerOnPlexOnlineKey`, a switch), Quality outside your home (`WanPerStreamMaxUploadRate`: Original quality, 20, 12, 8, 4, 2 Mbps), Secure connections (`secureConnections`: Required, Preferred); *Converting video* — Use the graphics chip (`HardwareAcceleratedCodecs`, shown only when Plex offers it), Conversion quality (`TranscoderQuality`: Automatic, Faster, Better picture), Conversions at once (`TranscodeCountLimit`: No limit, 1–4, 6, 8); *Libraries* — Scan when files change (`FSEventLibraryUpdatesEnabled`), Scan on a schedule (`ScheduledLibraryUpdatesEnabled` with `ScheduledLibraryUpdateInterval`: Off, every hour, 2, 6, 12 hours, every day), Empty the trash after a scan (`autoEmptyTrash`); *Upkeep* — Quiet hours for upkeep (`ButlerStartHour`/`ButlerEndHour`, pairs from 00:00 – 03:00 to 04:00 – 07:00), and Updates (In Plex, SST-05). “Secure connections: Off” isn't offered: it can only make Plex less safe. A setting Plex doesn't report is left out; when `/:/prefs` can't be read the tab says so with Open Plex. The tab starts with one line (“The settings that matter most, in plain words. Change one here, or ask {assistant} to do it for you.” · All settings in Plex) and ends with “Something else? {assistant} can change any other Plex setting for you. It asks you first.” · Ask {assistant}. | The dozen people actually look for; the names are what a person calls them, not Plex's ids. |
| SST-08 | **Ask about a card**: a panel may carry `ask` with one to three questions; its head gets a small **Ask** that opens “Ask {assistant} about {title}” with them as chips and “Or ask something else…”, each opening the Ask pane with that question. Plex's Now playing (why something converts, who watched most), Libraries and each settings group carry them. It is the only part of a page that needs the AI. | One click from any part of a page to the assistant, about that part. |
| SST-09 | **Changing a setting**: a switch flips at once (no question; flipping back and Put it back undo it). A setting with choices opens a sheet — a dialog on the web, a bottom sheet on the phone — with its name, a sentence, the choices with “Now” on the current one, a hint when Whombat knows one, and **Ask {assistant} instead** · Cancel · **Change it**. “Ask {assistant} instead” opens the Ask pane with “Help me choose {setting} for {service}.” | One way for every setting; the assistant stays one tap away for “I want the iPad to stop converting”. |
| SST-10 | **A service's verdict**: a page may carry one plain sentence under its name (`ThingPage.verdict`), like a server's (SPG-01). Plex's while it is fine: “Plex is fine: 2 people are watching and every library is up to date.” (or which library is being scanned); otherwise the page's own words from the same line as the badge: “Plex has a problem: …” or “Plex needs a look: No remote access.” (`verdictField`). | The first question is “is it all right?”, on a service's page too. |
| SST-11 | **What uses a server's space** (Storage): built in `@whombat/client/hosts` (`spaceUse`) from each machine's and app's own disk (`diskUsedGb`), biggest first, and — on a server with one volume — the rest of its used space as “Everything else”, under tiles in words (how full, when it fills up, disks to watch). When a recent on-request disk breakdown exists (measured within 24 hours by the hosts agent's `disk_usage` stream — through the assistant's `server_disk_usage` tool during a Look or chat, or on demand from the Storage tab), it replaces “Everything else” with the measured categories (container images, build cache, volumes, logs, system journal, package caches, VM disks and snapshots where known) and largest non-overlapping folders, together with when it was measured; spun-down disks are skipped and never woken. | Gives both the assistant and the person a real breakdown when a disk fills up without adding scheduled disk walks or waking sleeping drives. |
| SST-12 | **Generic `KindModule.settings` wiring**: `KindModule` carries an optional `settings?: KindSettings` built with `declaredSettings(declared)` (`things/kinds/settings.ts`). `connectionPage` inserts its `service-settings` tab right after Summary when the service was read, `actionFor` dispatches `setting` to `kind.settings.action`, and `describeThingAction` asks `kind.settings.describe` for the approval card's words. Plex moved onto it with no change in behaviour. | Adding settings to another service is one module and one field, with no duplicated tab, action or approval-card plumbing. |
| SST-13 | **Verdicts and Ask chips on core service pages**: Home Assistant, TrueNAS, Synology, Immich, Paperless-ngx, AdGuard Home, Pi-hole, Sonarr and Radarr each carry one calm plain verdict sentence (`ThingPage.verdict`) built from data the page already reads (naming real operational issues such as stuck downloads, paused blocking or failed jobs even when their checks start off) and two to three contextual Ask chips (`ThingPagePanel.ask`) on their key Summary cards. | The same instant “is it all right?” answer and one-click assistant entry Plex has, with no extra service calls. |
| SST-14 | *In part superseded by SST-15: Pi-hole’s DNSSEC and AdGuard Home’s block lists, safe browsing and parental control stay in the service.* **Declared settings for AdGuard Home, Pi-hole, Sonarr, Radarr and Immich**: each declares only everyday reversible settings that cannot cut off access, the network or delete stored data (SST-05), reading and writing full config objects where the service's API requires it so untouched fields (auth, upstream DNS, ports, storage templates) stay intact — AdGuard Home (`home/adguard-settings.ts`: block lists, update interval, safe browsing, parental control, query log on/off, anonymise IPs, statistics on/off; safe search engine selection, query-log retention and statistics retention stay in AdGuard Home), Pi-hole (`home/pihole-settings.ts`: blocking mode, DNSSEC, query log, privacy level via `/api/config/{dns,database,misc}` and `PATCH /api/config`, never `/api/config/webserver`; query-history retention stays in Pi-hole), Sonarr and Radarr (`media/arr-settings.ts`: rename on import, hardlinks, extra files, minimum free space, completed download handling, retry failed, unmonitor deleted, RSS sync interval via `/api/v3/config/{mediamanagement,naming,downloadclient,indexer}`, never `/api/v3/config/host`; Propers & Repacks stays in Sonarr/Radarr because replacing a file can permanently delete the old one), and Immich (`storage/immich-settings.ts`: video transcode mode and resolution, smart features, automatic database backups on/off, version checks via `/api/system-config` with 404 fallback to `/api/admin/config`, and a plain 403 non-admin message; trash retention and database-backup retention stay in Immich). OPNsense and UniFi Network keep no declared settings (lock-out risk). | Covers the common day-to-day settings safely across five more services while keeping lock-out and data-deleting retention settings in the service itself (Undo can restore a number, not deleted photos, backups or history). |
| SST-15 | **DNS validation and filtering switches stay in the service** (refines SST-05 and SST-14): Pi-hole's DNSSEC switch (`dnssec`, `home/pihole-settings.ts`) and AdGuard Home's DNS filtering switches (`filtering_enabled`, `safebrowsing`, `parental`, `home/adguard-settings.ts`) are read-only `elsewhere` rows (“In Pi-hole” / “In AdGuard Home”). Writable settings on Pi-hole are blocking mode for already-blocked domains, query log and privacy level; on AdGuard Home, block-list update interval (preserving `enabled`), query log, IP anonymisation and statistics; Sonarr, Radarr and Immich keep their existing writable settings (none touch auth, listeners, network or lookups). | Turning on DNSSEC against an upstream that lacks DNSSEC records, or enabling DNS block lists, custom rules or external filtering feeds that match Whombat’s own address, breaks name lookups after the service saves the flag — cutting the person off before they can reach Undo. |
