# A person's accounts as connections (ACC)

John, 2026-11-03 (main checkout): “On dev appliance I have configured two accounts (Github and Google), but they are
not shown in the Connections list (sidebar for example), these should be visible also, since it's a proper connection.
At Google it seems that Dusty is unable to delete messages, but I see nowhere I can change the permissions. We need to
be able to change permissions, I want it to be able to delete as well.” Google showed in the sidebar only as “Mail”
(and only with mail connected), GitHub not at all; there was no tool to delete mail, and the keys to an account were
only on Settings → Advanced → Keys, where trashing files was fixed at Ask.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ACC-01 | **An account is a connection like any other** on Home and in the sidebar's Connections: Google and Microsoft 365 by their name (“Google”, “Microsoft 365”) and mark, whatever services they bring, Email (IMAP) as “Email”. One with mail keeps its unread count in its line (“3 unread · you@example.com”), one without says which account it is. They open their page on the Connections tab (superseded in part by connection-dashboards.md CDB-80: they open a page of their own, with that page as its Connection tab). | John: “it's a proper connection”. A “Mail” row didn't read as his Google account, and a Google account without mail didn't show at all. |
| ACC-02 | **GitHub, connected for this Whombat, is a connection too** (`connection:github`): “GitHub”, its mark, “@login · 69 repositories on”, opening GitHub's page (superseded in part by connection-dashboards.md CDB-80: its own page). Read from the database only (the connection row and the repositories switched on), never from GitHub, as the things list is read often. It is always shown as fine there; what is wrong with the sign-in says GitHub's page. | Same reason; a call to GitHub on every Home read would be slow and could fail. |
| ACC-03 | **Moving mail to the trash** is a tool (`mail_trash`, up to 100 messages) and a key of its own (`mail.delete`, “Move mail to the trash”): Gmail's own trash (`messages/{id}/trash`, kept 30 days, within `gmail.modify`), Outlook's Deleted Items, IMAP's Trash folder (made when missing; a message already there isn't expunged). Never deleted for good. `mail_organize` still refuses Trash and Spam labels: one way to trash. | John wants Dusty to delete mail. Trash can be undone; deleting for good can't, and would need a wider Google scope. |
| ACC-04 | **Trashing is the person's choice**: `mail.delete` and `files.delete` may be held at `can` (`agentMaxTier: can`), start at Ask (`ACCOUNT_GRANTS`), and are Off · Ask · On under Advanced → Keys' All keys (`ACCOUNT_TIER_KEYS`). Sending, invites and sharing still always ask (`agentMaxTier: ask`). At Ask, a trash card names the first three emails by subject and sender (read only when it asks); at On it goes ahead and the log says only how many (“Moved 2 emails to the trash”, `mail.trashed`; files: `files.trashed`). Existing agents that read mail get `mail.delete` at Ask (migration 0028). Replaces CN-01…CN-08's “trashing always asks” and IN-15's “there is no mail trash action”. | Trashed mail and files can be taken back out; what reaches other people can't, and mail content could steer the agent into sending. |
| ACC-05 | **WHAT {ASSISTANT} MAY DO on the account's page** (web and phone, Google and Microsoft 365): the account keys of the services it brings, grouped (Mail, Calendar, Files, Contacts), each in a connection's tool words, Allowed · Asks · Off; the always-asking ones only Asks · Off with “Always asks you first.” A change saves at once through `agent.setKeys`, every other key as held (`withAccountTier`, `@whombat/client/connections`). | John: “I see nowhere I can change the permissions”. Where the account is, in the words its neighbours' tools use. |

Older apps read the agent's keys strictly (AP-707): until they update, Advanced → Keys on them says it needs a newer
app, because of the new key.
