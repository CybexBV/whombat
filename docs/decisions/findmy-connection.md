# Workstream: Find My as a connection (FMC)

John, 2026-11-23: “I would like to make this a separate connector which requires a Mac server to be configured.” Dusty
may see where people are (on by default), reading in the background is optional, and arriving and leaving automations
come with it. The plan is `docs/implementation/macos/findmy-connection.md`; how Find My is read on the Mac stays
`macos-servers.md` MAC-52…MAC-59. Names here are made up (Mara, Ruben and Noor Visser, a Mac called Studio).

## The box (2026-11-23)

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| FMC-01 | **Find My is a connection of its own that names one Mac.** Its `mac` field holds the Mac's id in the hosts fleet; its base is the Servers connection's (`whombat-hosts:`) and nothing is ever sent there: making or changing one skips the address and destination checks (`requireBaseUrl`, `requireDestination`). | The Mac is already one of Whombat's servers; a second address for the same machine would be one more thing to get wrong. |
| FMC-02 | **The `mac` check is the hosts fleet's word, never the Mac's screen**: the field's Mac (or the only Mac, when none is given) is one of the servers, runs macOS, and its helper (`macos.helper.version` on its detail) isn't older than 1.1.0; a version it can't read isn't judged there. The answer is the Macs as `{id, name}`, so `chooseServiceFields` takes the only one and asks when there are several. Words: no Mac (“Whombat doesn’t watch a Mac yet. Add your Mac in Servers first.”), a removed one (“Studio isn’t one of Whombat’s servers any more. Pick another Mac.”), a server that isn't a Mac, and today's `helper_old` words. A Mac that isn't as needed answers 409; a hosts service that doesn't answer leaves the status null (unreached). | The health pass runs every check every 5 minutes: it must cost a fleet read and one detail, never a screen. |
| FMC-03 | **The page opens on Find My, and the tab names its Mac** (`{ key: 'findmy', label: 'Find My', server: <id>, panels: [] }`, the kind's first tab); the apps draw it from that Mac's `hosts.findMy*` operations. Supersedes MAC-60's Find My tab on the Mac's page. No response-time chart (nothing answers). | Find My belongs to the person's family, not to the machine; the Mac stays the way in. |
| FMC-04 | **One Find My connection per Mac**: another on the same Mac is refused on its `mac` field with “Find My on Studio is already connected.” A second one on another Mac is named after its Mac (“Find My · Office”), as other services get their address then. | Each Mac is signed in to its own Apple Account; two connections on one Mac would read the same people twice. |
| FMC-05 | **`needs_mac` while there is no Mac**: the catalog says the entry isn't available yet when the fleet has no macOS server or the box has no hosts service; when the hosts service doesn't answer it is offered, and its check says why. | “Add a Mac first” is the one step a person can take; an unanswered fleet says nothing about Macs. |
| FMC-06 | **The card is the box's memory, never a read**: “3 people · read 5 min ago”, “Not read yet”, or the read's problem in a few words (“The Mac is in use”). No places on it. When the connection's own check fails the card says why in a few words (“The Mac is gone”). | Overview is also on the desk display; the health pass must never take the Mac's screen. |
| FMC-07 | **One check, “Find My can’t be read” (`findmy.read`), starts off**: the last read's problem as a line of Worth a look. | A read that failed is what a person opens the page for; nothing breaks while it does (watch-defaults.md). |

## Reading in the background (2026-11-23)

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| FMC-10 | **One loop for the box, a pass every minute** (`hosts/findmy-background.ts`, housekeeping), and at once when an automation that listens to Find My is published, switched or removed, or a connection is added, changed or removed. A connection reads when its Read in the background isn't Off, or when a live `findmy` automation listens to it: then at least every 15 minutes (`FIND_MY_LISTENED_MS`). | One place decides when a Mac's screen is used; a minute is fine-grained enough for a 5-minute pace. |
| FMC-11 | **Each background read is the page's own read** (`FindMyStore.refresh`, as Whombat at admin): one job at a time per Mac, a Mac in use left alone, a locked Mac put back (MAC-53). A Mac in use is tried again after 2 minutes; another failure waits twice the pace each time, up to an hour, and the pace comes back after a read that worked. A background read isn't a look. | The same steps keep the same promises; doubling waits keep a Mac without Find My from being opened every few minutes. |
| FMC-12 | **What a background read finds stays in memory while the connection reads** (`FindMyStore.hold`); once it stops, the hour after the last look by a person applies (MAC-55's hour). | A pace of an hour must not lose the read just before the next one; nothing is kept longer than someone needs it. |
| FMC-13 | **It stops** when the connection is removed, its Mac is no longer a macOS server in the fleet, or it is Off with nothing listening. A pass whose fleet can't be read keeps the last plans. | A hosts service that restarts must not stop the reads an automation needs. |
| FMC-14 | **`hosts.findMy` says how the Mac is read** (`HostFindMy.background`): `{ everyMinutes, automation }`, with the automation's name when it is why the pace is what it is (the person's own setting is sooner otherwise), null while it reads only when someone looks. The same on the refresh's and the exact picture's answers. | The page's line under the map (“Reads every 15 minutes for “Tell me when Mara gets home””) needs both. |

## Arriving and leaving (2026-11-23)

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| FMC-20 | **Every read that worked is compared** (the page's, the background's, the assistant's: `FindMyStore.onRead`), for each Find My connection on that Mac that a live automation listens to; one comparison at a time per Mac. A person without a place keeps their last place; a person with nothing kept gets their place kept without a change (a first read invents no arrival); a place that differs without case or spaces is a change. | Whoever read it, a change is a change; only listened connections need one. |
| FMC-21 | *Narrowed by FMT-02: with a timeline on, the people the person ticked are kept 30 days, street and spot included (`findmy-timeline.md`).* **Only while an automation listens does a connection keep anything**: each person's last place, Find My's words and since when, one row per person in `findmy_places` (migration `0017_findmy_places`, cascading with the connection), written only when it changes, through the commit pipeline (`findmy.places_noted`). The rows go once nothing listens any more (`findmy.places_cleared`, each background pass). Never a street, coordinates or history. | An update or a restart must neither make up an arrival nor miss one; without an automation there is nothing to compare for. |
| FMC-22 | **Each change is one signed `findmy.place_changed`** (`{ connectionId, person, name, from, to }`, no text, kind System) signed by the burrow's Find My signer (`principals.system = 'findmy'`, “Find My”, made on first use like Mail's and the trackers'). It nudges the automations engine. | Automations start only on signed events; a signer of its own says who noticed it, and no text keeps it out of every feed. |
| FMC-23 | **A run's scope**: `trigger.text` (“Mara Visser is now at Home (was in Lelystad).”: “at” Find My's own places Home, Work, School and Gym, “in” a town), `trigger.person` (the name), `trigger.personId`, `trigger.place`, `trigger.from`, `trigger.connectionId`. Its trigger info's summary is “Mara Visser is now at Home”. Only the Find My signer's events count, and a change noticed before the automation's version was published starts nothing. | A message step words it in one line; an event from before the automation listened was for another automation. |
| FMC-24 | **A `findmy` trigger names a Find My connection of the burrow** (`referenceProblems`); removing the connection leaves its automations as a removed mailbox leaves mail automations: they start nothing. | The same rule as mail and trackers. |
| FMC-25 | **The release gate holds `findmy` automations** until the release a box would go back to recorded schema `0017_findmy_places` or later in `runtime_releases` (`NewerAutomationsGate.findMyReady`): until then they aren't made, switched on or run, nothing is kept, and the agent's guide says “Find My automations come with the next update.” A run of one waits as mail runs do. | The release before them parses definitions strictly and would refuse, or send unchanged to phones, an automation it doesn't know. |
| FMC-26 | **Apps below session minor 4 get a form they read** (`older-apps.ts`): a `findmy` automation without its definition and draft and with a message's source, a `findmy` run's trigger as a message, its export refused (“Update the app”); mail automations stay whole at minor 3. | The same way mail automations reached apps below minor 3. |
| FMC-27 | **`automation_read` lists `findMyPeople`**: for each Find My connection, the people of its Mac's last read in memory and those it keeps, with the connection, Find My's id, the name and the place's words. | The agent needs the ids a trigger names; it gets no street or coordinates. |

## The assistant (2026-11-23)

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| FMC-30 | **`findmy_people { person? }`** is the connection's one tool, served by its adapter (`connections/adapters/findmy.ts`), only in an assistant's turn (as the Servers tools). It reads first when the last read is older than 2 minutes (the store's own rule), as the assistant for the person at admin, waiting up to 45 s; otherwise, or when that read doesn't work, it answers the last read with its age and why (“This is the read from 10 min ago. It couldn’t read again just now: …”). | A person asking where someone is wants now, but not at the cost of a Mac someone is using. |
| FMC-31 | **What it answers**: the Mac, when it read, and each person's name, place, street, when Find My saw them and whether they are nearby; `person` picks by whole name, then first name, then part of a name, and says who is there when nobody is called that. Never pictures or coordinates. | The street is what a person would say; a spot on a map is the Mac's guess and isn't needed in words. |
| FMC-32 | **The assistant's answers stay in the chat like any answer** (supersedes MAC-55's “in memory only” for what the assistant is told). Nothing read is logged. | John: the assistant may see where people are. |
