# The desk display: talking to the assistant (stream T)

Decisions the talk stream made building desk-display.md DK-06 and DK-07 (build brief
`docs/implementation/desk-display/assignment.md` §2.3, §2.4, §3 T) on contracts 0.100.0 (`DeskTalk`, `DeskServer`, the
voice socket's `VoiceHealth`, `VoiceSttRequest`/`Answer`, `VoiceTtsRequest`/`Answer`, `DispatchContext.desk`; CT-527,
CT-529, CT-532). No migration. Code: `apps/server/src/desk/talk.ts` (the talk, the reply watch, the words),
`talk-mark.ts` (the question's mark, the turn's `desk`, who reads the screen now); `apps/server/src/voice/speech.ts`
(the voice service's client, the WAV checks, `voiceHealth`) and `voice/testing/fake-voice.ts`; `runtime/context.ts` (`DispatchContext.desk`);
`domain/message-notify.ts` and `domain/notify.ts` (the push held back); `packages/agent-runtime/src/persona/desk.ts` and
`apps/runner/src/prompt.ts` (the runner's line).

| Id | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| DK-T01 | **Talk is one module behind a narrow host.** The desk module calls `createDeskTalk(services, host)` once and gets `start`, `get`, `audio`, `latest`, `forget` and `close`. `DeskTalkHost` is four questions: `place()` (the paired `{burrowId, personId}` or null), `unlocked()`, `voiceOn()` (“Talk to {assistant}” in Settings) and `deskServerByName(name)` (the glance's card), each answered at once or with a promise; since 0.105 an optional fifth, `speechVoice()` (the voice chosen, desk-display-server.md DK-S53). `start` refuses with a `DeskTalkError` carrying the desk socket's status and code: `not_paired` 409, `locked` 409, `busy` 409, `voice_unavailable` 503, `bad_request` 400, `bad_audio` 400. | The desk module owns pairing, the lock and the glance; Talk needs only these facts. Talk checks the lock itself as well as the route (DK-07: never on a locked screen) because the microphone's audio arrives here. |
| DK-T02 | **The voice service's client** (`WHOMBAT_VOICE_SOCKET`, §2.4) opens one connection per call (no keep-alive pool), waits 2 s for health, 60 s for speech to text (30 s of audio behind a queue of two) and 30 s for speech, reads at most 64 KiB (speech: the contract's `VOICE_TTS_BASE64_MAX`), validates both ways and turns `LocalError`s into `unavailable`, `busy`, `bad_audio`, `too_large` or `error`. `voiceHealth(services)` is cached a minute, a failure 15 s; no service or no answer is “nothing works”. `WHOMBAT_VOICE=fake` runs the fake in process (refused in production), `off` none. | A pooled connection the service had closed failed later calls (and once a write nobody listened to). The 15 s retry lets the screen find a voice service that started after the runtime. |
| DK-T03 | **Superseded in part by dictation.md DT-37, DT-40**: Talk's mic sends its recording to `hear`, which is voice typing's (at most five minutes, voice typing's model, language and names); this row holds for the recording an older screen still sends with its talk. **What is heard.** A recording is checked before anything (RIFF/WAVE, integer PCM, 16 kHz, mono, 16-bit, at most 30 s: else 400 `bad_audio`). One whose loudest sample is under about −40 dBFS is `not_heard` without transcribing. Whisper's prompt names what the person may say the way Whombat spells it: the assistant's name, then the servers' (the hosts fleet), then the things' (the connections the person sees), comma-separated, at most 400 characters. Its notes about the sound (`[BLANK_AUDIO]`, `(silence)`, `*coughs*`) are dropped; nothing with a letter or digit left is `not_heard`. The audio goes to the service once, is never stored or logged, and is dropped. | Whisper invents words for silence (“Thank you.”) and writes “PVE 02” for a server it hasn't been told about. DK-07: the audio is deleted once it is text. |
| DK-T04 | **Where the question goes.** `start_chat` as the person: a new thread in their Chat (in the crew shell its task chat). With `continue`, within 2 minutes of the last desk talk's last change (posted or answered) and for the same person, `send_message` as a reply in that thread; when the reply can't go (the thread deleted, the room archived) the question starts a new thread. Only the screen's own last thread is continued, never another person's. | DK-06's follow-up, with what is already there: the thread is an ordinary Chat thread, the assistant answers in it as it answers anywhere in Chat. |
| DK-T05 | **The turn knows it came from the screen through the question's client id**, `desk:<talk id>:v` (spoken) or `:t` (typed), the way an Undo's message carries its change (home-redesign.md HR-03). `buildDispatchContext` makes it `DispatchContext.desk = {spoken}` for the `dm`/`mention` turn the message woke; the runner adds one line to the user turn: “This came from the screen on {first name}’s desk[, by voice]: answer in one message of one or two short sentences that read well aloud — no lists, links, tables or code.” | No column, no migration; the client id also makes a retried post one message. The line is in the user turn, not the system prompt, so the prompt hash and a resumed session stay. A person who writes such a client id by hand only changes how their own question is answered. The claim's own contract carries the field to the runner (tested through `runnerApi.claim`). |
| DK-T06 | **What counts as the answer** (`ReplyWatch`). It listens on the bus from before the question is posted, keeping the assistant's frames until it knows the conversation. The answer is the assistant's last message in the thread after the question, when its turn ends: `dispatch.updated` done, failed or cancelled of the turn the question queued, or, when the question was merged into the turn already running there, of that turn. When the turn goes on after it said something, a message counts once nothing more came for 15 s. Nothing within 90 s: `failed/slow`. A turn that ended without a word, or with only a failed turn's card: `failed/failed`. An approval card without text counts as its question. | The runner posts a turn's final text just before it ends the turn, so the end is the reliable moment; the 15 s rule covers a turn that answered and then keeps working. The desk line asks for one message, so the first is rarely an interim “Let me check”. |
| DK-T07 | **The answer as shown and heard** (`speechText`): the light Markdown made plain — bold and italics as their words, mentions as names, links as their words (a bare address goes), headings and list items as sentences; code, tables, images and struck text go. At most 600 characters, ending at the last whole sentence that fits. It is spoken only when Talk is on and the box has speech and a speaker; the screen shows it either way. The talk turns `answered` once the audio is ready, with `audio: true`. | Markdown read aloud is noise (“asterisk asterisk”). One state change means the page can play the answer the moment it sees it, with no second poll for the audio. |
| DK-T08 | **The thing is a server named in the answer, else in the question**, matched without case, separators or leading zeros mattering (“PVE 2”, “pve02” = pve-02), the longer name winning at the same place; the card is the host's `deskServerByName`. Nothing named, or a name the glance doesn't know: no card. | `DeskTalk.thing` is a `DeskServer`. What whisper writes and what the assistant writes differ in spelling, not in the name. |
| DK-T09 | **The fake voice** (`testing/fake-voice.ts`) hears fixed transcripts (queued, then “Is pve-02 okay?”; nothing in a silent recording), checks recordings as the real one does, says a tone as long as the words take (24 kHz like Kokoro, DK-36) at a pitch per voice — its health lists the six desk voices, heart (440 Hz) first, and it refuses a voice it doesn't list (DK-50, desk-display-server.md DK-S55) — records prompts, texts and the voice asked for but never audio, can fail the next call, report less in `health` (no `voices`: a service from before 0.105), or hold transcribing until a test lets go. It runs in process (`WHOMBAT_VOICE=fake`: development, the appliance's dev world) or on a unix socket with §2.4's routes (`startFakeVoice`). | Tests and the dev world show the whole flow without whisper or Kokoro; the socket form tests the real client. |
| DK-T10 | **What stays in memory**: at most five talks, each for 10 minutes from its last change; the answer's audio for 10 minutes. `forget()` (the desk module, when the screen is unpaired or paired to someone else) drops them all and stops waiting for the one that runs; a talk started by another person drops the last person's. The desk module hides `latest()` while the screen is locked. | DK-07: the answer's audio lives in memory for a replay, nothing longer. DK-03: a locked screen shows no message words. |
| DK-T11 | **One talk at a time, until it ends**: from the first check of `start` until `answered` or `failed`, waiting for the answer included; a second `start` is 409 `busy`. | One microphone, one answer on the screen; the page holds the talk button until it knows. |
| DK-T12 | **Typing works without voice.** Talk off in Settings, no voice service, or one that can't transcribe refuses a recording (503 `voice_unavailable`) but takes a typed question; its answer then isn't spoken. | The keyboard cover is the way to ask on a box without a microphone, and a quiet one. |
| DK-T13 | **No push while the person reads the desk screen** (the lead's decision, 2026-10-20). The assistant's message in the thread of a person's own desk question (the thread root's client id `desk:…`) doesn't push to that person's phone while their desk screen is unlocked (`DeskTalkHost.unlocked()`, through `deskScreenShowsTo(services, personId)`, which `createDeskTalk` registers per `Services`). The inbox row and its realtime frame stay; once the screen locks, or on a box without a desk screen, the usual rules apply. The check is `deskReaderOf` in `notifyForMessage` and one draft flag, `pushHeld`, in `notify`. | They are reading the answer on the screen in front of them; the phone buzzing with the same words is noise. Holding only the push keeps the thread's unread state and history as any other Chat thread's. |
| DK-T14 | **The open conversation replaces the last thread** (DK-64, amends DK-T04): Talk holds one conversation `{conversationId, rootId, burrowId, personId, startedAt, lastAt}`. `continue: true` posts in it while it is the paired person's and `lastAt` is within `DESK_TALK_SESSION_IDLE_S` (30 min), else starts one; `continue: false` starts one. `lastAt` moves with every question and every message in its thread (the bus, while it is open). `close()` (`POST /v1/desk/talk/close`) ends it; `forget()` too. In memory only: a restart ends it. | The page sends `continue: true` for every question but the first after *Ask* on another conversation's card; a console from before sends it within its 2 minutes, which the open conversation covers. |
| DK-T15 | **The conversation as the screen shows it** (`session()`, `POST /v1/desk/talk/session`, only unlocked): the thread's root and replies (a flat room: its messages since the conversation started), the person's and the assistant's only, not deleted, the newest 40 (`earlier` counts the rest) and no more than `DESK_SESSION_MAX_BYTES`. Each message made plain per block (`sessionParagraphs`: paragraphs and headings one each, list items each with “• ”, at most 8 of at most 600 code points; code, tables, pictures, files and anything cut set `more`). An approval's card is `ask {number, state}` (`waiting`, `approved`, `denied`), its words the card's question; a failed turn's card is left out. A desk question carries its talk (its client id); the answer a talk took carries that talk (DK-T06's message id), with `audio` while its sound is kept and the server it names. Read again when its thread changes (the bus) and at most every 10 s otherwise. | Whombat holds what shows, so a kiosk restart or an unlock finds it again; 40 messages are more than a desk conversation has, and Chat has them all. |
| DK-T16 | **A follow talk** (`follow(thread)`, DK-S63): a talk without a question that waits for the assistant's next words in the thread as an answer is waited for (90 s, 15 s settle), except that a turn ending before it said anything doesn't end the wait (the turn that asked may end as the decision lands). It is said as an answer is (Talk on and a voice), and is the one talk running (`busy` meanwhile). | The person's decision wakes the assistant in that thread (HR-04); its next words are what they wait to hear. |

## Not now

- DK-05's “questions the assistant asked in Chat open Talk in their thread”: `POST /v1/desk/talk` names no thread, so
  with `continue` false the person's answer opens a new thread; the assistant's opening turn sees the end of its last
  thread with them (chat-channel.md CHT-02), which is usually the question's. Continuing the question's own thread needs
  an additive field on the talk request (the ask's number), a contracts change for later.

| DK-T23 | **A Dutch answer is written out as it is said before it is spoken** (`dutchSpeechText`): times (03:00 “drie uur”, 14:30 “half drie”, 14:15 “kwart over twee”, 14:45 “kwart voor drie”, else “negen uur vijf”); names of letters and digits (pve-02 “P V E nul twee”, sda1 “S D A één”: up to three letters, or four in capitals, are spelled, a longer run stays a word, “node drie”; digits with a leading zero digit by digit, else a number); ranges (“twee tot drie”); a leading minus “min”; percentages “procent”; ° and °C “graden”; TB, GB, MB, kB, GHz, MHz, kWh, W and ms as words; numbers as Dutch words (one word below a million, tweeëntwintig, honderdeen, tweeduizendzesentwintig, “één” alone; millions and billions apart; digit by digit past them; dots grouping thousands, a comma or a lone dot before decimals, “komma”; versions with “punt”); hyphens between words as spaces.
