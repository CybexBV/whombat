| Id | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| DK-S01 | **One `DeskService` per runtime, built only when `WHOMBAT_DESK_SOCKET` is set** (`managed/boot.ts`, beside the admin socket). Without it — every self-hosted server, a box whose socket couldn't be made (logged) — there is no desk: `desk.*` answer `not_implemented` (“This Whombat has no screen to show things on.”) and the `desk` feature isn't advertised (`runtimeFeatures({desk})`, `welcome.runtime`). The service is the one authority: the socket and the session ops call the same object. | The brief: the feature only where the socket runs. One object means the screen and Settings can't disagree about the lock or the pairing. |
| DK-S02 | **`desk_screens`: one row at most** (`id` = 1, a check), the paired person's burrow and person (cascading), the PIN hash, the settings as columns, `paired_at`, `wrong_pins`, `retry_at`. No row: unpaired. Written only by commands: `pair_desk` (an owner, as themselves; an upsert, so pairing again replaces), `set_desk_pin` and `update_desk` (the paired person), `unpair_desk` (an owner or the paired person), `desk_wrong_pin` and `desk_right_pin` (as the paired person, the counters only). Signed events: `desk.paired`, `desk.pin_changed`, `desk.unpaired` with words in the log (System), `desk.updated` without; the PIN counters write no event. | The brief's table and commands; `desk_right_pin` is added so the count resets through the pipeline too. A wrong PIN isn't anyone's act: an event signed by the person would say they did it; the fifth is told to them in Chat instead (DK-S05). |
| DK-S03 | **Memory only: the pairing letters, the unlock and a phone request.** A runtime restart ends a pairing and locks the screen; the PIN wait survives (it is in the row). | DK-04's “the unlock is held by Whombat, not the page”; letters, like the claim's (CC-01), never touch the disk. |
| DK-S04 | **The PIN is scrypt** (N 16384, r 8, p 1, a 16-byte salt, a 32-byte key; stored `scrypt$N$r$p$salt$hash` in base64url), compared in constant time, hashed before the transaction. It is never in an event, a log, a payload or a receipt (`desk.pair` and `desk.setPin` are `secret` ops). | About 50 ms per try is nothing at a desk and makes an offline guess of a stolen row cost 10⁶ × that. |
| DK-S05 | **Wrong PINs** (core `deskPinBackoffS`, `deskTriesLeft`): the first four say “{n} tries left”; the fifth and every one after make the screen wait 1 minute × 2^(n−5), at most an hour, and while it waits even the right PIN gets `backoff` (no oracle). The right PIN or a new PIN resets the count. The person hears it once per run of five, at the fifth: their assistant posts `DESK_WORDS.settings.wrongPins` (“Someone typed a wrong PIN on your desk screen five times.”) in their chat (`tell.ts`, as the disk alerts post). PIN tries and decisions on the screen run one at a time. | DK-04's numbers; one at a time so two quick tries can't both read the same count. |
| DK-S06 | **The lock as the screen sees it**: `unlocked` (until the last unlock, touch, decision or talk + 2 minutes) before `phone` (a request waiting) before `backoff` (the PIN wait) before `locked`. The lock button locks at once; a touch on a locked screen is 409 `locked`. | DK-04; the order is what the page should draw when two hold at once. |
| DK-S07 | **Phone unlock: one request at a time.** Pressing *Confirm on your phone* again while one waits returns the same request and sends no second push, so a passer-by can ring the person's phone at most once per 2 minutes. The push is `security`, ref and collapse group `desk-unlock:{id}`, preview `DESK_WORDS.settings.unlockRequest` · “Unlock it, or say no if it isn’t you.”; it is cleared when answered, when the PIN unlocks first, on pairing again or unpairing, and when the request runs out (a timer, which also sends `desk.changed`). `desk.get` shows `unlockRequest` to the paired person only, and only they answer it (`desk.answerUnlock`); an answer to one that ran out is `request_expired`. | DK-04; the notice belongs on the paired person's devices, not every owner's. |
| DK-S08 | **Superseded in part by done-today.md DN-03**: done today reads the finished tasks (`done_tasks`), not `doneRecently`. **The glance reads Home's own pieces**: `needsYou` (only approvals and questions count as the desk's Needs you; alerts, reconnects and enrollments show through the servers, the tiles and the status line), `workingTask` (newly exported from `home/overview.ts`), `doneRecently` filtered to today in the person's zone (`accounts.preferences.timeZone`, else the burrow's), the servers from the hosts service's fleet when it serves the burrow (worst first — bad, off, warn, ok — then by name; the mark from `hostOsMark`, else `server`), the tiles from the person's Home widgets, then devices, then connections with a figure (mail accounts left out: an unread count isn't a home figure), and the assistant's `doing` while it works on something. | The brief; the screen shows what Home shows, so nothing new to keep in step. Asks the screen can act on are the ones a person answers. |
| DK-S09 | **An ask on the screen**: an approval's `detail` is its explainer (else summary), `facts` its command's first line and its target, `where` the server a Servers call is about, `approveLabel` its title without the question mark and “ on {server}” when that fits 40 characters (“Restart Plex”), else “Approve”; `onDesk`/`phoneReason` from `deskMayDecide` with a connection call's deferred arguments opened for it (`callArgsOf`: sealed everywhere else, CX-21), in the glance and again before deciding. The status line's `firstAsk` is the first ask's title (Whombat keeps no shorter “what it is about”). A question's number is the screen's own, from 1 000 000 and stable while the runtime runs (`QuestionNumbers`): questions have no number and `decide` finds no approval by it (404). Every display string goes through core's `deskText` (after Markdown's marks go, `screenText`); figures are “21.4 °C”, “3.2 kW”, “54 %”. | The mockup's ask sheet; numbers that can't collide with an approval's. |
| DK-S10 | **The glance is read at most every 10 s and cut per answer.** The full glance (with every ask's detail) is cached; a `home.changed` on the person's `me` topic marks it stale; a stale one is read again in the background (one read at a time) while the screen gets the cached one, so the console's 1 s poll never waits on Home. Each answer is `trimGlance(cache, lock)`: locking or unlocking changes what shows at once without a read. After a decision on the screen it is read again before answering. | The brief's cadence; the runtime stays the authority on every answer. |
| DK-S11 | **What shows while locked** (`trimGlance`, core `deskStatusLine`): *Only the time* — “All fine.” or “Something needs you.” and the count, no servers, tiles, titles, work or done; *A summary* — the line from counts and the worst server or thing (`down` when it is off or doesn't answer), the count, servers and tiles; *Everything* — also the asks' titles (never their detail, facts or button), Working on, Done today and the assistant's own line with the first ask as its muted second part. Unlocked: everything with the detail. The latest talk only while unlocked. | DK-03. |
| DK-S12 | **The power as a UPS: `HostPower`** in the managed runtime (whether or not the box has a desk), every minute on the housekeeping tick, reading `HostNow.power` (range `hour` from now: no points). Per burrow it keeps what the owners last heard in `burrows.host_power_alert` (0 nothing, 1 out, 2 low): *out* after a minute on battery (by `since`, else from when this process first saw it), *low* at ≤ 15 % after *out*, *back* on mains after either — so a box that shut itself down at 10 % and starts on mains still says the power is back. Each goes to the burrow's owners and admins (`overseersIn`) in their assistant's DM, each account once per check. A manager that doesn't answer is left alone for 5 minutes. | DK-10, as the disk alerts do it (BS-09); the level in the database because the interesting restart is the one after the battery ran down. |
| DK-S13 | **The power line in `DeskState`** is the latest reading, read again in the background when older than 10 s, so the screen never waits on the manager; `hasBattery` in `desk.get` is a fresh reading with power. | The console answers within 1 s or shows nothing. |
| DK-S14 | **`hasDisplay`**: a `/sys/class/drm/card*-*/status` that reads `connected`, read at most once a minute; a box whose sysfs the runtime can't read has none. | The brief. The runtime's sandbox must let it read `/sys/class/drm` (for K). |
| DK-S15 | **Superseded by one-person.md OP-S08**: nobody leaves. **A person who leaves the burrow takes the pairing with them** (`onPersonRemoved`: the row is deleted in a pipeline transaction, no event), and a paired person without their assistant reads as unpaired on the screen. | The screen must never show someone who isn't there any more, or act as them. |
| DK-S16 | **Talk is T's** (`talk.ts`): `createDeskTalk(services, host: DeskTalkHost, options?) → {start, get, audio, latest, forget, close}`, refusals as `DeskTalkError` (`code`, `status`), and `voiceHealth(services)` (`voice.ts`, also re-exported by `talk.ts`). The core's host gives `place()` (the paired burrow and person, null when unpaired; from memory after the first read, since only this service changes the row and the notifications ask it for every reply in a desk thread, DK-T13), `unlocked()` (memory), `voiceOn()` (the person's Talk switch) and `deskServerByName(name)` (the glance's servers, case-insensitive). The core checks the lock before every talk call (409 `locked`), keeps the screen unlocked on a talk, passes the WAV as bytes (decoded from the request's base64), shows `latest()` only while unlocked, calls `forget()` on pairing again, unpairing and the person leaving, and `close()` on shutdown; `voiceHealth` feeds `hasMicrophone`, `hasSpeaker`, `voiceAvailable` (`stt` and `tts`) and the state's `voice` (the switch, `stt` and a microphone). | One seam: T's files take the place of the stubs and nothing else changes. |
| DK-S17 | **Superseded in part by one-person.md OP-S79**: the desk's errors hold without `not_owner` and `not_paired_person`. **`desk.*` errors** are `DESK_ERROR_STATUS`'s generic codes with the desk's code in `details.code` and `DESK_WORDS.errors`' words: `wrong_code` (with `triesLeft`), `pairing_expired`, `not_paired`, `not_owner`, `not_paired_person` (the PIN, settings and phone answer of someone else's screen), `request_expired`. An owner without an assistant here gets a plain `conflict` (“You need your assistant on this Whombat first.”). `desk.pair` pairs to the calling owner's own person (the principal id `bootstrap.me.id` carries) in their oldest burrow where they have an assistant, checked before the letters so a refusal doesn't use them up; `desk.unpair` signs as the owner's person in the desk's burrow, else their oldest. `desk.changed` goes as a bus frame on `me:{person}` to every owner's person in every burrow and the paired person (and the one paired before). | The contracts' codes (CT-525); `no_assistant` isn't one, so it stays generic. |
| DK-S18 | **The socket**: 0660, the console's group (set by the setgid directory, else here when this user is in it), `LocalError` `{error: {code, message}}` with the contracts' status codes, the contracts' strict request schemas, bodies at most `DESK_REQUEST_MAX_BYTES` (talk: `DESK_TALK_REQUEST_MAX_BYTES`); a larger body is read to its end and refused 413, past four times the limit the connection is cut. Outside production a `DeskState` that fails `deskStateValid` is an error here, not an answer. Talk's `get` and `audio` answer only while unlocked. | As the admin socket (MR-15); a refused body still gets its answer. |

A question's number has *Not now* too (its message's ref); *Approve* on it is 404. | A real decline the agent acts on was never what “Not now” says, and Home already means a snooze by it. |

| DK-S21 | **A tap on the unlock push opens This Whombat**: `push.resolve` asks registered resolvers before the inbox (`registerPushResolver`); the desk's matches the keyed ref of `desk-unlock:{id}` against the unlock pushes it sent that account within a push's life (`PUSH_TTL_S`, an hour, memory only) and answers the desk's burrow with an external link to `{app origin}/settings?tab=appliance`; any other account gets the usual `not_found`. | The web had nowhere to go for a `security` push; the phone's fallback is the same page. |
| DK-S22 | **A PIN unlocks only the pairing and PIN it was checked against** (review F2, 2026-10-20). PIN tries, the phone unlock and its answer, decisions, pairing, a new PIN, unpairing and a person leaving all run one at a time in the service's queue, so pairing again waits for a PIN being checked and then starts locked. After the scrypt check the row is read again: when its person, `paired_at` or PIN hash changed meanwhile (a change from outside the queue), the PIN is checked again against the new one, at most three times, else the screen stays locked (409 `locked`). | Before, only PIN tries and decisions were queued: a re-pairing that landed during the ~50 ms check let the earlier person's right PIN unlock the new pairing. The row's identity, not a counter in memory, so a change made anywhere is caught. |
| DK-S23 | **A power alert that reached nobody because posting failed is due again at the next check** (review F13). The level is still kept before posting (one check at a time per burrow); when every post for that burrow failed, or finding its owners did, the level goes back to what it was (only if nothing moved it since) and the next minute's check posts it again. An account already told in this check counts as reached. Nobody to tell (no owner with an assistant here) stays told, as before. `HostPower` takes the post function (tests make it fail). | An outage message lost to one failed transaction was never sent; retrying when someone did hear it would say it twice. |
| DK-S24 | **An ask's optional words with nothing to show are left out, never sent empty** (review F5): `detail` (a one-line question has none; an approval without explainer, summary or body neither), `where`, `approveLabel` and `facts` (an empty list too) go only when there is something; the status line falls back to “All fine.”, the person's initial is at most two code points. The ask builders are pure (`deskApprovalAsk`, `deskQuestionAsk`) and unit-tested through `DeskState` and `deskStateValid`. | The contract's display strings have at least one character: one empty `detail` failed the whole `DeskState`, and the console showed no desk while the screen was unlocked with such a question waiting. |
| DK-S25 | **The desk reads the command a Servers call runs** (review F1, contracts 0.100.1 CT-535). Every path that decides whether the screen may answer — the glance's `onDesk`/`phoneReason`, *Approve* and *Not now* — passes `deskMayDecide` the deferred call's opened arguments (`callArgsOf`, with `server_command`'s `command`), checked before either decision. Core keeps a command that deletes on the phone (`rm -rf /srv/backups`: `delete`; its own stricter list, then the guard's reading on that server), so its *Not now* is refused too; `docker restart plex` is answerable. A `server_command` whose arguments can't be opened stays on the phone as `delete`. | Before, a Servers call was judged by its tool only: the screen could approve `rm -rf` on a server. |
| DK-S26 | **`desk.get` says whose screen it is: `paired.you`** (review F8, CT-536): true when the session's account is the paired person's account. | A session is an account's, with a person per burrow: from another burrow `personId` can't say it. |
| DK-S27 | **What counts as a real screen** (`hardware.ts`, DK-16): per DRM connector its `status`, its card's driver (`card<N>/device/driver`, and a `virtio*` device's under it), the card's PCI `vendor` and the `edid`'s base block (`parseEdid`: header, checksum, the PNP maker from bytes 8–9, the first 0xFC name). Virtual: a driver starting with `virtio`, `bochs`, `qxl`, `vmwgfx`, `vboxvideo`, `hyperv`, `cirrus`, `simpledrm`, `simple-framebuffer`, `efidrm`, `efi-framebuffer`, `vesadrm` or `vesa-framebuffer`; or the vendor of Red Hat's virtio/QXL, QEMU's VGA, VMware, VirtualBox or Hyper-V (`0x1af4`, `0x1b36`, `0x1234`, `0x15ad`, `0x80ee`, `0x1414`); or a `Virtual` connector; or the EDID maker `RHT`, `VMW` or `VBX`. A laptop's own panel (eDP, LVDS, DSI) on a real card is real even when its EDID can't be read. The name is kept for a virtual display too (“QEMU Monitor”). Of several, the one that says most: real, then a monitor before the panel, then named. Read once a minute; *on* in Settings reads it afresh. | Each sign alone is enough, so a hypervisor that hides one still reads virtual. DSI panels often expose no EDID and a VM never has such a connector, so DK-16's EDID rule would miss real tablets for nothing. The VM's own name explains Settings' “looks like a virtual machine’s screen”. |
| DK-S28 | **The switch is `desk_display.screen_on`** (null or no row: nobody chose). `on` = what an owner chose, else paired or a real screen connected (`screenOn`): a screen that paired itself keeps reading on while its monitor is off or unplugged, and nothing unpairs on a disconnect. Pairing by the letters is an owner choosing on (`pair_desk` writes it; 0123 counts a paired box as on). `set_desk_screen` (owners, replacing `unpair_desk`) writes the choice in the same transaction: *off* deletes the pairing; *on* pairs the owner (`paired_via` `switch`, no PIN) when nobody is paired and keeps a pairing there (*Pair to me instead* stays the letters'). One event, `desk.screen_set` {on, personId, unpairedPersonId?}, “Turned on/off this Whombat’s screen”. *On* without a connected screen is `no_screen`; an owner without an assistant here gets DK-S17's conflict. | A DisplayPort monitor asleep reads `disconnected`: unpairing then would end the pairing every night. A second owner's *on* that took the screen over would bypass the letters. |
| DK-S29 | **Superseded in part by one-person.md OP-S77**: pairing itself is still signed by the burrow's Servers signer, for the person, when nobody is paired and the person didn't choose off. **Pairing itself is signed by Whombat**: the burrow's Servers signer (`serversPrincipal`, “Whombat”, `system` `servers`) commits `pair_desk_itself` {personId} in the person's burrow; only that signer may. The checks in the transaction let an owner's *off* that lands while the check runs win. |

Then the person's assistant posts `settings.selfPaired` in their DM (a background job after the commit: a post that fails isn't tried again, the pairing stands). | The console answers within a second; the claim → “Claimed!” → glance needs no one to open Settings. |
| DK-S31 | **The panel is read with the power** (`HostPower`, `box.ts`): each reading keeps `now` and `disks.data` too; while the screen asked for the panel within a minute, the reading asks for points — the whole hour first (and after an hour away), then `since` the end of the last point it has — and `HostSeriesWindow` keeps the last hour (gaps stay null). | DK-S34 | **A PIN of 4 to 6 digits** (`pin.ts` `newPinRefusal`, core `deskPinTooEasy`) is checked before it is hashed: in `desk.pair` before the letters are checked, so an easy PIN doesn't use them up, in `desk.setPin` after the paired-person check. | DK-S35 | **The lock's camera rules** (`lock.ts`, DK-28 made exact). Before the first face since this unlock, and whenever the latest answer isn't `watching`: DK-04's rule, the last face counted as a touch (2 minutes after the later). Once a face was seen and the camera watches: 30 s after the later of the last face and the last touch. A face counts from `seenAt` (else the latest frame's time when `face`), only from frames after the unlock and before its 4 hours, never later than now. ## For the appliance (K) and the apps

- The calm glance (DK-S120…DK-S131, contracts 3.2.0): `glance.calm` is made for the lock (`everything` unlocked, else
  the person's locked view; in *Only the time* only the sentence, the clock and the bar choices). `digest` comes once
  per return with its own id, for a minute or until a touch after a state first carried it: show each id once.
  `sample` is there while Settings tries a scenario (until `until`); any `POST /v1/desk/touch` (locked too) ends it and
  a digest that showed, so send a touch when the person touches the banner. `name` is in the state, paired or not,
  once the app gave it; the state has no org's name before that.
- The runtime needs `WHOMBAT_DESK_SOCKET=/run/whombat/console/desk.sock`; it makes the socket 0660 in the console's
  setgid directory (2750 `whombat:whombat-console`). The console server must be in `whombat-console` and connect with
  write permission; the runtime's user must be in it too (as for the console file).
- The runtime reads `/sys/class/drm/card*-*/{status,edid}`, `card*/device/{driver,vendor}` and
  `card*/device/virtio*/driver` (DK-S27): its sandbox must not hide `/sys/class/drm`, and the links under it must
  resolve (`/sys/devices`, `/sys/bus`). Unreadable, the box reads as having no screen and never pairs itself.
- `HostNow.power` is read from the manager's `POST /v1/host/metrics` with range `hour` and `since` = now, every minute
  and every 10 s while the screen is shown; while the screen shows *This Whombat* the same call asks for the hour's
  points once, then `since` the last point (DK-S31), and reads `cpuTempC` and `uptimeS` when the manager sends them.
- A screen that paired itself or was turned on has no PIN (`paired.hasPin` false): the console offers only *Confirm on
  your phone*, and a PIN sent anyway is 409 `no_pin`.
- `DeskServer.mark` is `server` when `hostOsMark` knows no mark: draw a generic server.
- The presence service (DK-S36, DK-S37): the runtime needs `WHOMBAT_PRESENCE_SOCKET=/run/whombat-presence/presence.sock`
  and must be able to connect (the socket 0660 `whombat-presence:whombat`). It asks `GET /v1/presence/health` at most
  once a minute (15 s after a failure), `POST /v1/presence/watch` every 2 s only while the screen is unlocked with the
  switch on, and `POST /v1/presence/stop` at every lock (since DK-S72 also for the clock, below). A watch is answered from the latest frame within 1.5 s, else it
  counts as no answer. `frameAt` matters: a `watching` whose frame is more than 5 s old counts as not watching, and a
  `seenAt` from before the unlock doesn't count.
- The voice service (DK-S52): `GET /v1/voice/health` lists `voices`, its default first; `POST /v1/voice/tts` gets
  `voice` only when the last health listed it, so a service without `voices` never sees one.
- The app's *Listen* (DK-S54): `open{stream: <a new odd id>, spec: {kind: 'export', op: 'desk.voiceSample', input:
  {voice}}}` on an enrolled session → `opened{meta: {kind: 'export', contentType: 'application/json', size}}`, the bytes
  in DATA frames (give CREDIT), `stream_done{size, sha256}` and END; the bytes are UTF-8 JSON `{"audioWav": "<base64
  WAV>"}` (`DeskVoiceSample`, a WAV of at most 1 MiB: mono 16-bit at the voice's rate, 24 kHz for Kokoro). Refused:
  `error{code: 'stream_refused', details: {code: 'no_voice' | 'not_paired' | 'not_paired_person'}}` (a voice outside
  `DESK_VOICES`: `stream_refused` with `bad_request`). A `request` for it answers `bad_request` with
  `details.code: 'use_export_stream'`. Offer *Listen* only where `DeskScreen.voices` is present.
- The console: `paired.pinDigits` comes only with a PIN (absent: 6); `lock.camera` and `lock.present` come only as
  `true` and only while unlocked, `present` only with `camera`.
- The boot id (DK-S73): the runtime's sandbox (`ProcSubset=pid`) hides `/proc/sys/kernel/random/boot_id`, so
  `whombat-server` needs `WHOMBAT_BOOT_ID=%b` in its environment (systemd expands `%b` to the boot id), else an unlock
  that stays never outlives an update.
- The presence service (DK-S70, DK-S72): with *Use the camera to see when you’re here* on, the runtime also watches every
  2 s while the screen is locked, by day on mains power while the console asks for the state; it sends `stop` when
  nothing wants the camera (quiet hours, battery, the console silent for 10 s, the switch off), no longer at every lock
  while the clock still wants it. The camera's light can be on all day then (DK-72).
- The console (DK-S71, DK-S72): `lock.stays` comes only with `unlocked`, its `until` 2 minutes after the state's `now`;
  `presence` comes only while the camera looks for the clock and only while paired. `here` is a face within a minute;
  when the picture goes dark `watching` turns false at once while `here` lasts until a minute after the last face. A
  console that wants DK-71's 10 minutes to run from the last face too (not only the last touch) can count the last
  `here` as a touch. The camera stays on only while the console asks at least every 10 s.
- The servers (DK-S82…DK-S90, contracts 0.115.0): `POST /v1/desk/servers` `{v: 1}` → `DeskServersAnswer`; `POST
  /v1/desk/server` `{v: 1, id, range}` → `DeskServerPage` (about 2–3 KB for a full Proxmox node). Refusals: 409
  `not_paired`, 409 `locked` (locked in *Only the time*), 404 `not_found` (not a server of the fleet, or one still
  waiting to be let in), 503 `unavailable` (the hosts service didn't answer: keep the last page), 400 `bad_request`. Each
  hosts read gets 5 s, so an answer comes within about 5 s at worst, usually in one round trip to the hub. Reading
  isn't a touch: send touches as on the glance. `disk.used` may come without its unit (“12.5” of “24 TB”).
  `DeskTalkRequest.server` with an id the runtime doesn't know is ignored (the talk goes on); an older runtime refuses
  the member (400). A card without `id` (a server whose id isn't a `DeskServerId`) doesn't open.
