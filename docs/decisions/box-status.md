# How the box is doing

John, 2026-10-11: “I would like to see how the appliance is doing. I would like to see: CPU usage and the history, disk
space usage, network utilization, so bandwidth, how many data has been sent and received today in megabytes/gigabytes,
perhaps a complete overview with token usage even. If you hear this, how would you implement this and where would you
show this in the UI?” After the proposal and its mockup (a new first section of Settings → This Whombat): “Yes please”.

Before this, nothing on the appliance kept CPU or network history: the manager computed `/persist` free space (statfs)
for control's `status.report` only, the runner read `/proc/meminfo` and PSI for turn admission only, and tokens were kept
per turn (`dispatches.usage`) and summed for today, 7 and 30 days (`spend.overview`) without a series. The server is
sandboxed with `ProtectProc=invisible` + `ProcSubset=pid` (it can't read `/proc/stat`); the manager sees all of `/proc`,
already runs periodic loops, survives runtime restarts and updates, and already answers the runtime on its socket.

Contracts 0.78.0 (CT-334): `HostMetrics` and the session op `host.metrics` (`@whombat/protocol/session`), the manager's
`POST /v1/host/metrics` (`@whombat/control-contracts/local`), `spend.daily`, and the copy in `@whombat/core/text`
(`text/host.ts`). Appliance: the manager's sampler (`docs/decisions` of the appliance). App: whombat-app
`docs/decisions/box-status.md`.

## Decisions

| ID | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BS-01 | **Superseded in part by one-person.md OP-S06, OP-S07**: the section stays, and it is the person's: there are no owners or members. **Settings → This Whombat gets a first section, “How it’s doing”, for owners only** (feature `host.metrics`). No new tab, no sidebar entry. Members don't see it. On a self-hosted server (no manager) it shows only the Tokens card. | It's about the box itself, which is what This Whombat is; Settings went from 13 tabs to 7 and the sidebar was cleared on purpose (SS-01). |
| BS-02 | **Superseded in part by one-person.md OP-04**: the manager measures the same, without a computers' share of memory. **The manager measures, every 10 s:** processor (`/proc/stat`, busy = everything but idle and iowait, all cores together), memory (`MemTotal − MemAvailable`; the computers' share from their cgroups), network bytes on the **uplink** (the interfaces of the IPv4 default route) and on the computers' bridge (`wbc…`), and the disks by `statfs` (`/persist`, `/`, the backup disk when mounted), with `/persist` split by the btrfs quota groups (computers 1/101; work folders = the workspaces subvolume; database = the postgres subvolume; the Whombat's own data = the rest of 1/100). | The manager can read all of it without new privileges; the server is the exposed process and stays without `/proc`. |
| BS-03 | **History lives in the manager:** 10 s points for the last hour in memory, 1-minute buckets for 48 h and 15-minute buckets for 35 days on disk under `/persist/manager/metrics`, written in place (fixed-size records, a few KB a minute, no whole-file rewrites). Nothing goes into Postgres or through the commit pipeline. | Measurements aren't burrow data; they must survive a runtime update and cost nothing when nobody looks. |
| BS-04 | **Four ranges:** Last hour (10 s × 360), 24 hours (5 min × 288), 7 days (30 min × 336), 30 days (2 h × 360). A point averages processor and memory, keeps the busiest sample (`cpuPeakPermille`) and sums the bytes; only finished steps; null where nothing was measured. | Enough points for a smooth chart at every width; peaks don't get averaged away at 30 days. |
| BS-05 | **“Today” and Data per day count calendar days in the burrow's time zone** (the zone `spend.overview` counts “today” in), from the 15-minute buckets: 30 days, today last. Kernel counters restart at boot; the manager keeps its own totals and treats a counter that goes backwards as starting from zero. | The same “today” as the tokens; correct across reboots and 30/45-minute offset zones. |
| BS-06 | **Superseded in part by one-person.md OP-S06**: the op stays, for the person's devices; there is nobody else to refuse. **`host.metrics` is a session op** (owners; others get `forbidden`): the runtime asks the manager (`POST /v1/host/metrics`, 2 s timeout) with the burrow's time zone and passes `since` through; `reason: not_managed` without a manager, `unavailable` when it doesn't answer or is older (404). | One place decides who may see it; older managers degrade to a sentence, not an error. |
| BS-07 | **Superseded in part by one-person.md OP-S73**: turns and tokens per day stay, over every turn (no scope between people). **`spend.daily`**: turns and tokens per day for 30 days from `dispatches.usage` by `finished_at` in the burrow's zone, every day present, the scope rule of `spend.overview`. | The Tokens card's columns; the per-agent split of today comes from `spend.overview`. |
| BS-08 | **Live while open:** the app polls `host.metrics` every 10 s with `since` (only new points) while the section is on screen and the window visible; a range change fetches the whole range; `spend.daily` and `spend.overview` load on open and every 5 minutes. Nothing runs when the section is closed. | A live view without a push stream; a poll is a few hundred bytes. |
| BS-09 | **Superseded in part by one-person.md OP-S74**: the disk alerts stay, in the person's Chat. **Disk alerts to owners**, through the owners' Assistant DMs like the AI gateway's alerts (AIG-14): at 85 % and at 95 % of the data disk used (`hostDiskAlertDue`: once per level, a level re-arms 5 points below it), checked every 10 minutes by the server; the words are `hostDiskAlertText` (“The Whombat’s disk is 86 % full, with 12.4 GB left. Most space goes to agents’ computers (22.9 GB).”). No processor or network alerts. | A full disk stops agents; short processor spikes are normal while agents work. |
| BS-10 | **Superseded in part by one-person.md OP-04**: the section stays, without the computers' share of memory. **The section** follows the mockup: four tiles (Processor now with the range's peak and a sparkline; Memory used of total with a meter and the computers' share; Disk used of total with a meter and what's free; Today received with sent and a sparkline), then Processor (area chart), Network speed (download and upload in Mbit/s), Data per day (14 days, received and sent stacked, the computers' share in the tooltip), Disk (a stacked bar of the parts, Other and Free, with sizes; the system disk and the backup disk as lines) and Tokens (today's total and cache share, 14 days of columns, today's agents with their pictures, “More in AI” → Settings → AI → Spend). The range control scopes the Processor and Network speed charts and the peak; the daily charts always show 14 days. | John approved this layout. |
| BS-11 | **Charts are the app's own SVG**, no chart library: 2 px lines, a 10 % area wash (16 % dark), columns with 4 px rounded tops and a 2 px gap between stacked parts, hairline grid, a crosshair with one tooltip for every series, the latest point on keyboard focus, and a “Show as a table” switch per chart. Colours are the tokens `chart1`–`chart3` (blue, orange, green; validated for colour blindness in both themes). | Small and consistent with the run bars; accessible without hovering. |
| BS-12 | **Android** has the same section on its This Whombat screen: the four tiles in a 2 × 2 grid, then one chart at a time with the range control and a chart switcher (Processor, Network, Data per day, Disk, Tokens), polling only while the screen is visible. | Phone width; the same data and words as the web. |

## Details

| ID | Detail | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BS-D01 | `host.metrics` and `spend.daily` are advertised as one feature, `host.metrics` (contracts 0.78). A managed runtime advertises it always (an older manager answers `unavailable`); the self-hosted server's REST `spend.daily` exists regardless. | One switch for the section; the box parts degrade by `reason`. |
| BS-D02 | The disk alert's kept level (0, 85 or 95) is stored with the burrow through the commit pipeline (a system command, no message when it only changes the level), so a restart doesn't repeat an alert. The check reads `host.metrics` with range `hour` and `since` = now (no points, just `disks`). | Alerts once per crossing, across restarts. |
| BS-D03 | The zone of `host.metrics` (the op names no burrow): the asking owner's oldest burrow on this Whombat, else its oldest burrow, else UTC. The disk check asks in UTC (it reads only `disks`). A self-hosted server has no session ops, so it has no `host.metrics` to answer; the app reads `not_managed` or the op's absence the same way, and `spend.daily` is a REST route in both modes. | The same “today” as the Tokens card in the usual case of one burrow, without a burrow parameter the contract doesn't have. |
| BS-D04 | The manager call is a `ManagerPort` method (`hostMetrics`, like the runtime's other manager calls, so tests use the in-memory fake): the runtime caps the whole call at 2 s (not only the socket's idle time), takes answers up to 256 KiB (a month's 360 points and the days are about 70 KB) and parses them with the contract. A 404, a timeout, no socket or an answer outside the contract is `unavailable`, logged at warn at most once a minute (“this manager doesn’t measure the box yet” for a 404). | A live view polls every 10 s; a missing manager must not fill the journal or fail the section. |
| BS-D05 | `spend.daily`'s days begin where core's `dayKey` days begin: the server walks back from today's `zonedDayBounds` (the bounds `spend.overview` uses) one day at a time and buckets each turn's `finished_at` among those first instants with `width_bucket`, in one grouped query over `(burrow_id, finished_at)`; not Postgres's `at time zone`. The turn rule (finished and ran) and the cache clamp are `spend.overview`'s. The 30 days are calendar days, so the oldest can hold turns `spend.overview`'s rolling 30 days no longer counts. | Today in the Tokens card is exactly `spend.overview`'s today and the app's `dayKey`, whatever Postgres's time zone data says; 23-, 25-hour and 45-minute-offset days come out right. |
| BS-D06 | The disk alert's level lives in `burrows.host_disk_alert` (migration 0102, expand: integer, default 0), written in the burrow's transaction through the commit pipeline without an event. It is kept before the alerts are posted (an alert that fails to post isn't repeated, as AIG-14's). Recipients are the burrow's owners and the Whombat's admins in it (`overseersIn`, AIG-14's) with an assistant DM; an account in several burrows on the box hears it once per check, in the oldest. The check runs in managed mode only, from the server's start outside read-only (first at the next housekeeping tick, then every 10 minutes), and skips while drained or quarantined. | Once per crossing for each person, across restarts and updates. |
