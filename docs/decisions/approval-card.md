# One approval card, and the Inbox's one way forward (ACD)

From John's accepted final design (`docs/design-handoff/council-ui-2026-11/16-final/approval/` and `inbox/`), built into
the web (desktop and phone width) and the phone app (iOS and Android). Ids ACD-01…ACD-20.

Supersedes ui-refresh-inbox.md UIR-38's last two sentences (the details' own “Yes, do it · No” and “the yes is the one
filled button”), UIR-30's line limit on a row's description, UIR-37's “Settled · 3” when nothing is settled, inbox.md
IBX-01's Settle beside the way forward on a line that needs the person, and app phone-refresh-asks.md AP-7402's filled
“Yes, do it”. It extends app duo-polish.md AP-6553 (no box for the person's own yes) to the web. Not in: a recommended
answer, a consequence line or an undo promise on an approval (UIR-39, UIR-40 stay), a raised text cap.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ACD-01 | **One approval card everywhere an everyday ask appears**: the thread's card, the approval's dialog (`?approval=`), its Inbox row and the phone's approval screen draw it from the same pieces in the same order — “{assistant} needs your OK” with when it asked, the title, what happens to what (its `whatHappens`; an Inbox row its subject and body), *Permission used*, *Still waiting for* (conditions not met), the box “**Yes, tonight** means Thursday, 24 September 2026 at 03:00 · Europe/Amsterdam.” when a later time is offered, a *Details* fold, then the answers. Core `approvalCardFacts` and `APPROVAL_CARD_WORDS`, client `needsYouApprovalCard`; web `AskCard`, phone `ApprovalScreen` / `approvalFacts`. | The person reads what happens, under which permission and when, before any yes, in the same words in every place. |
| ACD-02 | **The permission is the approval's key**, its catalogue label in quotes (`approvalPermissionText(keyId)`). An Inbox item carries no key, so its card says *Not supplied in this request*; nothing borrows another approval's key and no contract field is added. | Only what the request supplies. |
| ACD-03 | **The exact moment *Yes, tonight* means** is spelled out with weekday, date, year, clock and zone (`exactMomentText`): on a card from the Whombat's quiet-hours start for changes (`nextAt`, as the button runs it), in the Inbox from the item's own `approve_later.at`. It shows only while the answers do. | “Tonight” alone left the person guessing which night and whose clock. |
| ACD-04 | **The answers have equal weight and one order**: *Yes, now · Yes, tonight · No* (*Yes, do it · No* without a later time) on the card, in the dialog, in the Inbox and on the phone, all outlined alike. A command keeps *Allow once · Allow for this task · Always allow for {assistant} · Don’t allow* and a deploy *Approve · (Tonight) · Always allow … · Decline* in its card's order in Details too, also all outlined. | No answer looks recommended, and no place reorders them. |
| ACD-05 | **The approval's dialog offers *Yes, tonight* for every everyday ask**, as the Inbox already does; the thread's card keeps offering it only in a chat with the assistant (AP-1280). | The dialog has the same answers as the card the person came from, and the Whombat accepts a later time for any approval. |
| ACD-06 | **No box for the person's own yes**: the “A person says yes · Not yet · you” checklist is gone wherever the person's yes is the only condition (web card and dialog; the phone already had none, AP-6553). A deploy keeps its checks and review rows, including who approved. | The question and its answers already ask the person. |
| ACD-07 | **Details holds the technical rest**: the target, the call and what it sends, a message's whole body, the explainer and the lasting yes behind its own confirmation (UIR-41 stays). The dialog opens Details when there is a message to read; on the card it starts folded. The Inbox's Details holds *Open approval*. | The facts the person decides by stay short; the rest is one tap away. |
| ACD-08 | **A long command or email never sits under the answers**: in the dialog and on the phone the facts and Details scroll in one region (“Approval content”) above a separate answer bar (“Approval answers”); below 650px of height, everything scrolls as one with the answers last. No line clamp or fixed-height payload. | Nothing the person decides by is hidden behind a button. |
| ACD-09 | **An answer whose outcome is unknown is never resent**: when deciding fails with an unknown outcome (`phaseForError` = `unknown`: the connection dropped after it left, or `unknown_outcome`), the answers give way to “Your answer may have gone through.” · “Whombat didn’t get a reply. Check what happened before answering again.” · *Last reviewed details*, and one button, *Ask {assistant} to check*, which starts a thread asking whether it went through and not to send or run it again. On the web every place showing that approval knows it (a store for the page's life, `unknownAnswer.tsx`); it ends once the approval is no longer pending. In `warn-text`, not red. | command-safety.md: a resend of a delivered answer can act twice. |
| ACD-10 | **The approval's read states**: *Loading the approval…* with skeletons; *No approval to show.* · “Open a request from Chat or the Inbox.” · *Open Inbox* when it isn't there; *Couldn’t read the approval.* · “There’s no saved copy to show on this device yet.” · *Try again* when the first read fails; *Couldn’t refresh the approval.* · “Showing the saved details already on this device.” above the saved card, without answers until a read succeeds. Try again only reads. | A failed read is never shown as nothing to do, and an answer is given only on current facts. |
| ACD-11 | **Answered, the card keeps its state** (UIR-42): its answers give way to where it is (“Scheduled for tonight, 03:00”, “You said no · 09:46”). | Unchanged from UIR-42. |
| ACD-12 | **An Inbox line that needs the person shows one way forward**: its first button (*Ask {assistant} to handle it*, the step the assistant found, *See it*), and *Settle* with the rest in its More (`needsYouButtons`, the `more` flag). The phone's swipe still settles. Asks (approvals, questions, skills) and suggestions keep their own answers. The broken item still comes first (UIR-33). | One clear next step on what is broken; the rest stays a tap away. |
| ACD-13 | **An approval in the Inbox is the approval card** (ACD-01) across the row, its More (talk it over, remind) under it. Its body shows whole, at every width and the largest text; no row's description is clamped any more. | All its facts and the exact time before its Yes buttons, never clipped. |
| ACD-14 | **No zero counts**: an empty Settled is just “Settled”, a group heading shows its count only when it has rows, and no badge says 0. | A zero is noise. |
| ACD-15 | **The Inbox's read states**: *Loading the Inbox…* with skeletons; when nothing is in it, “All good. Nothing needs you.” · “New questions and things that need you appear here.” · *Back to Chat*; a first read that fails says *Couldn’t read the Inbox.* · “There’s no saved copy to show on this device yet.” · *Try again* · *Ask {assistant}* (a thread asking why it couldn't be read) · *More detail* (the error); a refresh that fails says *Couldn’t refresh the Inbox.* · “Showing the saved details already on this device.” above the saved list, kept in its order. In `warn-text`. | A failed read never looks like an empty Inbox or a new broken thing. |
