# Weather and local news (WN)

John, 2026-11-26: “I realized that we need to improve more capabilities in Whombat by default, for example weather
information which will be useful for Dusty in both dashboard view (morning page for example), but also for Dusty's "Look
ahead" feature. Perhaps we should also add local news (country / state)? I realize that this will become a bigger task,
but I am sure it's worth it.”

The services were chosen by their terms (checked 2026-11-26): MET Norway's forecast is open data (NLOD 2.0, CC BY 4.0)
that a sold product may use with its credit; Nominatim may be used within its policy, which counts every Whombat
together as one application; most broadcasters offer their RSS for personal, non-commercial use, so Whombat reads news
the way a feed reader does, only from sites the person turned on.

| Id | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WN-01 | **Where this Whombat is** (`Place`, `Burrow.place`): a name, a latitude and longitude kept to two decimals, a country (ISO 3166-1), and where Nominatim gives them its region (ISO 3166-2 level 4), area (level 6) and the region's name. The place the person chose (`burrows.place`), else the one its time zone suggests (`placeFromTimeZone`: the zone's city in `zone.tab`'s country, `guessed`), else none (`UTC`, a standard time's name). `zone.tab`, not `zone1970.tab`, which merges the Netherlands into Brussels. | It works the day a Whombat is set up, without asking anyone; two decimals (about 1 km) are enough for a forecast and no closer to the house. |
| WN-02 | **Looking a place up** (`place.find`): OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, a search by words or a reverse by the device's location rounded to two decimals, `format=jsonv2` with `addressdetails`; up to five places. One request a second per box (a look-up waits up to 5 s for its turn, else “Many look-ups at once”), each answer kept a day, only when someone asks. Settings shows “© OpenStreetMap” by the results. `place.set` keeps one, or null goes back to the guess; signed as `burrow.updated` with the place's name, never its coordinates. | Nominatim's policy: an identifying User-Agent, at most one request a second for the whole application, no background look-ups, credit (ODbL). |
| WN-03 | **The forecast is MET Norway's** Locationforecast 2.0 `complete`, one kept per place in memory. A place is asked again only after MET's `Expires`, never within 30 minutes, 2 to 8 minutes later at random and never on the hour; `If-Modified-Since` with the kept `Last-Modified`, and a 304 keeps it. Asked only while something needs it (a shown widget, the assistant, Looks ahead); a failed read keeps the last forecast and tries again after 10 minutes. Every place shown with MET's data says “Data from MET Norway”; no Yr name or logo. | MET's terms: an identifying User-Agent with a real contact, at most four decimals, respect `Expires`, no requests on the hour, credit, and nothing that looks like Yr. |
| WN-04 | **Sunrise and sunset are worked out on the box** (`sunTimes`, NOAA's equations, about a minute), polar day and night included. **Units**: Celsius and metres per second, Fahrenheit and miles per hour in the United States; rain in millimetres everywhere; no setting. Symbols are MET's `symbol_code`, drawn by the screen. | No second request for what a formula gives; one rule instead of a setting nobody asked for. |
| WN-05 | **News is a feed reader**: Whombat suggests sites for the place's country and region (`NEWS_SUGGESTIONS`), and reads nothing until the person turns a site on or adds a source of their own (an https address: a feed as it is, a page by the `<link rel="alternate">` feed it names, or one of the kinds in WN-14, through up to three redirects while adding; the feed's final address is kept). Up to 30 sources (WN-17). Only the headline, its site, its time and its link: no pictures, no article text. The assistant may turn sites on or off as the person asks; adding a site asks first (its words reach the assistant every 15 minutes, and an address stays the person's to give). | Most broadcasters' feeds are for personal use: the person choosing the sites, as in any feed reader, is the closest to that. |
| WN-06 | **Reading a feed**: through the outbound rules (https only, public addresses, no redirects, 10 s), with Whombat's User-Agent, kept 15 minutes per feed; a feed is read up to its first MiB, and when it is longer (a podcast's often is) the item the cut fell in is left out. RSS 2.0, RSS 1.0 (RDF) and Atom (htmlparser2, XML mode); headlines without markup, and a post without a title (Mastodon, Bluesky) headlined by its first 200 characters; an item without a date or a link is left out; a time ahead of now is now; at most 30 per feed; all sites together newest first, each once by link and by the same words. A site that can't be read keeps its headlines and says why (“The site moved its feed”, “Couldn’t read it just now”), and is tried again after 5 minutes. | Some feeds list sections among their articles, or write local time as UTC; a moved feed must not lead the box somewhere new by itself. |
| WN-07 | **The suggestions** come from `scripts/weather-news/news-feeds.psv`, each row a feed checked to answer over https with recent items (423 in 35 countries, the last checked on 2026-11-27): the “all news” feeds of a country's broadcasters and big outlets, a few of their topic feeds (`topic`: tech, business, sport, science or culture; none means all the news), and regional ones by ISO 3166-2 level 4 (`regions`: a province, Land, county or state; several where one site serves several) or level 6 (`areas`: Belgium's provinces), each with the place's own name (`where`, “Noord-Brabant”). Per country the all-news feeds first, then the topics, then the regions. Nearest first: the area's, the region's, the country's. `scripts/weather-news/check.ts` reads a row as a Whombat does (its User-Agent, no redirect, the first MiB, RSS or Atom with recent items) and notes the day and what it found; a row keeps its id once it is in, since a burrow keeps the ids it turned on. Ireland has RTÉ News and RTÉ Regional. | Region codes are what Nominatim gives, so the right regional broadcaster comes first without a map of our own. |
| WN-08 | **Two widgets** of Whombat's own (desk-display.md DK-200, DK-201): `weather` with its own `weather` view, and `news` as a `list`. Neither is private on a locked screen. Each is read once per burrow, the views made again every few minutes from what the forecast and the feeds keep. | Weather and headlines are public; a screen full of widgets mustn't multiply requests. |
| WN-09 | **Older apps** (session minor below `WEATHER_NEWS_MINOR`) get layouts, views, *Add a widget* and a screen elsewhere's grid without the two widgets and the `weather` view; when such an app saves or tries a layout, the widgets it couldn't see go back on the same page and shape at the same place while it is free, else they are left out. | Their contract's enums are closed: one unknown widget would make the whole layout unreadable. |
| WN-10 | **Session minor 16** (`WEATHER_NEWS_MINOR`): `place.find` and `news.sources` (reads), `place.set`, `news.setSources` and `news.addSite` (changes), the burrow's `place`, and the widgets. In the assistant's settings, on This Whombat's Overview: finding and reading are reads, setting the place and turning sites on or off are changes, adding a site always asks. | “We live in Breda” works in Chat; a site of the person's own stays their yes. |
| WN-11 | **The assistant's tools** `weather_forecast` (here, or a town it names, looked up first, in that town's own hours) and `news_headlines` (the sites that are on; `query` filters here, nothing goes out): read-only, no key, served in the person's own turns (desk Talk is one), in automation steps and in Looks ahead checks; never in outsiders' turns or a look through new mail. | A morning automation may well say the weather; strangers' turns get nothing they didn't need. |
| WN-12 | **Looks ahead** (looks-ahead.md): both tools are a check's (`AHEAD_TOOL_NAMES`, the Hermes policy). The weather of another place only in a calendar check while its web is open, by public words (`aheadQueryRefusal`); here's always. A calendar check gets the forecast where the Whombat is for the plan's day when it is within the week (`AheadDispatchContext.weather`). A weather-only finding names `{ weather: true }`, which Whombat writes as “Data from MET Norway”; a headline's link may be read and named as a source. Weather findings use the kinds there are (travel, other). | The forecast is the check's own context, not a stranger's page; `AheadSource` is only the report's input, which the box serves, while `AheadKind` is read by the apps. |
| WN-13 | **Privacy**: the privacy policy (version 2026-11-27) and privacy-and-security.md name MET Norway, Nominatim, the news sites the person chooses and Apple's podcast directory among the services a Whombat talks to itself; YouTube, Reddit, a Mastodon server, Bluesky and GitHub are such news sites, asked only once the person adds one; each request names Whombat (`Whombat/<version> (+https://whombat.com)`), never the person. Tests never reach the network (`useOpenDataForTests`). | They see the box's address and what it asks for. |
| WN-14 | **Kinds of sources** (`news.addSite`, `NewsSource.kind`): a YouTube channel (`/channel/UC…` straight to `feeds/videos.xml?channel_id=`; `@handle`, `/c/` and `/user/` by the channel id in its page, read once up to 2 MiB with a cookie that skips YouTube's cookie question, a redirect to its consent page refused as unreachable; a playlist by `playlist_id`), a subreddit (`/r/x/.rss`), a Mastodon account (`/@user.rss`, else the page as it is), a Bluesky account (`/profile/x/rss`, kept at the account's lasting address Bluesky answers from), an Apple podcast (its feed from `itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=…`, read over https), and a GitHub project (`releases.atom`; GitHub's own pages are pages). Its name is the feed's own title. The kind is the address's, else a podcast when the feed has the iTunes namespace or audio enclosures, else what its feed address says (`newsSourceKind`). | People follow news there, and each has an open feed: one reader under the same outbound rules, no keys, no APIs. |
| WN-15 | **Browsing** (`news.browse`): every suggestion of one country (the place's unless another is asked), with its language, topic, whom it is for and how near it is to the place, and whether it is on; every country with suggestions, the most first. It reads only the table on the box. | The first suggestions for a place aren't all there is, and looking through them asks no one anything. |
| WN-16 | **Importing another reader's list** (`news.importSites`): the device reads the OPML file and sends its feed addresses (up to 30). Each is checked as `news.addSite` checks one, four at a time, all within 20 seconds (one not read by then is skipped as unreachable), and the ones found are added in one commit (`burrow.updated`, “Added the news from … from another reader’s list”). An address already on, or twice in the list, is passed over without a word; one that can't be added is skipped with `news.addSite`'s words; once the room is used, the rest are skipped as full without being read. The assistant asks first, as for adding one. | An app waits half a minute for an answer and a commit after that is refused; a list someone exported shouldn't cost thirty requests it can't use. |
| WN-17 | **Up to 30 sources** (`NEWS_SOURCES_MAX`), read six at a time. In the news widget and the assistant's headlines without a query, no source gives more than its share of the newest (the list's length divided by the sources, at least 3); a query looks through every source's headlines. | Thirty sources at thirty items each are 900 headlines: one busy national feed mustn't push out the local one. |
| WN-18 | **Session minor 19** (`NEWS_MORE_MINOR`): `news.browse` (a read), `news.importSites` (a change the assistant asks for), `kind`, and 30 sources. An app below it is shown the first 12 sources that are on; when it saves its list, the sources it couldn't see stay on after it unless it names them; it can add a source only while fewer than 12 are on (“Up to 12 sources”). Newer apps, the assistant and everything outside a session get all of them. | Its contract caps the list at 12, so a longer one would make the whole answer unreadable; saving from an older phone mustn't turn off sources it never showed. |

## Weighed and left

- **Open-Meteo**: its free tier is for non-commercial use only; a paid plan for a sold product.
- **Google News RSS**: its feeds are “solely for … a personal feed reader for personal, non-commercial use”.
- **Yle**: its terms rule out paid services and apps.
- **Weather warnings (MeteoAlarm)**: later. Matching a place needs the warning's polygons or EMMA ids, and its terms'
  extra rules for redistribution weren't read in full.
- **The Overview's tiles**: later; its tiles take only things and accounts.
- **BBC's English, Scottish and Welsh regional feeds as suggestions**: their regions have no ISO 3166-2 code a place
  keeps; the person can add theirs as a site of their own.
- **MET's Sunrise API**: a formula on the box gives the same to a minute.
- **States Newsroom's state sites as US suggestions**: their feeds answer an automated reader with a challenge page;
  nonprofit newsrooms of the states that answered are suggested instead.
- **Reading YouTube, Reddit or Bluesky through their APIs**: each needs a key or an app of Whombat's own; their feeds
  need neither.
- **A units setting**: metric, Fahrenheit and mph in the United States, until someone asks.
- **The place and the news sites in a backup file**: the box's own backups keep them with the database; a backup file
  doesn't yet.

MET Norway's terms ask that a responsible person read and accept them once: John accepted them for Cybex on 2026-11-26.
