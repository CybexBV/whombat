# Dashboards: the server side

Decisions the server stream made building dashboards.md (DS-01…DS-19) on contracts 0.89.0 (CT-394…CT-398). No
migration (0111 not used). Code in `apps/server/src/things/` (`series.ts`, `machine.ts`, `panels.ts`, `page.ts`,
`points.ts`, `devices.ts`, `kinds/*`) and `apps/server/src/home/health.ts`.

| Id | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| DS-S01 | **The range**: `things.page` reads `?range=` (`ThingPageQuery`); a connection's or device's page offers 1 h · 6 h · 24 h · 7 d · 30 d, a range it doesn't offer (90 d, the servers') or none is 24 h, a word that isn't a range is 400 (the contract). Every connection and device page answers `range`, `ranges` (the five) and `refreshS` 60; a device still being learned has nothing over time and no picker. | DS-03, CT-397. |
| DS-S02 | **The grid**: Whombat's points on steps of 5 min (1 h, 6 h), 15 min (24 h), 2 h (7 d), 6 h (30 d) — 12, 72, 96, 84, 120 points; the hosts service's and Hetzner's metrics on the hosts steps (1 h at 60 s). Buckets end on multiples of the step, the last one is the current, partial one ending now (the hosts service's shape), each the mean of its points. The **whole range is drawn with null gaps**, also before a new connection's history starts (CP-S03 trimmed the start; with a picker the axis must be the range chosen). | DS-11; one grid for both clients and the hosts data beside it. |
| DS-S03 | **Points kept 30 days** (`connection_points`): the hourly job (still `home.connection-points-prune`) deletes points past 30 days and rolls points older than 48 hours into one per key per **UTC** hour (`date_bin`, whatever the session's zone): their average, null when none had a value, `at` on the hour. One statement deletes an hour's rows and inserts its average, so an hour is never half rolled; an hour already rolled stays. No migration: the primary key `(connection_id, key, at)` and the `at` index serve the reads and the job. | DS-11; ~1,250 rows per key and connection at most. |
| DS-S04 | **New series from the health pass**: Home Assistant `log_err` (the running counts of its log's ERROR and CRITICAL entries, all sources: errors since it started), `log_err_top` (the noisiest integration's) with that integration on the glance (`ConnectionGlance.logTop`, not on the wire), `db_mb` (the recorder's size); UniFi `gw_cpu`, `gw_mem` (%), `clients`; and **`disk_pct`/`disk_gb` for every connection whose machine Whombat knows** (resolved as on its page, read without metrics, 5 s each at most), Home Assistant OS's Supervisor's disk when Whombat doesn't watch its machine. Personal connections (no burrow) have no machine. | DS-10, DS-11: a forecast needs the disk's history for any connection with a machine, not only Home Assistant. |
| DS-S05 | **The machine on every connection's Summary** (`machine.ts`): figures CPU (small line, blue), Memory (small line, violet), Disk (bar; “76 % · full in about 2 months” when the 30-day trend says so, else “76 % used”; amber from 75 %, red from 90 %) first, then the kind's, six at most; chart cards CPU (blue, a wash, axis from 0) and Memory (violet, a wash, axis 0 to its size) before the kind's; “Disk 80 % full” or “Disk full in about 3 weeks” (within 60 days) in Worth a look. Beside the machine's figures a generic connection shows Status (its line “1 failed check in 24 h” when there were any) and Response time; Last used and Problems go, so the row keeps six. The CPU card's foot (a guest's own processes) only where Whombat knows them: Home Assistant OS's Supervisor (Home Assistant and its three busiest add-ons). Home Assistant draws its machine itself (`ownMachine`) so its Supervisor stands in when there is no machine. | DS-10, DS-14; one module for every kind. |
| DS-S06 | **Places** (`layout`, replaces `pairWidths`): figures `top`, chart cards `chart`, Worth a look `side`, Show more here `bottom`, everything else `main` unless its builder says otherwise; panels come in a phone's order — figures, charts, Worth a look, the main column, the rest of the side column, the bottom. Chart cards are packed in rows of three: a later card fills a gap a wider one left, and the last card of a row that stays short widens to fill it (a lone Response time card spans 3; UniFi with a machine: CPU · Memory · Gateway, then Internet across). `width` stays for 0.88 clients: top, charts and bottom whole width, main wide, side narrow, paired as before (a lone one whole width). The kinds' other tabs (Integrations, Logs, Servers…) keep their panels without a place: one whole-width panel each, as before. | DS-04, CT-394: no hole beside a chart; old clients keep a sane page. |
| DS-S07 | **Errors in the log** (bars, two columns): a bar per hour for 24 h, per 2 and 6 hours for 7 and 30 days (≤ 120 bars; the card's meta says “per 2 hours”), the grid's step below a day (“per 5 minutes”). A bar is how much the running count grew: a drop to under half the count before is a restart and counts again from the new count; a smaller drop (Home Assistant's log keeps 50 entries and lets old ones go) adds nothing. The noisiest source orange (named by `logTop`), “Everything else” grey (all minus it, never below 0), their legend figures the counts since the restart (“11,280”); “Since the restart on Thu 07:12” and “**12,286** errors · about **510** an hour” (the count over the hours since the recorder's run began). | DS-10 and `ha.png`; a bar per hour over 30 days would be 720. |
| DS-S08 | **Disk space and Database cards follow the page's range** like every chart (DS-03), though `ha.png` shows 30 days: their right lines are the range (“24 h”, “7 days”) and the change over it (“+ **3.6 GB**”, “− **0.4 GB**”, “No change”). The forecast always uses up to 30 days of `disk_pct` (least squares, at least two days of history, only while growing, within a year); the dashed line is drawn only when the full date is within three ranges of now (else the axis would be mostly forecast), the foot says “Full · about 18 Dec” and the figure “full in about 2 months” either way. Axis 50…100 % (lower when the line dips under 55 %); amber from 75 %, red from 90 %, else blue. Foot: Old backups (Home Assistant's own on its disk — `backup.local`/`hassio.local`, every backup before 2025.1 — except the newest), Database, Full. Database: GB from 1,000 MB, “recorder”; foot Engine (“MariaDB 10.11”) and Grows (“110 MB a day”, least squares over up to 30 days, at least one day). The mock's “Keeps 10 days” and “Biggest” aren't read: the reads Home Assistant gives the page don't have them. | DS-03 is settled; the forecast still gets its month. |
| DS-S09 | **Home Assistant's columns**: main “Integrations not loading” as a list (“Shelly · Living room plug”, “Retrying · Device unreachable”, its dot; “2 of 171”; “All integrations”), as `ha.png`, without Reload (not built; Worth a look's “Look into it” is the way out); Backups: the machine's and Home Assistant's newest, and with three or more on its own disk one line for them (“9.0 GB of Home Assistant backups on its own disk”, “3 backups, the oldest from March”, amber while the disk is filling up). Worth a look says “Disk full in about 2 months” · “6.0 GB of it is old backups” and “Modbus fills the log” · “92 % of 12,286 errors, still coming in” when one source has half of at least 100 errors. The machine's facts panel is gone: the figures and the head say it. | `ha.png`; less is more. |
| DS-S10 | **Hetzner's metrics** (`GET /v1/servers/{id}/metrics?type=cpu,disk,network&start&end&step`, the hosts steps) for up to six servers, running ones first, through the connection's signed GET, kept 60 s per server and range (a failure isn't kept, the server is left out). Units from Hetzner's reference: `cpu` is a percentage per vCPU (100 is one vCPU busy), divided by the server's cores; `network.0.bandwidth.in/out` bytes/s × 8 / 10⁶ → Mb/s; `disk.0.bandwidth.read/write` bytes/s / 10⁶ → MB/s; kept 30 days by Hetzner, our longest range. Traffic: the servers' `outgoing_traffic` of their `included_traffic` this billing period, shown in Hetzner's TB (1024⁴ bytes: “20 TB” included, not 22). Figures Servers (“1 of 2 running”, “6 vCPU · 12 GB in total”, a bar), CPU (running servers' average, small line), Traffic this month (a bar; amber from 80 %, red from 100 %), Cost (“€ 12.84” “a month”, the existing euro format). Charts: one server its own lines (network in green and out orange, disk read blue and write violet); several one line per server (in and out together). | DS-12; Hetzner documents the per-vCPU CPU. |
| DS-S11 | **Hetzner's servers table and note**: Server (dot, name mono, “Debian 13 · Falkenstein”), Type (“CX22”, “2 vCPU · 4 GB · 40 GB”), CPU (now, a bar), its small line (column “24 h”/“7 days”), Address, and “Open” to the Whombat server whose address is the Hetzner server's IPv4 or lies in its IPv6 /64 (watched servers only). The button column (empty label) is there only when a server matches; a 0.88 client then skips the Summary's table — the Servers tab keeps the old one. A running server Whombat doesn't watch gets the note “Hetzner can’t see memory or disk space. Add web-1 as a server to see them here.” with “Add as a server” → `/things/servers?add=1&address=<its IPv4>` (Add a server fills the address in; several servers: “Add a server” without one). Notices in the mock's words: “web-1 has no backups” · “Hetzner backups are off · 20 % of the server price” (ask “Turn on…”), “Delete protection is off” (for a one-server project; else “… for web-1”) · “Anyone with the API token can delete it” (ask “Turn on”), “Traffic is at 83 % of what’s included”. Badge “All running” / “1 server not running”; head facts “**2** servers” and the cities; Volumes became a tab with its count. Its head button reads “Open in Hetzner”. | `hetzner.png`, DS-12. |
| DS-S12 | **UniFi**: figures Devices (a bar), Clients (small line), Internet (down's small line), Gateway CPU (small line); Access points left out (the Devices table and Worth a look name them). Charts Internet (down green with a wash, up orange, two columns, “312 Mb/s” “down · 40 Mb/s up”, average and peak of down) and Gateway (CPU blue, memory violet, 0–100 %). Main: Devices, offline first, ten (“All devices” when there are more). “Mb/s”, not “Mbit/s”, on the page and the tile. | DS-13; the copy rule's units. |
| DS-S13 | **Devices**: each of up to three numeric readings is a chart card (blue, orange, green; its figure with the unit apart, average and peak with the unit), the same line small under its figure; Readings (main), The device (side), Worth a look. | DS-14. |
| DS-S14 | **Words**: DS-15's figures (“2.4 %”, “63 %”, nought “0”); a peak's time as weekday and time within six days (“Sun 03:10”), else “8 Oct 14:07”, “Now” when the peak is the newest value; “in about 5 days / 3 weeks / 2 months”; Response time's line “average 140 ms in 24 h” / “in 7 days”. Problems stays “failed checks in 24 h” whatever the range (rolled-up hours keep an average, not each failed check). | DS-15. |
| DS-S15 | **Caches**: the machine's read 30 s per machine and range (none for the health pass's figures-only read), the kinds' snapshots 30 s per connection as before (they don't depend on the range), Hetzner's metrics 60 s per server and range; points are read from the database each time (one indexed range scan per page). | DS-03's “30 s per connection and range” where the range matters. |
