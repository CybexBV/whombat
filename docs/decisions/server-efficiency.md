# Server efficiency (SV-01…SV-13, SV-14…SV-23)

Stream SP of the 2026-10-07 improvement-review build (`docs/implementation/improvement-review/assignment.md` §4.1,
report A2-1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12 and the server half of A3-3). No contract change: `@whombat/core` stays as
vendored; every response, realtime event and ordering a client sees is the same. Migration **`0084_read_indexes`**
(expand, indexes only).

Measured on a database of our own (`whombat_ir_sp` on the shared Postgres :55432): the seeded mock world plus a
20 000-message `#billing-v2` (38 members: 8 seeded, 30 extra; 1 % deleted, every 10th a thread reply, every 20th
mentions Mara, 400 agent questions), 60 000 messages spread over four other rooms and 200 000 listed-size audit
events (743 bytes a row on average, like real ones). Query times are the mean of 10–20 runs after warm-up, through
postgres.js on the same host. “Before” is `improve` at 0acfe320 (an exported copy), “after” this branch.

| # | Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SV-01 | **Unread badges in one query per room, on a top-level index.** `publishUnread` counts every member's unread and mentions in one statement (`memberUnreadCounts`: `conversation_members` → `read_states` → a lateral `count(*)` per member) instead of one query per member, and bootstrap's `unreadCounts` uses the same lateral shape per conversation (`unnest` of the ids). Both read a range on the new partial covering index `messages_top_level_idx` on `messages (conversation_id, ord) INCLUDE (author_id, mentions, deleted_at) WHERE thread_root_id IS NULL` (0084): the marker is a position (`ord > coalesce(last_read_ord, -1)`, `ord` starts at 1), so each count is an index-only range scan (heap fetches 0) and deleted rows are filtered from the index. Same counts as before: `read/read-indexes.int.test.ts` compares both functions with the old SQL for members with no marker, a marker without a position, behind, caught up, mentions, deletions, replies. **Measured**, `#billing-v2` badges after a message (37 members to tell): caught-up readers (36 within 30 messages, 2 never read) **91.7 ms → 2.8 ms** (37 statements → 1); markers spread through the history (worst case, 466 k rows counted) 126.4 ms → 32.9 ms. `EXPLAIN ANALYZE`: the old per-member query was a seq scan of 80 k rows (9.8 ms each); the new one is `Index Only Scan using messages_top_level_idx … Heap Fetches: 0`, 3.8 ms for all 37. Bootstrap's unread for Mara (6 rooms, 15 k unread elsewhere): 23.1 ms → 4.3 ms. | The badge query ran once per member on every top-level message, before the sender's response, and scanned the room each time; the only index (`conversation_id, created_at, ord`) can't range on `ord`. |
| SV-02 | **What the sender doesn't need runs in the background.** A commit's `TxSession.background(what, hook, key?)` hooks start right after its outbox frames are published, beside the awaited `after` hooks, and are tracked by `services.background` (`lib/background.ts`, the `UsageService.track` pattern: failures logged, `settle()` for shutdown and tests; `app` close settles first). In the background: a room's unread badges (`publishUnread`, also after deletes and clears), a message's whole notification fan-out (`notifyForMessage`: rows, `notification.created`, push; also an edit's new mentions), the push delivery of every other `notify()` (approvals, handoffs, plans, run recovery keep their rows and `notification.created` in their awaited hook; control/device delivery moves, in the rows' order, one failure stopping nothing), and live-activity and cleared-approval pushes. **Order**: `serial(key)` keeps one room's badges (`unread:<conversation>`), and one run's or approval's live activity (`live:run:<id>`, `live:approval:<id>`) in arrival order; a mark-read badge goes on the room's key too, so a count computed before it can't land after it. Dispatch enqueue stays an awaited hook (its ids are in the response), as do search indexing, status settling and run answers. **Measured**, a person's message in `#billing-v2` with a push transport taking 50 ms a request: plain **353 ms → 16 ms** (79 → 41 statements before the response), mentioning three people 506 ms → 25 ms, `@channel` (32 pushes) **2 000 ms → 25 ms**. | The hook chain held the response for N badge queries and for every push transport, with 250 ms + 1 s retry sleeps on a failing one. |
| SV-03 | **Idle work by events, safety ticks slow.** (1) The **claim long-poll** wakes on every `dispatch.updated` frame (a turn queued, ended, cancelled, requeued) and a new turn's thinking frame, and re-checks on its own every **5 s** (`pollMs`, was 500 ms); before the claim lock and transaction it reads one indexed row (any queued, due dispatch?). (2) The **automations engine** passes when nudged, when a commit may bring it work while it has some (a frame with a signed event, a dispatch or an approval changing; at most one pass a second, `kickMs`), at the next schedule fire (+50 ms), and otherwise every 10 s with work (a live run or a live automation that reads events) and 30 s without; its lock check is every **5 s** (`lockCheckMs`, was 500 ms). Only burrows with a live automation whose current version has a message, reaction or GitHub trigger are scanned (no burrow lock, no cursor write otherwise). (3) One **housekeeping tick** (`lib/housekeeping.ts`, 15 s, each job on its own with its own period and deadline, SV-23) runs the runtime sweep (15 s, was 5 s), the feedback sources' due check (15 s, was 5 s), the #general welcomes (15 s), the realtime credential re-check (every heartbeat, i.e. 30 s, was 25 s) and the usage schedule (60 s; its first look stays 30 s after start); the usage tick keeps its 5-minute sign-in list (SV-21). **Measured**, the server's background work as `index.ts` starts it plus a runner long-polling claims, no clients, 3 minutes: **55 200 → 8 300 statements per idle hour**. Of those, claim long-poll 29 360 → 860; engine lock check 6 960 → 720; engine passes 10 500 → 360; runtime sweep 2 160 → 720; feedback sources 720 → 240; welcomes 480 (same); the GitHub poller's ≈ 2 000 is stream SG's (PL-01). | ≈ 30–40 k round trips an hour on an idle box, 7 200 of them under the global `CLAIM_LOCK`, while `wake()` already reached every waiting claim. |
| SV-05 | **Outbox written once.** `flushOutbox` returns only each row's id and time; the frames published are the in-memory ones copied as JSON (what the row reads back as, so nothing the command does with its objects afterwards reaches a frame). `published_at` is no longer written (one UPDATE per commit gone); `drainOutbox` deletes rows older than a day, at boot and hourly on the housekeeping tick (it was boot only, so the table grew between restarts). Amends MD-11's description (`managed-db.md`: rows aren't marked published; nothing read them). | Every frame went over the wire three times (insert, returning, update) and the table was pruned only at startup. |
| SV-06 | **Superseded in part by one-person.md OP-S62, OP-S79**: the lean reads hold; the person decides every pending approval, and conversations carry no member ids or counts. **Bootstrap reads what it shows.** Member counts by `count(*)` per conversation (member ids only for direct conversations, which name them); branch-room dots from each pull request's state, checks and reviewers (`branchRoomPullRequests`, three statements without the diff column, files, commits or repository names, was six); the approvals a person may decide counted in SQL (`decidablePendingCount`: the first `person_approves` condition decides, private ones only by their approvers, owners any other, named approvers theirs, the rest everyone; agents none) — `read/bootstrap-lean.int.test.ts` checks it against `canDecide` for seven shapes and five viewers, and every count and dot against the full reads. | Every bootstrap loaded every member row of every room, every branch room's files, commits and diff, and every pending approval row, to show counts and dots. |
| SV-09 | **Audit counts on a partial covering index.** `events_listed_kind_idx` on `events (burrow_id, kind) INCLUDE (actor_id, about_id) WHERE text IS NOT NULL` (0084): the per-kind counts of the log page are an index-only scan, with the actor and “who” filters inside the index. **Measured** on 200 k listed events: everyone **25.4 → 9.2 ms**, agents 25.1 → 9.6 ms, one person (`who`) 65.4 → 8.8 ms (`Parallel Index Only Scan … Heap Fetches: 0`). | The counts read the whole log (wide rows) on every page view; only `(burrow_id, actor_id)` was indexed. |
| SV-10 | **Superseded in part by one-person.md OP-S14, OP-S69**: a frame the same for every socket is still serialized once and room membership still comes from the cache; frames are filled at delivery without a per-viewer rule, and there is no presence or typing between people. **Hub fan-out without needless work.** A frame's personalizer is chosen synchronously by its type, a message's card and an approval's action (`framePersonalizer`): plain messages, typing, badges, presence wait for nothing (were five awaited personalizers each). A frame the same for every recipient (no personal view, no reactions marked `mine`) is serialized once; each socket gets it with its own `seq`, byte for byte what `JSON.stringify` of the `ServerMessage` gave (tested). A room's members — the audience of burrow-topic frames and who may type there — come from a 30 s cache (`MEMBERS_TTL_MS`, at most 2 000 rooms) dropped at once by the room's `conversation.updated` frame (every join and leave publishes one, and the hub drops before delivering that frame), `membership.removed`, `principal.created` (a person joining the burrow) and `principal.removed`; a read that started before a drop isn't kept; someone typing whom the cache doesn't list is looked up (and the room re-read). Amends MD-11's “current membership at delivery” to “membership as of the last change the hub saw”. | One membership query per audience frame and per typing frame, five awaits and one `JSON.stringify` per recipient per frame, all in the single delivery chain. |
| SV-11 | **A known credential costs one statement.** What a session secret or API token resolved to — its session row or token id, its account id and expiry — is kept 30 s per process (`auth/token-cache.ts`, at most 5 000); the **account, the burrow and the person in it are read fresh**, in one statement for burrow routes, `/ws` and files (`authenticateIn`), so a name, a role, an admin grant or a removal counts on the next request as before. `session.revoked`, `api_token.revoked` and managed `endpoint.revoked` / `authz.changed` drop entries at once; an expired one is never used. **Measured**: a burrow request with a known credential runs 1 statement instead of 3 (`auth/token-cache.int.test.ts` counts them). The assignment named a cache of session/account/principal; caching the rows too would have needed invalidation at a dozen account and principal write sites for at most one more statement saved. | Three uncached lookups on every REST, socket and file request. |
| SV-12 | **Open questions by place.** “Nobody answered the agent's question” (Work view, agent status `hasOpenQuestion`) is three anti-joins (`unansweredByPeople`): a person after it at the top level (a range on `messages_top_level_idx`), in its thread, or in its own thread (ranges on `messages_thread_idx`) — the same rows as the old `thread_root_id is not distinct from … or …` predicate, a deleted reply still answering. **Measured** with 45 open agent questions in the seeded rooms: **33.6 ms → 1.4 ms**. | Each candidate scanned its whole conversation after it: the OR and `is not distinct from` can't use an index. |
| SV-13 | **Precompressed web files.** `web/static.ts` sends `<file>.br` or `<file>.gz` when the build wrote one and the request accepts it (q-values and `*` honoured; br before gzip), with `Content-Encoding`, the variant's length and its own ETag (`-br` / `-gzip` suffix), and `Vary: Accept-Encoding` on every file that has a variant; otherwise the file as it is. `index.html` stays uncompressed (`no-store`, small). The web build writes the siblings (WE-03, stream WA). | The self-hosted origin streamed the 3.1 MB main chunk raw (report A3-3). |
| SV-14 | **Migration 0084 by hand.** `0084_read_indexes.sql` with its journal entry and `compat.json` row (`expand`, floors `0047_delegation`, runtime reads/writes from 0084), like every migration since 0069: the drizzle snapshots stop at 0068, so `drizzle-kit generate` would re-diff everything since, and drizzle can't declare `INCLUDE`. The schema files declare both indexes (key columns and predicate) with a comment naming the included columns. `CREATE INDEX` without `CONCURRENTLY` (migrations run in a transaction, as 0076's did): on a box's message and event counts it is seconds. | Keep journal, compat manifest and schema consistent with the repo's practice. |
| SV-15 | **Concurrency of hooks.** Background hooks run concurrently with each other and with the awaited chain; the awaited `after` hooks keep their order. | Several awaited hooks commit follow-up work in the same burrow (dispatches, status, run answers) whose order is observable and which would only queue on the burrow lock while holding more pool connections. |
| SV-16 | **Tests wait for background work where they assert it.** The harness's `t.commit` and the automation world's `commit` settle `services.background` before returning (as when those hooks held the commit); tests that post over HTTP or call `notify` directly settle themselves (`local-ipc`, `push-resolve`, `personal-agents`). | Same assertions, without making production wait. |
| SV-17 | **A queued dispatch gets its unrunnable check on time.** `unrunnableMs` (+250 ms) after a `dispatch.updated` frame with state `queued`, one timer at a time, once the sweeper runs (`unrunnableSoon`, on by default; the feedback assessment test switches it off as it drives the sweep itself). Keeps AS-04's reply at ≈ 5 s with the sweep at 15 s. | The quiet “I can't run” reply would otherwise wait for the next sweep (up to 20 s). |
| SV-18 | **Automation cursors when nobody reads events.** The engine moves a burrow's cursor only while a live automation there reads events; `ensureCursor` (publish, publish-and-enable, switch on) moves an existing cursor to the latest event when no *other* live automation of the burrow reads events (else leaves it). Refines BP-C07 (“started at the latest event … history never replays”): before, switching an event automation back on in a burrow with no other published automation replayed what happened meanwhile; now it doesn't. `automations/engine-ticks.int.test.ts`. | Skipping the scan must not let history replay into the next automation that listens. |
| SV-19 | **The engine's handover is two lock checks (10 s).** CS-11's rule is unchanged with `lockCheckMs` 5 s: a new holder waits 10 s before its first pass (was 1 s); a lost lock is noticed within 5 s (was 500 ms). Amends CS-11's 500 ms and BP-C05's 1 s loop. | The check was 7 200 statements an hour on every box for an event (a failover) that a server restart also covers. |
| SV-20 | **Runtime cadences.** `runtime-server.md`'s “re-checks every 500 ms” is 5 s with the wakes above, and “a sweep every 5 s” is 15 s on the housekeeping tick (`sweepMs`): an expired lease (60 s) is requeued within 75 s instead of 65 s. | Amends runtime-server.md. |
| SV-21 | **Usage keeps its sign-in list five minutes.** The usage tick no longer forces a new list every minute: it probes from the list as cached (`SIGN_INS_TTL_MS`), and drops snapshots of sign-ins that are gone only against a list read in that same tick — never against an older list that may lack a sign-in made since. A sign-out's snapshot goes within about five minutes (was one). Amends UI-R10's schedule. | The minute tick re-read every person's sign-ins through the runner for a list that changes when someone signs in or out. |
| SV-22 | **Measurement harnesses stay out of the repo.** The idle counter (a postgres.js `debug` hook around the services `index.ts` starts, with a simulated runner) and the send-latency and query benches ran from `~/.cache/wb-tmp/ir/SP/`; `pg_stat_statements` isn't loaded on the shared Postgres. | No production code for a one-off measurement. |
| SV-23 | **A stuck housekeeping job holds up nothing else.** At each tick every due job starts on its own (never twice at once), so a slow or hung one no longer delays the jobs after it or the next ticks. Each run has a deadline (5 minutes unless the job sets `deadlineMs`): there its `AbortSignal` aborts and the tick stops waiting; a job that ignores the abort stays “running” and isn't started again until it really ends. Each job's last success is kept; a job without one for three periods, at least 15 minutes (counted from when it was added before its first), is **stale**, and the admin socket's `GET /health` says so in `checks.maintenance` (`{ok: false, detail: "maintenance is stale: backup files last finished 40 minutes ago"}`, contracts' `RuntimeHealth`, optional). It doesn't make `status` degraded: a job can keep failing for a reason outside the box (a service that is down), and a degraded runtime makes the manager undo an update. Shutdown gives running jobs 5 s, then aborts them. | The tick awaited every job in turn with no deadline: one hung job stopped every later job and every later tick, and health didn't show it. |
| SV-24 | **A claim's reads run four at a time** (`buildDispatchContext`, `atMost` in `lib/side-by-side.ts`): its two dozen reads still go side by side, but at most four hold a pool connection at once; a part that waits on another part waits outside the gate. The pool stays at ten. | Started all at once they took every connection of the pool, so the commits behind one claim queued until it finished. |

## Known limits

- A credential revoked by another process would keep working for up to 30 s (SV-11); no other process revokes
  sessions or tokens today (the host's `whombat admin` changes only the admin flag, which is read fresh).
- The members cache trusts every join to publish `conversation.updated` or `principal.created`; one that published
  neither would reach the hub's audience filter within 30 s (none known). Subscribing to a room is still checked
  against the database.
- The claim's safety re-check (5 s) is what notices a `notBefore` passing without a requeue frame (a retried turn has
  its own timer) and the end of a drain; those start up to 5 s later than before.
- Unread counts still count every unread row (no cap): a reader who never opened a 20 k-message room costs a 20 k-entry
  index range per badge.
- The outbox is still written for every frame (report A2-5's “consider not persisting”): the managed event log and
  crash bookkeeping read nothing from it, so dropping it is the next step if the insert shows up.
- Migration 0084 may collide with another stream's 0084 at merge: renumber (self-contained, indexes only).
