# The legal texts (workstream `legal-texts`)

What the Terms of Service, the Privacy Policy, the Appliance Terms and the Subscription Terms say, and how they
change. The texts are `apps/web/src/legal/*.md` (the website keeps word-for-word copies). The Appliance Terms and the
Subscription Terms are accepted by version (`APPLIANCE_TERMS_VERSION`, `SUBSCRIPTION_TERMS_VERSION`); nobody ticks the
other two. Decided 2026-11-22 for the launch, after the council's launch review.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| LGL-01 | **The seller's details are on every legal page**: Cybex B.V., James Cookstraat 12G, 7825 AN Emmen, the Netherlands, KvK 77711815, VAT NL861104432B01. The hosted app's legal page shows them in a line under every text (`SELLER_LINE` in `LegalPage.tsx`), and so does the website's. The Terms of Service and the Privacy Policy name them in their text too (version `2026-11-22`); the two accepted texts get them in their next version (LGL-03). | A web shop must show who sells; the page line reaches the accepted terms without changing a version people accepted. |
| LGL-02 | **The Privacy Policy names the services a Whombat talks to itself**: GitHub (update files and the release list), Anthropic (the Claude Code download), Let's Encrypt with its public Certificate Transparency logs (a Whombat's own address becomes public there), Cert Spotter (the box's certificate watch), Hugging Face (the speech-to-text model while voice typing is on, the Dutch voice only after opt-in) and the NTS time servers. They aren't Cybex's processors, so they are a list of their own under “Who helps us”. | The council found them missing; each one is in the appliance's code (`appliance/nix/profiles/prod-instance.nix`, `appliance/manager/internal/clients/upstream.go`, `appliance/manager/internal/ownaddress/env.go`, `appliance/voice/NOTICES.md`, `appliance/nix/modules/base.nix`). |
| LGL-03 | **The Appliance Terms and the Subscription Terms `2026-11-22`** (contracts 4.12.0, CT-1787, CT-1788). Changing an accepted text means a new version (appliance `licence.md` LC-18). Control accepts the current version and the one before it at the claim, the Whombat link code and Link's start, and records the version the person accepted, so control goes out first and the hosted app and both phone apps follow when they update; an app that still sends `2026-11-19` keeps working. They fix: Claude Code downloads from Anthropic's own site, not through Whombat's update channel; section 6 keeps only the keys a self-installed Whombat really has (the recovery kit and the backup password; it has no disk encryption and no disk recovery key, `docs/app/privacy-and-security.md` 5.1); Attachment A, the Dutch voice's use restrictions; the seller's details; and the Subscription Terms say Whombat Link is sold in EU countries only for now, as control enforces at checkout (`LINK_COUNTRIES`). Owners who claimed already aren't asked again: acceptance is checked only at the claim (remote-help.md RH-11). | A fix to an accepted text must not break claims on the day it lands, and the next one shouldn't need every part released at once. |
| LGL-04 | **The Dutch voice's use restrictions are accepted where it is turned on.** Settings' *Dutch voice* already shows Open RAIL-M's restrictions, links the full licence and records who agreed and when (desk-display-talk.md DK-T26); turning it on is the agreement. The Appliance Terms repeat the restrictions as their Attachment A from version `2026-11-22` (LGL-03), so the terms a person accepts carry them too. | The licence asks that its use restrictions be part of the agreement under which the model is used. |
| LGL-05 | **People read what changed in `docs/changelog.md`** (“What's new”), linked from the README: newest first, grouped by day, one plain sentence per change people can notice, no version numbers or hashes. A change people can notice adds its line in the same pull request (`CONTRIBUTING.md`). It started from the release records' `what` lines of 21 and 22 November 2026. | The release records are written for maintainers; people need a short page in their own words. |
