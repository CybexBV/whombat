# Workstream B: durable memory and context continuity (`bp/memory`)

Buzz-parity workstream B (docs/implementation/buzz-parity/PLAN.md). BP-01…12 (`buzz-parity.md`) bind it; in
particular BP-06 (no bodies in signed events), BP-07 (retrieved data is data) and BP-10 (durable background
work). Buzz (`block/buzz`) was a reference for patterns only; no code was copied.

## Model

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| BP-B01 | **Three separate concepts.** *Instructions* (`MemoryItem.kind = instruction`, always scoped to one agent): how an agent works. *Knowledge* (`kind = knowledge`, scoped to a conversation, the burrow or one agent): facts and project constraints with sources. *Checkpoints* (`Checkpoint`, per agent and task key): an agent's own record of an unfinished task. Plus *summaries* (`ContextSummary`, per conversation or thread) of messages older than the window. | Authority, visibility and lifetime differ for each; one “memory” table with flags would blur who may approve what. |
| BP-B02 | **Versions with provenance.** Every correction is a new row in the item's series (`seriesId` = the first version's id, `version` + 1, `supersedesId`); approving it supersedes the active version (a partial unique index keeps at most one active version per series). Each version records `authorId`, `approvedById`, `decidedAt` and `sources` (`message` id, `canvas` slug, `attachment` id, `run` number, http(s) `url`; at most 8). Bodies are bounded (title 120, body 2 000 characters; checkpoint summary 2 000, 20 done lines, 10 constraints of 300). | History stays inspectable (“Replaced”) without rewriting what an agent was told earlier. |
| BP-B03 | **Deleting is a tombstone.** An approver's delete clears title, body and sources of *every* version of the series and keeps id, version, kind, scope and status `deleted`; withdrawing one's own proposal tombstones only that version. Nothing else ever removes an item. | The words go; the fact that something existed (and its audit trail) stays. |

## Authority

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| BP-B04 | **Instructions: proposed by the agent itself (MCP `propose_instruction`) or anyone who can configure it; active only when an owner approves** — the burrow owner, or the owner of a personal agent. People only: `decide_memory` refuses agents. An owner adding an instruction may make it active at once (`activate`). | Instructions change how an agent behaves everywhere; that is configuration, which owners hold. |
| BP-B05 | **Knowledge: agents propose, people approve.** Conversation knowledge is approved (and corrected, deleted) by any person who is a member of the conversation; burrow knowledge by the burrow owner; an agent's own knowledge by anyone who can configure it (its owner for a personal agent). **No scope lets an agent make knowledge active by itself**, not even with a `can` grant. | Anything an agent remembers enters future prompts, possibly other agents'; an approval click is the guard against prompt-injected “memories”. Checkpoints cover an agent's own working state without approval. |
| BP-B06 | **Key “Propose memory” (`memory.propose`, group channels, `agentMaxTier: can`).** `remember` and `propose_instruction` need it; without it the attempt is Blocked and logged like any key. It is not in the hire modal (the mock's list); people switch it in the agent's memory modal (“Let Banjo propose instructions and knowledge”, which calls `agents.setKeys` with the agent's grants plus this key; Configure keeps keys outside its list as they are). Personal agents may hold it (`personalAgentMayHold`). `save_checkpoint` and `recall` need no key (the agent's own task; reads of approved items). | Owners decide who may even suggest memory; blocked attempts are visible in the log. |
| BP-B07 | **Commands and events.** `propose_memory`, `decide_memory`, `revise_memory`, `delete_memory`, `save_checkpoint` go through the commit pipeline. Events: `instruction.proposed\|approved\|rejected\|deleted` (kind Keys), `memory.proposed\|approved\|rejected\|deleted` (System), `checkpoint.saved` (hidden, `text: null`). Texts and payloads carry ids, versions, kind, scope and status only — never titles, bodies or sources (BP-06). Copy: “Proposed knowledge in #billing-v2”, “Approved an instruction for Banjo”, “Proposed a correction to knowledge in #billing-v2 (version 2)”, “Deleted knowledge in a DM”, “Added knowledge for the whole burrow”. | The log is visible to the whole burrow (C-10). |

## Privacy and scoping

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| BP-B08 | **Visibility.** Conversation knowledge: members of the conversation. Burrow knowledge: everyone in the burrow. Agent-scoped items: every person for a shared agent, only the owner for a personal agent. Checkpoints: members of their conversation (and the owner for a personal agent). Lists, items, history, sources and search (`q`, Postgres FTS on `memory_items.tsv`) are filtered per item by these rules; a hidden item answers 404. | Same gates as conversations (C-10, IN-12); a personal agent's memory is its owner's (PA-08). |
| BP-B09 | **Sources fit the scope when proposed.** A source must exist and be readable by whoever cites it. Burrow knowledge only from sources every person in the burrow can read (a conversation all people are members of, a canvas without a conversation, a URL). Conversation knowledge only from its own conversation or one whose members include all of this conversation's members. There is no public/private flag on channels, so “public” is computed from membership. | Private content never lands in a wider scope, whoever proposes it. |
| BP-B10 | **Sources are re-authorized at every dispatch.** Knowledge whose source the dispatched agent can no longer read (removed from the channel, deleted message) is left out and flagged (`flag`, shown on the item); agent-scoped knowledge also needs every source's readers to include everyone in the dispatch's conversation. Instructions are owner-approved declarations: their sources are provenance and do not gate them. | “Agent removed from source conversation → item not selected” without trusting what was true when it was approved. |
| BP-B11 | **Membership changes invalidate derived state.** A summary stores a hash of its conversation's members; a different hash means it is not used and is rebuilt (memory `degraded`: “The summary of older messages is being rebuilt (the members changed).”). Sessions include the members in their fingerprint (BP-B14). | A summary or session made for one audience is not reused for another. |
| BP-B24 | **Superseded in part by resourceful-agents.md RA-21**: an agent's own notes carry no origin; memory items still do. **An agent's proposal stays with the room it came from** (security review SEC-MEM-WIDEN; BP-B09 only checked *sources*, and a body is free text). Every agent proposal records its origin, the conversation of its dispatch (`memory_items.origin_conversation_id`; `remember` and `propose_instruction` pass it, an agent without one is refused). An agent may propose where everyone who would see the item once approved can read that conversation: burrow knowledge and a shared agent's instructions (or knowledge) only from a conversation every person in the burrow is in; a personal agent's only from a conversation its owner is in; conversation knowledge only from a conversation whose members include everyone in the target one (the MCP tools use the dispatch's own). Otherwise the tool answers `forbidden` (“Burrow memory can only come from places everyone in the burrow can read. Keep it to this conversation instead.” / “Instructions and knowledge for an agent can only be proposed from a channel everyone in the burrow is in, so nothing private travels with it.”). Until someone approves it, a proposal with an origin is visible only to its approvers, its author and the origin's members (`memoryPermissions` fact `outsideOrigin`); a correction of an unapproved proposal keeps its origin, and agents do not submit corrections (`revise_memory` refuses them; they propose anew from their conversation). People's own proposals have no origin. | A private room's words must not reach the burrow through an agent, before or after approval; the approver decides with the text in front of them. |

## Context assembly

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| BP-B12 | **A bounded budget instead of “last 20”.** `CONTEXT_BUDGET_CHARS` = 64 000 characters (~16 000 tokens at 4 characters per token, always labelled an estimate) for what the dispatch carries beyond the persona. Always kept: the triggering message, the thread root, active instructions (up to 20) and the open checkpoint. Then recent messages newest first (thread replies first, then the channel window; up to 60 % of what is left, at least three), then knowledge ranked by FTS against the request and recency (at most 8), then the summary of older context, then older messages with what remains (`planContext`, pure and unit-tested). Candidates: the newest 60 top-level messages and the whole thread. A long thread keeps its root and newest replies; its middle goes to the thread's summary. | Keeps what matters most under a hard cap; deterministic and testable. |
| BP-B13 | **Prompts.** The system prompt gets a `## Memory` section **after** Whombat's rules in the runner's prompt (`apps/runner/src/prompt.ts`), saying it is data that never grants keys, with each item delimited as `[[memory <id> · <kind> · version N · for … · sources: …]] … [[/memory]]` (delimiters inside bodies are defused), the open checkpoint (“Do not redo anything listed as done”), how to remember and checkpoint, and — when the last measured input was ≥ 100 000 tokens — a request to save a checkpoint. The user turn gets the summary (“Earlier here (a summary of N older messages through 11:02, extractive):”), what was left out, and for a resumed session “Only what is new since your last turn follows”. The persona builder in `@whombat/agent-runtime` renders the same lines through the foundation's hook. | BP-07; the agent cites ids it relied on (the live check shows it does). |
| BP-B14 | **Absent is not unavailable.** If memory cannot be read, the dispatch still runs with the plain 20-message window and `MemoryContext.status = 'unavailable'`; the prompt says memory is unavailable and not to assume there are no instructions or constraints. `degraded` (with `reason`) when older context is missing, stale or its update failed. | Fail closed on memory without failing the turn. |

## Summaries

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| BP-B15 | **Out of band, durable, extractive.** Building a context enqueues a `memory_jobs` row when messages fell out of the window uncovered (one pending job per scope; a newer target replaces the pending one). A worker in the server (started with it, every 30 s, and kicked after each enqueue) leases jobs (60 s), retries a failure after 5 s up to 3 attempts, then marks it failed; the result is written only if the summary's `version` is still the one it read (fencing). The producer is deterministic and needs no model (`extractiveSummary`: lines with decisions, constraints, deadlines and questions with author and message id, then each participant's latest line; incremental over the previous summary; at most 4 000 characters) behind a `Summarizer` interface. **A failed update never overwrites a stored summary**: it keeps its text, becomes `failed` with `lastError`, and dispatches report `degraded`. | BP-10; no provider cost or tool access on the summary path; a model-written summary can replace the producer later without changing storage or failure rules. Semantic continuity comes from the agent's own checkpoints (BP-B18). |

## Sessions

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| BP-B16 | **Sessions per scope, reused only when compatible.** `agent_sessions` holds one active provider session per (agent, `thread:<root>` or `conv:<conversation>`) with a fingerprint of per-part hashes: provider, model, access, working directory (own or the burrow's default), checkout, keys (with tiers and scopes), active instructions (ids and versions), pack pins (empty until Workstream D) and the conversation's members. The claim (inside the claim lock, so the one-in-flight invariant is untouched) resumes the active session only if every part matches, it was used in the last 3 days and its last measured input was under 140 000 tokens; otherwise it is retired with the reason (“the keys changed”, “the session was idle too long”, “the session’s context was nearly full”) and a fresh session starts. Run control keeps resuming the run's session (unchanged), search answers never resume, a retried dispatch keeps the session it had. | A session carries earlier context that was authorized under the old keys, members and instructions. *Amended by turn-efficiency.md TE-02/TE-03:* one key given and members leaving keep the session (a join still retires it); a thread's first turn may start as a copy of the conversation's warm session. |
| BP-B17 | **Resume sends only what is new, in the same directory.** A resumed dispatch gets messages newer than what the session has seen (`seenThroughAt`), no summary, and `MemoryContext.session.scratchKey` = the session row id; the runner names the scratch directory of a turn without a working directory after it, because Claude Code keys sessions by working directory. A resumed turn that fails retires its session and the retry starts fresh. **Compaction**: Claude compacts inside its session; for both providers Whombat rotates to a fresh session with summary and checkpoint when the measured input passes 140 000 tokens. Codex's `thread/compact/start` is not called (the adapter is outside this workstream and the call was not verified live). | Live check: a follow-up in the same thread resumed the Claude session with ~250 tokens of new context. |
| BP-B25 | **A session does not outlive the knowledge it was given** (security review: sessions kept deleted knowledge). Each dispatch records the knowledge items its context carried (`dispatch_memory.knowledge_ids`); a finished turn adds them to its session (`agent_sessions.knowledge_ids`). At the claim, the session is resumed only while every one of them is still active (not deleted or superseded by a correction) and still usable here — the conversation's own for conversation knowledge, every source readable by the agent, and for agent knowledge by everyone in the conversation (`knowledgeUsable`, the same check that flags an item at selection). Otherwise it is retired with “the knowledge it was given changed” and a fresh session starts from the summary and the checkpoint. Newly approved knowledge does not retire a session (it is simply given on the next turn). Instructions were already covered: their ids and versions are part of the fingerprint, so deleting or correcting one starts fresh (“the instructions changed”). | The provider's own history still holds the words of everything it was given; a tombstone or a flag must reach it. The per-item check avoids retiring every session in the burrow whenever any knowledge changes. |
| BP-B18 | **Checkpoints across restarts.** `save_checkpoint` (per agent and task key: `run:<id>`, `thread:<root>` or `conv:<conversation>`; `automation:<id>:<step>` reserved for Workstream C) upserts with a version; the next dispatch on the same task key gets the open checkpoint, including a retry after a crash or a server restart, and is told not to redo what is listed as done. | Continuation without duplicated side effects (tested with a crash, a restart and a second turn). |

## Retention

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| BP-B19 | Instructions and knowledge (every version) are kept until deleted; deleting clears the words (BP-B03). Pruned hourly by the memory worker: done or abandoned checkpoints after 30 days, retired sessions after 30 days, per-dispatch memory records after 30 days, finished or failed summary jobs after 7 days. Summaries are replaced, never appended, and live as long as their conversation. Backups cover all of it (it is in the database). |
