# Service dashboards: a page that makes sense for each service (SD)

John, 2026-10-21 (main checkout): “when we have Plex connected and I click on Plex I would rather see the current movie
library, issues it may have, etc than what I see now. So we shouldn't take over functionality from the service itself
… Home Assistant, we should show a dashboard with buttons and lights that we can operate, … how it's doing, issues it
might have (what we currently are already doing is good). But when I connect Overseerr, SABnzbd, etc we should see a
dashboard that actually makes sense for the service.” My research (each service's API checked against its 2026 docs)
and the list per service, then “I agree fully with this and I would like you to implement this fully as well”.

Builds on connection-pages.md (CP-*), dashboards.md (DS-*), connections-page.md (CN-*), fusebox-page.md (FP-*) and
connections-more.md (CM-*). Contracts 0.121.0 (CT-1000…CT-1049), whombat-app 0.123.0 (AP-4530…AP-4729), migration
0135. Server details are SD-S* (`## Server details` below); the brief is `docs/implementation/service-dashboards/assignment.md`.

## The rule

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| SD-01 | **A Service's page shows what is in it at a glance, what is happening now, what is wrong, and one to four buttons; never the service's whole app.** Four figures first; then “now” (what plays, downloads, waits for you); then the content at a glance (recently added, the queue, the inbox — a dozen items, newest first); “Worth a look” with the service's own problems; the service's own page one click away (“Open Plex”) for everything else. **This replaces CP-02 for services with a page of their own**: Whombat now shows what is inside a service, not only how it runs. | John's words; a page that only says “answering in 40 ms” is no reason to open it. |
| SD-02 | *In part superseded by service-settings.md SST-05*: a service's declared settings may be changed from its page; the rest stands. **What Whombat never does from a page**: delete anything (media, files, requests, downloads, containers, records), update or upgrade the service, restart or shut down the service itself (Home Assistant's existing Restart stays, CP-12), change its settings, send anything to customers (invoices, reminders), kick a viewer. These stay in the service, or with an agent and its approvals. | Buttons are for the obvious, reversible things you'd otherwise open the app for. |
| SD-03 | **The service's figures come first, the machine's second.** A service with a page of its own shows its own four figures, then the machine's CPU and Memory when Whombat knows what it runs on (CP-08); the machine's chart cards stay on the machine's page (the head's “Runs on …” links there) and its disk notice stays in Worth a look. The response-time chart is left out once a kind draws its own page (it stays in Settings' checks). | Plex's page is about films, not CPU; the machine is one click away. |
| SD-04 | **Superseded by one-person.md OP-S50, OP-S69**: the person manages every connection and reads every page in full. **People's names and personal details only for those who manage the connection**: viewers (Plex), requesters (Seerr), who opened a door (Access), device owners (Tailscale), photo owners (Immich), customers and payers (Mollie, Moneybird, HubSpot, Pipedrive). Others see the same panels with counts and titles (“2 watching”, “Asked by someone”). A personal connection is only its person's anyway. | FP-10's rule: other people's doings are the owners' business. |
| SD-05 | **Every Service has a card and a sidebar dot from its page's reader** (the 5-minute health pass, FP-01/FP-02): two or three of its figures on the card, and the dot yellow or red with the first serious line of Worth a look as its reason (“1 indexer switched off”, “Camera offline: Garden”). Bad lines make it red, warn lines yellow, info lines nothing. | One rule for every kind; the sidebar stays calm unless something is wrong (calmer-sidebar). |

## Building blocks (contracts 0.121.0)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| SD-06 | **Pictures on pages** (CT-1000): posters, camera snapshots, photo and document thumbnails. A page names a picture by an opaque `ref` the server signed (which connection, which path, until when); clients load it like an attachment image (HTTP `things.image`, or the session's download stream `thing-image`). The server fetches it as the connection (its key never reaches a browser or phone), or from TMDB's public image host for Seerr's posters, keeps only JPEG, PNG and WebP, makes it at most 480 px on its long side, and keeps it 10 minutes (camera snapshots 5 seconds). A ref is good for 24 hours and only on its own thing's page for people who may see that thing. | Pictures are what make a media or camera page; the key stays on the box. |
| SD-07 | **Buttons where the thing is** (CT-1001…CT-1003): a button on a row (Approve on a request, Retry on a failed download, Unlock on a door), on a panel's head (Pause all downloads) and **Controls** (CT-1005): switches, buttons and steppers grouped by room or kind (Home Assistant). Every button runs `things.action` with its target and value; the same rules as Restart (CP-12): only for people who may manage the connection, refused on Look only with a plain reason, confirmed when it matters (doors, locks, alarms, covers, power, anything for everyone), run through the commit pipeline. | One way for every button; nobody learns a second set of rules. |
| SD-08 | *In part superseded by service-settings.md SST-03*: a declared setting's change can be put back. **What a button leaves behind**: every button is recorded as the person's change on the thing — “Approved *Dune: Part Two*”, “Unlocked Front door”, “Restarted Minecraft” — in the thing's Activity and the burrow's log (Home's Done recently keeps listing only what agents changed, HR-05, as for Restart). **Everyday switches** — a light, a switch, a scene, pausing or resuming downloads, a speed limit, pausing blocking, marking a task done — are changes of kind `thing.control.<action>`: in the thing's Activity only, not in the burrow's log. No Undo: the page's own switch is the way back. | Turning on the kitchen light twenty times a day doesn't belong in the log. |
| SD-09 | **Live while open** (CT-1010): a page that shows something moving says how often to ask again (`refreshS`): 5 seconds while something downloads or plays, 10 seconds for Home Assistant's controls and camera snapshots, else the 60 seconds of CP-04. The server keeps those reads that short; everything else stays 30 seconds. A button's answer makes the client read the page again at once. | A download queue that stands still is wrong; reading every service every 5 seconds isn't needed. |
| SD-10 | **Services link to each other** (SD-S08): a request in Seerr shows where it is (“Downloading 63 % · 12 min left”) and links to SABnzbd's page, Sonarr's or Radarr's queue rows link to SABnzbd, NZBHydra2's grabs say which of them asked, Plex's recently added says who asked for it (SD-04). Only between connections the viewer sees. | The media chain is one journey; each page shows its part and the way to the next. |
| SD-11 | **Older apps** leave out what they don't know (tolerant decoding, AP-255): a gallery, controls or a button an older phone can't draw are simply not shown, the rest of the page is. The web always matches its runtime. | No feature gate needed; the phone catches up with its next release. |

## Server details

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| SD-S01 | **One kinds registry** (`things/kinds/index.ts`): each kind is a module `{ page, prefetch?, seen?, asksFirst?, actions?, liveS? }`; `page.ts` and `home/health.ts` read the registry instead of their own `KINDS`/`PREFETCH`/`ASKS_FIRST` maps and `seenOf` branches. Group files (`kinds/media/index.ts`, `kinds/home/…`, `kinds/infra/…`, `kinds/storage/…`, `kinds/business/…`) export their kinds; the four existing kinds stay where they are. Fusebox's specifics stay as they are (PREFETCH's time zone, gateway rules, FP-S*). | Thirty kinds without thirty edits to two central files. |
| SD-S02 | **KindView grows** `machine: 'after'` (SD-03: the kind's figures first, then the machine's CPU and Memory, no machine chart cards), `charts` present means “no response-time chart” even when empty, `refreshS` (SD-09), and `seen` (card figures + status from the notices, SD-05) built by one helper `seenFromView`. | The rules of SD-03 and SD-05 in one place. |
| SD-S03 | **Images** (SD-06): `ref` = base64url of `{c: connectionId, s: 'c'\|'t', p: path, w?: width, e: expiry}` + `.` + an HMAC-SHA256 (first 16 bytes, base64url) under a key derived from the server's secret with the label `thing-image`; refused when the signature, expiry or thing don't match (404, never saying why). Fetch: `signedConnectionFetch` GET (source `c`) or the link-preview fetcher for `https://image.tmdb.org/t/p/w342…` (source `t`); 8 s deadline, 3 MB at most, sniffed JPEG/PNG/WebP only; downscaled to 480 px through the preview worker when larger; in-memory cache 64 MB, 10 minutes (5 s for refs with `w: 0`, snapshots). `Cache-Control: private, max-age=300` (snapshots `no-store`). | Signed refs make the route useless as a fetcher of anything else. |
| SD-S04 | **Actions** (SD-07): `things.action` takes `{ action, target?, value? }`; the kind's `actions[action]` checks the target and value against what it offers (Home Assistant: only an entity among today's controls and a service its domain allows), runs it as the connection, and returns `{ message, title, everyday }`; the server records it with `thing.action` (title in Done recently) or, for `everyday`, a change of kind `thing.control.<action>` that Done recently leaves out (SD-08). Look only, not managing, an unknown action or target: refused in plain words. | Kinds own their buttons; the rules stay central. |
| SD-S05 | **Reads stay polite**: one read per connection per page build (cached as CP-S07, 30 s; `liveS` kinds 4 s for their live part only), pictures and per-item details (Seerr's posters, Sonarr's covers) capped at 12 per page and cached an hour, Airtable-style metered APIs left out. A part that fails leaves its panel out and says why in `problem`; the rest of the page stays. | Pages open every 5 seconds must not hammer a NAS or a rate-limited API (Plausible 600/h, Pipedrive costs). |
| SD-S06 | **Points** (DS-11, 5-minute pass, 30 days): SABnzbd `sab_mbps`; Plex `plex_streams`; OPNsense `gw_delay_ms`, `gw_loss_pct`; Pi-hole/AdGuard `dns_q`, `dns_blocked` (for the card's history); TrueNAS, Synology, QNAP `nas_used_gb` (the space used, for the Trend, service-slots.md SLT-80); Immich `immich_photos`, `immich_videos` (the server's totals, for its Trend, service-slots.md SLT-73); others none. | Only where the service keeps no history of its own, one point key per figure; a daily reading for a Trend is such a point (service-slots.md SLT-19). |
