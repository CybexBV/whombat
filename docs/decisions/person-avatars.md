# A person's photo (PV)

John, 2026-10-05: “Add the ability to change a persons avatar.” Until now a person was always their initials on a
tone. Contracts 0.47.0 (whombat-contracts CT-265): `Person.avatarUrl`, `me.setAvatar` (`PUT /me/avatar`
`{image: base64 | null}` → `ProfileResponse`), feature `people.avatars`, retry safety `RECEIPT`. This file is the
runtime's side; whombat-app `docs/decisions/person-avatars.md` is the clients'.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| PVA-01 | **Account-wide, like the name** (`commands/profile.ts` `setAvatar`, next to `updateProfile`): the account keeps the photo (account state, not signed), then the person in every burrow the account is live in follows through the commit pipeline, one burrow at a time (`set_member_avatar`). `null` removes it everywhere. The route only calls `setAvatar`; every write is a transaction of the pipeline (`transactAccount`, `commit`). Served over the managed session like `me.updateProfile` (the dispatcher serves every contract `api` op it has a route for; `docs/managed/parity-inventory.md` row added); `people.avatars` is advertised. | One photo per person, everywhere they are, as with C-17's name and title. |
| PVA-02 | **A photo is an attachment with no conversation.** Migration 0073 (`expand`) makes `attachments.conversation_id` nullable. Each burrow has its own ready row (`kind` image, `conversation_id` and `message_id` null, uploader = the person, name `photo.jpg`/`.png`/`.webp`, never a name a person chose, `url` = `attachmentUrl(slug, id)`), its bytes the account's blob. Upload paths keep it out: `uploads.discard` answers 404 for it, a send refuses it (another conversation), completing or re-uploading finds it ready, dispatches, the runner's fetch, `read_attachment`, automations and memory only see files on messages. | Clients load a photo exactly like an image attachment, over HTTP or the session's resource streams (both through `readableAttachment`, previews included), with the same safe headers (`nosniff`, sandbox CSP, ETag = sha256). No new transfer, no new store. |
| PVA-03 | **The person points at their row**: `principals.avatar_attachment_id` (FK, `on delete set null`) and `avatar_url`, set together. `toPerson` gives `avatarUrl` only while both are set, else null, so every Person (bootstrap `me` and `principals`, members, `people.get`, `ProfileResponse`, realtime `person.updated` / `principal.created`) carries it; agents are unchanged. The URL is stored because burrow slugs never change and `toPerson` has no burrow at hand. | One mapping for every place that builds a Person; a row an older binary deleted can't leave a dangling URL. |
| PVA-04 | **The account keeps the bytes**: `accounts.avatar` (jsonb `{sha256, contentType, size, width, height}`), so a person made later gets the photo (PVA-09). A blob an account names is in use: the sweep and `removeIfUnused` leave it. | A burrow joined next year still gets the photo, even if every row that had those bytes is gone. |
| PVA-05 | **What a photo may be**: base64 that decodes to 1…`AVATAR_MAX_BYTES` (32 KiB) bytes, sniffed by `files/sniff.ts` under a name without an extension (so text, SVG and HTML never pass) as `image/jpeg`, `image/png` or `image/webp` (GIF refused), each side 32…1024 pixels. Otherwise 400 `bad_request` with `details.reason` (`empty`, `too_big`, `type`, `damaged`, `too_small`, `too_large`) and a sentence people read (“Choose a JPEG, PNG or WebP photo.”, “That photo is too big. Photos can be up to 32 KB.”, “That photo is too small. Choose one at least 32 × 32 pixels.”…). No re-encoding: the bytes are served with the sniffed image type and the attachment headers. | The contract's limits; the sniffer is the one uploads trust. Re-encoding would need an image codec in the runtime for 32 KiB the client already scaled. |
| PVA-06 | **Who may read a photo**: anyone in the burrow now (person or agent, not removed or retired) while the row is the photo of someone in that burrow, a former member included (PVA-10). A row that is no one's photo is not found, like everything else nobody may read. | A photo is as public in the burrow as the name next to it; nobody outside the burrow can fetch it. |
| PVA-07 | **At most 10 photo changes an hour per account** (`AVATAR_CHANGES_PER_HOUR`), setting and removing both count, the same photo again doesn't. Counted in `upload_reservations` under `avatar:<account>` (burrow `''`: account-wide) under the upload rate lock class, so it is durable and the sweep prunes it after two hours. Over it: 429 `rate_limited`. **Photos don't count against the burrow's file quota check** (never refused for quota: one row of at most 32 KiB per person), though their rows are in the burrow's usage sum. | Enough for trying a few crops; a loop can't churn signed events and rows. A full burrow must not stop someone changing their face. |
| PVA-08 | **Replacing and removing**: `set_member_avatar` inserts the new row, points the person at it, deletes the old row and, after the commit, removes the old blob if nothing uses it; `setAvatar` tries the old blob once more after all burrows. Each change signs `member.updated` (“Changed their photo” / “Removed their photo”, payload `principalId`, `avatar: {attachmentId, sha256, contentType, size} \| null`, `previousAttachmentId`; never bytes) and publishes `person.updated`. The same photo again (same sha256) changes nothing and signs nothing in that burrow. The orphan sweep never deletes a row that is someone's current photo; any other row without a message goes after the orphan window as before. | Rows and bytes don't pile up; history says when someone changed their photo, as it does for their name. |
| PVA-09 | **Superseded in part by one-person.md OP-S03**: a person made with the burrow takes the account's photo; there are no invites to accept. **People made later take the account's photo**: creating a burrow and accepting an invite insert the person's row from `accounts.avatar` in the same transaction; `burrow.created` / `member.joined` carry `avatarAttachmentId` (and the sha256). Someone coming back after leaving shows the account's photo now (or none). If the account's blob is missing (only after a rollback to an older binary whose sweep took it), the person gets the initials instead of a broken URL. | Same as the name (`personFromAccount`). |
| PVA-10 | **Superseded in part by one-person.md OP-S02, OP-S08**: nobody is removed; a former person's row kept for the signed history keeps its photo. **Someone removed from a burrow keeps the photo they had there**, like their name: their row stays, readable by the burrow, and later changes skip that burrow (as `updateProfile` skips it, EP-02). | History shows who said what, with the face they had. |
| PVA-11 | **New photo, new URL.** Every change makes a new attachment id in every burrow, so the URL changes and clients' caches (`cache-control: private, max-age=3600`, ETag = sha256) never show an old photo. A removed photo's URL answers 404 at once; clients show the initials then (and while loading or on failure). | No cache busting needed. |
| PVA-12 | **Rollback (migration 0073, `expand`, floors 0047)**: older binaries ignore the new columns and show initials. Their sweep may delete photo rows older than the orphan window; the FK clears the person's id, so the URL is ignored and the person shows initials; setting the photo again repairs everything (the blob is stored again even when the account's sha256 is unchanged). The seed and mock worlds have no photos. | A rollback at worst loses photos, never breaks reads. |
