# Workstream: One-time Find My watches (FMW)

John, 2026-11-23: “I want Dusty to tell me when somebody leaves a certain location where they are at the moment when I
ask it in a chat, so only then.” A `findmy` automation fires every time and needs a plan card turned on, and it knows
places only by Find My's words (a town), so it couldn't. He agreed to the proposal: a one-time watch started from the
chat, with a card and Cancel, “left” meaning another street and more than about 250 m away, ending after 24 hours, and
arriving somewhere too. Find My as a connection is `findmy-connection.md`; the timeline `findmy-timeline.md`. Names here
are made up (Noor, Ruben and Mara Visser, a Mac called Studio).

## The watch (2026-11-23)

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| FMW-01 | **A watch is one-time and starts from the chat.** The assistant's `findmy_watch` tool (`action: start`) starts one in the turn's conversation and thread, for `leaves` (where the person is now) or `arrives` (a place in Find My's words: Home, Work, a town). It ends the first time it happens. Every time stays an automation's job (`findmy-connection.md`). | John: “only then”. |
| FMW-02 | **It starts at once, without a plan card.** It only tells the person in their own Chat, as a reminder does (automation-chat.md AT-17): the card shows what it watches and until when, with Cancel. | Asking for a yes on something the person just asked for is a chore. |
| FMW-03 | **The tool reads Find My first** (the `findmy_people` read: up to 45 s, a Mac in use left alone) and starts from that read; a read older than 15 minutes refuses with why. Someone Find My has no place for can't be watched; an arrival where they already are starts nothing and says so; a name that matches several people asks which. | “Where they are at the moment” must be now, not the last read an hour ago. |
| FMW-04 | **Someone has left** when a read has them on another street *and* more than 250 m (`FIND_MY_WATCH_AWAY_M`) from where they were, or more than either spot's accuracy when that is larger. Without spots (the geocoder found none) another street is enough, and without streets other place words. A read with no location is never a leave. `findMyWatchLeft` in core. | Find My's spots are the Mac geocoder's guess of a street address, about 100 m: a smaller jump, or the same street, is the same place. |
| FMW-05 | **Someone has arrived** when Find My names the place for them (without case or spaces), as a `findmy` trigger's arrival. `findMyWatchArrived` in core. | Find My's own places (Home, Work) are what people ask about; a street arrival would need geocoding an address the person names, not asked for. |
| FMW-06 | **The box compares every read itself, with no AI turn**: the page's, the background's and the assistant's (`FindMyStore.onRead` → `FindMyWatcher.noted`), one Mac at a time. | A turn per read would cost money every few minutes to compare two addresses. |
| FMW-07 | **A connection with a watch reads at least every 5 minutes** (`FIND_MY_WATCH_EVERY_MIN`, `paceWithWatch`), and the background pass starts at once when a watch starts. `HostFindMy.background.watching` names whom (“Reads every 5 minutes while watching Noor Visser”); an automation's name stays the reason when the pace is the same. | Telling someone 15 minutes late isn't telling them when it happens; 5 minutes is the pace John reads at already. |
| FMW-08 | **When it happens the assistant says so in the watch's thread**, server-written in its name (“Noor Visser has left Keizersgracht 12, Amsterdam and is now in Amstelveen.”), as any reply: inbox and push. A watch started outside a thread answers under its card. The card turns into one line. | One message the person reads on the phone; the thread it was asked in holds the answer. |
| FMW-09 | **A watch ends by itself after 24 hours** (`FIND_MY_WATCH_HOURS`, each background pass) and says so: “Noor Visser is still at … after 24 hours, so I’ve stopped watching.” | A watch nobody remembers must not fire days later. |
| FMW-10 | **A removed connection or a Mac no longer among the servers stops its watches**, with a message. The row's `connection_id` is set null when the connection goes so the next pass can say so; a Mac counts as gone only when the fleet was read. | Silence would leave the person waiting for a message that can't come. |
| FMW-11 | **Cancel** is the card's (`connections.findMyWatchCancel`, the person) or the assistant's (`action: cancel`, the assistant that started it); it posts nothing, the card says “Stopped watching …”. A watch that already ended answers as it is. `list` gives the assistant the watches running. | Stopping is one tap; the assistant needs the ids to stop one when asked in words. |
| FMW-12 | **At most 10 watches run at once** per Whombat; a new watch of the same person for the same change on the same connection replaces the running one. | “Tell me when Noor leaves” asked twice means the newer place. |
| FMW-13 | **Where they were is kept only while it watches**: the street, place words and geocoded spot live on the watch's row (`findmy_watches`, migration `0019_findmy_watches`, expand) and are cleared when it ends; the card keeps its words, which the chat already says. No event of its own: the card's message is the record, so the event log never holds a spot. | The promise of `findmy-connection.md`: no street or coordinates kept without a reason. |
| FMW-14 | **Only the person's own turns** start, list or cancel watches (as `findmy_timeline`): a turn for nobody or an outsiders' turn is refused. The tool's access is `read`, so it is on by default like `findmy_people`. | It tells the person where someone is going; nobody else asks for that. |
| FMW-15 | **The `findmy_watch` card is a new card kind, resolved on read for every reader alike, with no session minor**: an older app leaves an unknown card out and shows the message's text (“I’ll tell you when …”), and an older release going back shows the same text. All the card's words come from core (`findMyWatchCardLines`). | The text says the same thing; a minor would hold the feature back for nothing. |
| FMW-16 | **The guide steers between the two**: `findmy_watch` for “once”, a `findmy` automation for “every time”. | The assistant picks by what the person said. |
