# The agent's face shows what it is doing (FM-01…FM-15)

John, 2026-11-27: “How can we improve the current Whombat Characters, including animations and gestures”, then, after
the research and a mockup, “make sure that this animation and new character doesn't influence the CPU a lot”, then
“Implement this fully”. The research (two models, independently) and the CPU measurements agreed on one rule: **the face
moves when something happens, then stands still.** It replaces the still face where the agent is present and doing
something; everywhere else the still files of crew.md stay.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| FM-01 | **States, each a drawn pose.** `idle` neutral, `listening`, `thinking`, `working`, `speaking` (neutral with the happy pose's open mouth), `needs` (neutral, eyes and a brow glance aside and stay), `done` (happy, its own gesture, then calm), `sorry` apologetic, `asleep` sleepy, `welcome` (wakes, ears perk, a nod), `celebrate`. A face first drawn in a state shows its resting pose; the sequence plays when the state changes, or at once when the place asks it to greet (`greet`). | The person reads at a glance what the agent does, without words. Replaying “done” on every page load would be noise. |
| FM-02 | **Built from the handoff's drawings.** The poses are the ten expressions per face in `docs/design-handoff/whombat-crew/expressions/`, stored as what each changes on the bust (head and ear transforms, eyes, brows, mouth, glasses, blush, the small symbol): `packages/avatars/src/faceData.ts`, generated by `pnpm --filter @whombat/avatars face-data` and tested to give back every drawing exactly. `faceSvg` draws one pose inline with its ids prefixed, so faces share a page. The fifteen files stay the still face and the first paint. This replaces crew.md CRW-01's “nothing draws them” and five-agents.md AP-646's “nothing moves” for the live face. | The art is the designer's: nothing is redrawn by hand, and a new drawing comes in by running the generator. |
| FM-03 | **Size decides how much moves.** Under 25 px the still small head. 25–39 px: pose changes only, at once, in the head file's framing. From 40 px: blinks, blends between poses, gestures and the stepped speaking mouth. From 64 px: the symbol beside the head and the thought dots lit in turn. From 96 px: small loops (breathing while working, at most 20 s; ears following the voice while listening). A round face that always shows the head asks for `crop="head"`. | Detail that can't be seen costs as much as detail that can. |
| FM-04 | **One face moves per page**: the biggest, then the newest. The others change pose at once and never blink. | Several faces moving at once compete for attention and multiply the cost. |
| FM-05 | **Idle costs nothing.** Between events nothing runs but a blink timer (every 3.5–7 s, one in six a double blink), and blinking stops two minutes after the last touch, key or state change until the next one. Nothing runs while the page is hidden or the app is in the background. A tap pokes a face where nothing else answers a tap (`pokeable`), at most every 2 s: a small squash, surprised, back, its own gesture. | Measured: a still face costs 0.46 % of a core, a blinking one 0.85 %. |
| FM-06 | **Three motion modes.** `full`; `calm` (no loops: blinks, blends, gestures, the stepped mouth and dots stay); `reduced` (poses change, nothing moves). The system's “reduce motion” and the device's **Moving faces** switch (Settings → Often used, web and phone, kept on that device; on the web `localStorage` `whombat.faceMotion`) give `reduced`. Its words: “Moving faces”, “Faces blink and show what your agents are doing.” | People who find motion tiring keep the information without the motion. |
| FM-07 | **The box's own screen is always `calm`.** On a replica of the kiosk (cage, Chromium without a graphics chip): still 0.46 %, blinks 0.85 %, pose changes 0.93 %, stepped dots about 1 %, the stepped mouth 2.2 %, but anything smooth without end 5–9 % (breathing 5.2–6.4 %, a smooth mouth 8.75 %). The page can't tell whether a chip draws it, and the kiosk forces reduced motion without one, so the desk passes `calm` itself. | One-off moves are cheap on the box; loops are the whole cost. |
| FM-08 | **One set of rules, two engines.** `faceMotion.ts` holds the states, sizes, timings, easings and moves for every engine. The web and the desk screen use `puppet.ts`: it writes each part's `transform` attribute from animation frames, round the part's pivot in the drawing's units, and is loaded on its own after the still file shows. The phone parses each pose's drawing once with react-native-svg and moves its parts with Reanimated on the display thread from frames worked out ahead, so a move costs no React render. Held moves (a glance) stay under later ones (a blink). | The same face behaves the same everywhere; no CSS `transform-origin`, which browsers and SVG renderers treat differently. |
| FM-09 | **Each face has its own gesture**, played when a run someone waited on is done, when poked and when picked: Dusty pushes his glasses up with a brow raised, Banjo looks across and nudges his glasses, Neo lowers his sunglasses, Skipper's tuft springs as he cocks his head, Hazel draws a tick on her clipboard and nods twice. | Character, in about a second, once. |
| FM-10 | **Where the live face shows.** Web: a run's header (thinking before it starts, working, needs you, done, sorry), a new chat (pokeable), an approval (needs you while it waits), onboarding's Meet your agent (greets, pokeable, a picked face plays its gesture), the agent's profile (pokeable, a picked face gestures), and the thinking line while the agent works on a reply (FM-15). Phone: the run screen, a new chat (pokeable), the Name and face part, and the thinking row (FM-15); the newest reply's face is 24 dp, so it stays still. The desk: Talk's newest face (listening while the mic records, thinking, working, speaking, sorry), the glance dozing while nobody is there and in quiet hours and saying hello when someone is back, a look's face (thinking, working, done). Lists, search, council faces, canvases and the other small faces stay still. | Motion only where the agent is present and acting. |
| FM-11 | **The desk's speaking mouth follows a talking rhythm.** The answer plays through an audio element without a loudness reading, so the mouth steps with a rhythm of its own while the answer is said; while listening the face gets the mic's loudness. | A frozen open mouth looked like shouting. |
| FM-12 | **Screenshots never catch a move.** A browser driven by tests (`navigator.webdriver`) gets `reduced` faces, and the visual harness waits until every live face has swapped its still file for its drawing. | Deterministic baselines. |
| FM-13 | **Three art fixes, in both copies** (`packages/avatars/svg/` and the handoff's `svg/`, with their SHA-256 pins): Hazel's pencil sits behind her ear, Neo's suit has a light edge that keeps him apart from a dark page, and the small heads (24 px and under) are framed closer so the eyes and nose read. | Found while the faces were looked at closely for the research; small and safe. |
| FM-14 | **Releases** (2026-11-27): app 3.175.0 (web, Android, iPhone), the runtime's 0.316.0 with the desk screen; the thinking line's face (FM-15) in app 3.176.0. | |
| FM-15 | **The thinking line starts with the agent's face.** John, 2026-11-27, after seeing a chat without any motion: “show Dusty when it's responding (so show the avatar and a typing indicator) … so we can actually see its expressions”. While an agent works on a reply, its thinking line (the dots, what it does, Stop) starts where its next message will: its live face in the avatars' column, 40 px on the web and 40 dp on the phone, the smallest size that blinks and eases between looks, though a room's other faces are 40 px, a task chat's 34 and a thread's 32. The words follow 14 px after it in a room, 12 px in a task chat and a thread, as their messages; a task chat draws it as its messages do, the head on its face's tint. It thinks until the turn reports an action, then works, and looks at the person while an action waits on them (an approval); it doesn't switch back with each tool call's end, so quick reads don't flicker. The face is decorative: the words say who and what. A reply's own face above doesn't think as well. This replaces the mock's faceless thinking line (three dots and the words 54 px in). | The face beside an older reply was out of sight or too small to move; the thinking line is where the person looks while they wait. |
